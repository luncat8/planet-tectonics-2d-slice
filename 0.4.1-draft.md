## What the request means, restated as constraints

1. **The cut is drawn on the globe's map, not typed.** A viewer drags a line across the top view
   of a running world (or of an Earth pack) and that line is the section.
2. **The transfer is a copy or a file, not a network.** "copy or save … then paste or load":
   clipboard text and a saved file are the transports, because both projects are opened as files
   and neither may depend on a server. A live link is a later convenience, not the contract.
3. **The cross-section is reconstructed here**, in the 2D slice: the section's own columns,
   stacks, surface and renderer, seeded from what the cut carries.
4. **0.9 is the integrated globe**: the top-view simulation and one cross-section, side by side,
   in sync. This version is the one that makes that possible, not the one that ships it.

## Constraints carried in from the repository

- the standing rules apply: file:// friendly, classic script tags, no build, no modules, guarded
  `module.exports`, single-tab indentation, preallocated typed arrays, no allocation in the frame
  loop, no internet links;
- one owner per concept (`0.4.0-sync-plan.md` §1.1): the globe owns the map, the grid, the plates
  and the draw; the section owns the vertical dimension, the section view and the seeding. A
  shared file is shared-identical or it is not shared;
- every number in the plan comes from a script in `experiments/`;
- the plan is written so a fork or a reimplementation is possible from the document alone, with
  no reference to a chat.
