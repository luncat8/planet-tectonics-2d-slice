# 0.1.6 — Plan: plate forces, and the collision that has to absorb them

0.1.5 made contact *quiet*: topology events stopped stepping the surface, a collision built
a belt, and the audit became a gate. What it left was a planet that barely moved. Measured
over seeds 1–5 at 50 kyr/frame, the plates averaged **3.10 mm/yr** with a fastest plate at
15.5 mm/yr, against a mantle flow of 14.03 mm/yr — the plate averaging was throwing away
78% of the drive, and the boundary forces that were supposed to steer plates were being
divided by the wrong width. This series fixes the drive, re-derives the collision that has
to absorb it, and closes the mass ledger.

Status: the force and ledger work is **done and gated**. The collision work is **measured
and open** — §5 carries the fixture that decided it and the fix it points to.

## 1. The contract

R1–R6 are carried from 0.1.5 unchanged (`0.1.5-plan.md §1`), and `contact-audit.js
--strict` still gates them. This series adds three:

- **R7 The drive survives plate averaging.** A plate is driven by the *mean* of the flow
  under it, so a mantle mode shorter than a plate cancels in that mean and the plate
  crawls however fast the flow is. The modes are `n_k = round(k·wrap / (2π·Lm))` for
  k = 2..5 (`0.1.0-design.md §4.1`); the contract is that `Lm` is set so the plate-mean
  keeps at least 30% of the surface flow. *Measured: 41% at `Lm = 4000 km`
  (modes 3, 5, 6, 8), against 22% at `Lm = 2000 km` (modes 6, 10, 13, 16).*
  Gate: `m2-check` L76.

- **R8 The topology resolves one contact per frame.** The separation floor is applied to
  *positions*, once per frame. A pair that closes further than one floor gap in a frame
  is inside the floor every frame, so the consuming edge fires every frame and the trench
  runs at the frame rate instead of at the convergence rate — the failure `0.1.5-plan.md`
  §2 M1 was written to prevent, arriving from the other side. The condition is

  ```
  max|edgeRelN| · dt  <  gFloor · w0
  ```

  At `gFloor = 0.05` (3.91 km) that is 78 mm/yr at 50 kyr/frame and only **19.5 mm/yr at
  200 kyr/frame** — slower than any plate on this planet. *Measured with the 0.1.6 drive:
  2291 deaths in 3000 audit frames at `gFloor 0.05`, against 65 at `gFloor 0.12`
  (`contact-audit.js 3000 1 50`).* `gFloor` is now **0.12** (9.4 km), which leaves 3.9×
  of headroom at 50 kyr/frame and 1.0× at 200 kyr/frame — the 200 kyr leg of `m2-check`'s
  700 Myr fixture is at the edge and is the reason R8 is a contract rather than a comment.

- **R9 A draining record is never a mass source and never a sink.** A sliver owns no
  crust, but it is not nothing: melt can be parked in its chamber, and retiring the
  record drops the slot. Everything a sliver still holds is booked at retirement.
  Gate: `m2-check`'s 700 Myr per-lithology ledger, `ledger-check`.

R4 ("convergence is absorbed") now has a fixture of its own — `experiments/r4-check.js`.
**It is not met.** §5 has the table and the fix.

## 2. Mechanisms

### M0 — The drive is a wavelength problem, not a magnitude problem

Raising `U0` alone is unphysical: it is also the fan-advection speed, so it moves magma
and heat as well as plates. The reason the plates were slow is that at `Lm = 2000 km` the
four modes (6, 10, 13, 16) are all shorter than a plate, and a plate's drive is the mean
of the flow under it — the mean of a cosine over more than a wavelength is small.
Retention by plate averaging, measured in `scratch/sweep.js`:

| `Lm` (km) | modes | plate-mean / flow |
|---|---|---|
| 2000 | 6, 10, 13, 16 | 0.19 |
| 4000 | 3, 5, 6, 8 | 0.41 |
| 6000 | 2, 3, 4, 5 | 0.62 |

`Lm = 4000 km` with `U0 = 8e4` is the chosen point: it is the first scale whose modes are
longer than a plate, and it does not push `max|plU|` into the `vMax` clamp. `extRef`
follows, because the extension field is derived from the same flow (§M4).

### M1 — Boundary forces are line forces

A boundary force belongs to the *boundary*, not to a column. 0.1.5 wrote ridge push, slab
pull and collision resistance into `wB[i]`, a per-column velocity that `PLT.solve` then
averaged over the whole plate — so one boundary column's collision resistance was divided
by a plate 50 columns wide and braked nothing. `PLT.fP[p]` (m²/Myr) is now the plate's
boundary line force, summed once per plate:

```
slab pull     F = vSlab · slabPullK · s(age) · L_slab        onto the downgoing plate
              s(age) = clamp(colAge/70, 0, 1),  L_slab = SLAB.pullLen(x, dir)
collision     m = vColl · fb · (−relN)/vRef · L_belt          ±  onto the two plates
              fb = clamp(COL.beltFel / hFelLand0, 0, 2),  L_belt = COL.beltW
```

and `PLT.solve` adds `fP[p]·icD` to the plate's force sum before dividing by its width.
Two defects this exposed and fixed: the slab-pull sign (one unsigned `+=` pulled half the
trenches the wrong way) and `SLAB.pullLen`, added because `SLAB.findRibbon` *allocates* a
ribbon when nothing matches and so cannot be used for a read.

### M2 — One belt measurement

`COL.beltAt(st, n, i)` is the single place the model measures an orogen: the flanks are the
ground just outside the reach of the orogenic flow (`P.beltFeed = 2` columns each side, so
`i−3, i−4` and `j+3, j+4`), and the run is everything from the pair outward that stands
`beltRise` above them. It returns the run length and leaves `COL.beltW`, `COL.beltFel` and
`COL.flankH`. The plate brake (M1), the orogenic flow (`CRU.belt`) and the audit's R2 gate
all read this one measurement, so they cannot disagree about what a belt is.

A flank *inside* the flow's reach is ground the flow itself thickens: the belt then raises
the bar it is measured against and shuts its own flow off one column in. That was the state
of the code before this series, and it is why the belt stopped at two columns.

### M3 — Convergence is absorbed → **not achieved, see §5**

### M4 — Extension is the mantle's, not the plate's

`PLT.extension` is `d(uMantle)/dx`. The 0.1.5 form `d(uMantle − uPlate)/dx` reduces to
`−d(uPlate)/dx` inside a plate, which is *positive* at a convergent boundary — damage was
being made at trenches and not at rifts (measured at t = 5 Myr: interior 0.39, rift 0.14,
trench 0.75; `ext > 0` at 0 of 8 rift columns). With the sign fixed, `extRef` is
recalibrated to `5e-1`: equilibrium damage is `10·ext/extRef`, so at the old `1e-2` every
column sat at 1 and `splitScan` decided rifts on geometry alone (58 rifts in 50 Myr).
Reference: `archive/reference/geotectonics-0.1.5-final-design.md` line 236.

### M5 — The mass ledger closes

Two entries, both found by bisecting a 700 Myr residual down to a single frame:

1. `MAG.plumeColumn(st, x)` — a plume head arriving over a trench melted into the draining
   record's chamber. `MAG.arc` already skipped slivers; the plume path used the bare
   `MAG.nearest`. *Measured: two slivers retired in one frame at t = 112 Myr holding
   3.386e3 and 1.0157e4 m³ of mafic melt — exactly the −1.354e4 m³ residual.*
2. `COL.drain(st, c)` — whatever a sliver still holds when the trench retires it is booked
   to `ledCons` before the slot is dropped. This is the invariant, not a patch on one
   caller: after it, no code path can drop a slot with mass in it.

### M6 — Geometry that the faster plates exposed

Three topology rules, each one a configuration that only appears when a plate moves a
sizeable fraction of a column per frame:

1. **A crushed column is absorbed.** A column whose *both* gaps are at the floor has less
   territory than one floor, so no position of it clears both boundaries and its width
   collapses to zero. The boundary-age gate (`evAge`) cannot be allowed to hold the
   topology open that long, so a crushed column is consumed across whichever gap has a
   real column on the other side, on geometry alone (`COL.crush[]`). *Measured: without it
   the 200 kyr leg of `m2-check`'s 700 Myr fixture reaches `colW = 42 m` and then
   `z = Infinity` at t = 496.6 Myr.*
2. **A sliver pile-up drains from the left.** A trench eating several columns in a row
   leaves a marker per column, and the retirement rule (both gaps at the floor) waits on
   the rightmost one, whose far gap is still a full column wide. A sliver with another
   sliver on its right retires on its left gap alone.
3. **An orphaned sliver is retired.** A sliver whose trench gap has *opened* marks no
   trench; what is left is a zero-crust record inside its own plate, stealing width from
   the column beside it for as long as that plate lives. The floor cannot help — a plate
   is rigid, so both of them move together.

## 5. Open: the collision mechanism (request: "the current one looks not good")

`experiments/r4-check.js` is the fixture that decides it. Two continents, half the planet
each, both continental, driven together by a prescribed far-field force of 15 mm/yr per
plate (30 mm/yr of closing) added to `PLT.wB` — a force, not a velocity, because the
collision brake acts on exactly that term. 200 Myr at 50 kyr/frame, damage off (`kDam = 0`,
otherwise the prescribed continents split in under a Myr). Closing speed against belt
width, every 10 Myr:

| t (Myr) | 0 | 10 | 20 | 30 | 50 | 80 | 100 | 130 | 160 | 190 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| closing (mm/yr) | 3.6 | 8.2 | 17.5 | 20.5 | 23.3 | 20.7 | 22.0 | 27.0 | 31.7 | 32.3 |
| belt (km) | 156 | 1417 | 400 | 244 | 88 | 88 | 88 | 88 | 88 | 88 |
| root (km) | −2.7 | 2.8 | 2.3 | 1.8 | 1.5 | 1.2 | 1.0 | 0.7 | 0.6 | 0.6 |

**The belt never widens: it collapses to one pair by 50 Myr and stays there, and the
closing speed rises to 32.3 mm/yr — 108% of the drive.** The brake is not inert (the
negative control with `vColl = 0` ends at 37.7 mm/yr, so the brake buys 15%), but it
cannot grow, because it is proportional to a belt that never forms.

The cause is in the flow's gate, and it is specific to continent–continent collision:

```
excess = 0.5·(hTot[i] + hTot[j]) − flankH − beltYield
```

The flanks of a C–C contact are the *same continental crust* as the pair, so `excess`
stays at or below zero until the pair has been shortened by `beltYield` (3 km) on top of
two 35 km margins — and by then the pair is being drained by every other process in the
model. The orogenic flow only ever fires where a thick continent meets thin oceanic crust.
So the arrest that M3 was supposed to deliver is available at a margin and not at the
collision the contract is about.

Two changes follow, in this order:

1. **Gate the flow on shortening, not on the flank contrast.** The pair's excess over the
   *flanks* is the wrong measure at a C–C contact; the pair's excess over its own
   pre-collision thickness (or the root it has built, `peak − flank`, which is what the
   audit already measures) is the right one. This makes the belt form where the contract
   needs it, and the brake grows with it.
2. **Only then re-test the speed term.** `0.1.5-plan.md §5` rejected a convergence speed
   limit on `plU` for want of evidence. There is now evidence for reopening it — but only
   after (1), because with the belt unable to form, *any* geometric brake looks
   insufficient and the fixture cannot tell the two apart. Re-run `r4-check.js` after (1);
   if the closing speed still does not fall, add the speed term and re-derive `vSuture`.

Both are gated by `r4-check.js`, which prints this table and states the verdict.
