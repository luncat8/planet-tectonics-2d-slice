# 0.3.1 — Plan: remelting buried deposits

This plan treats the deferred G5 path from deposited material back to mobile melt. It does not
change P3/P4's frozen behavior by simply adding `PH.dep` to `melt.js::detach()`. The order of
work is measurement first, a source-aware routing rule second, and integrated acceptance last.

The motivating observation is specific: G4's phase law updates deposits as well as mantle, but
G5 segregation only admits mantle. Thus a deposit may carry an equilibrium melt fraction that
never enters the mobile-melt path. G6's cooling exchange applies only at `y <=
P.depositCoolDepth` (30 km); it is not a bound on what can happen to a deposit after burial.
P4.1 separately transfers qualifying cold deposits to the deep inventory and books that source
phase debit as `thr.deepDep`.

## 1. Scope and invariant

The feature, if accepted, is **buried-deposit remelting**, not general deposit routing:

- Keep newly landed and newly sedimented material in `PH.dep` while it is in the shallow
  cooling zone. Do not allow a hot surface deposit to skip cooling and immediately re-enter
  the chamber path.
- Let a previously cooled deposit contribute its equilibrium melt to the existing G5 mobile
  melt transit, chamber, vent, flight and landing path once it is buried and reaches
  `P.meltHold`.
- Keep mass, enthalpy, felsic and water composition conservative. The phase transition is an
  internal transfer; it is not a heat source, deposit sink or new melt law.
- Preserve the separate mantle-segregation and deposit-remelting throughput books. A deposit
  debit must not be mislabeled as mantle `thr.seg` or `thr.whole`.

The work is still deferred if the measurement does not justify shipping the new route. No value
in `params.js` is to be tuned from the routing-exclusion count.

## 2. M0 — focused measurement

`experiments/pt-buried-deposit-melt.js` is the report-only owner harness. Its canonical live leg
uses seed 1, 50 kyr/frame and 300 Myr with the shipped contact, slab, arc and erosion passes on.
The harness wraps the G5 detach call only to observe it: each sample is taken after G4's phase
and slab passes and immediately before `melt.detach()`.

At that boundary, the thermal candidate is exactly:

```
S.ph[p] === P.PH.dep
&& S.y[p] > P.depositCoolDepth
&& S.melt[p] >= P.meltHold
```

`S.melt` is the phase fraction G4 equilibrated from `H` and is the exact field the existing G5
detach kernel reads; the measurement does not invent a different, post-slab re-equilibration.
The strict depth comparison matches the cooling mask, which includes the 30 km boundary. It does
not infer exposure from whether `detach()` changed a phase.

The harness makes two readings, because a zero exposure count has two very different meanings and
only the second tells them apart:

- the **exposure** reading — the stock, count and mass-time of the deposits that already qualify;
- the **margin** reading — how close the buried deposit population comes when nothing qualifies:
  per depth band the sample with the highest equilibrium melt fraction, that parcel's own solidus,
  its specific enthalpy against the enthalpy the threshold would need
  (`Ts + meltHold * meltGap`) and the mass-time histogram of the melt fraction, whose top bin is
  exactly the threshold and therefore the same number as the exposure reading.

A zero with a large remaining enthalpy margin says the route is not merely unpicked-up but far from
engaging in this configuration; a zero one sample below the line says the opposite and is what a
`meltHold`/thermal-state discussion would be about. Neither is a bound by itself.

The harness runs one live leg, or a **cell matrix** of them: a cell is a seed with its own clock
(the slider's kyr/frame) and duration, so the seed axis, the clock axis and the long-lived leg are
the same run mode. The matrix repeats its first cell and requires the two legs to agree on the
complete-state fingerprint and on both mass-time readings — a cell must not depend on the cells
that ran before it.

Report, without editing the engine:

- instantaneous buried-deposit and thermally eligible host mass, parcel count and melt-equivalent
  mass (`m * xi`), including their peaks;
- sampled mass-time (`km²·Myr`) and parcel-time, with depth bands `30–250`, `250–600`,
  `600–2400` and `>2400 km`;
- the closest approach per depth band and the hottest buried deposit: melt fraction, depth,
  temperature, solidus, composition, enthalpy margin and the time of the sample, so the summary
  line can be read as a distance to the threshold and not only as a flag;
- **the local node field's own temperature at every one of those samples**. A parcel's
  equilibrium temperature can sit above the field it is embedded in: the marker-to-grid pair is
  the adjoint of the deposit (`grid.js` renormalizes by `mu_k / W_k`), which hands a node's
  discrete heat change to its markers by *node measure per arriving mass* rather than by an equal
  specific increment, so a light parcel in a heavy node receives a large specific increment and a
  heavy one in a light node a small one. The pairing is what makes the intake exact against the
  operator's own wall flux, and its distributional consequence is a separate, reportable fact:
  a sample whose temperature is far above its local field is reaching the threshold by that
  concentration, not because the mantle there is that warm. Both are candidates for M1, and the
  routing specification below treats them the same way, but the decision cannot be read without
  knowing which one was measured;
- the mass-time histogram of `xi` over the buried population, and how many sampled parcel IDs were
  ever above a zero melt fraction: whether the population is being warmed at all;
- the hottest non-deposit parcel below `yDeep` as the interior yardstick beside the deepest band:
  the thermal head a deposit would have to be carried into, and whether any deposit ever gets
  there;
- consecutive eligible sample duration by parcel ID. This is a frame-sampled residence
  statistic, not a material tracer: coalescence keeps one ID and erosion changes parcel
  grouping. Use the per-frame stock and mass-time as the robust burden measures;
- `thr.deepDep` separately, as actual deposit mass transferred to P4.1's deep records.

At 50 kyr/frame, the G5 observation interval is 0.05 Myr. A zero report means no exposure was
observed at that cadence in that run. It does not prove that other seeds, clock settings or
sub-frame transients have zero exposure, and it does not turn `thr.deepDep` into a remelt
measurement.

## 3. M1 — burial eligibility and G5 routing specification

A future runtime route must meet all four conditions at the G5 detach boundary:

1. the parcel is still `PH.dep`;
2. `y > P.depositCoolDepth` (outside the entire shallow cooling mask);
3. a persistent `depQuenched` latch is set;
4. its G4-updated `S.melt[p] >= P.meltHold`, the same phase field the current G5 kernel reads.

`depQuenched` is per-particle state, not a depth inference or a reuse of `age` (the crust law
resets `age` when material warms). It starts false on admission/landing and is set only after a
positive-time shallow cooling pass has left the deposit below the mobile threshold:

```
y <= P.depositCoolDepth && equilibrated melt < P.meltHold
```

The latch is checked/set even when `FLIGHT.cool()` has no enthalpy delta to apply, and it stays
set if the cooled material later warms at depth. A hot deposit that moves beyond 30 km before
this certification remains ineligible; this conservative case is preferable to bypassing the
surface quench. The cooling test must run before any later G5 remelt attempt can consume the
certificate. The G5 route still requires `y > 30 km`, so a certified parcel at the surface is
not mobile there.

Propagate the latch conservatively with the existing pool operations:

- New air-to-deposit landings and erosion from non-deposit rock start uncertified. A carried
  fragment of an already certified deposit may retain its certificate.
- `remove()`/compaction moves it with its parcel. A deposit merge is certified only if both
  inputs are certified. A `carry()` into a deposit is certified only if all transferred source
  material and any existing receiver are certified; material taken from mantle starts
  uncertified. A split into a non-deposit phase does not give the child a deposit certificate.
- A partial remelt leaves the residual deposit's certificate in place. The new `PH.melt` child
  has no deposit latch. A fully molten deposit becomes melt in place and leaves the deposit
  population.

At a qualifying partial melt, transfer the entire equilibrium melt fraction `m * xi` into a
new mobile-melt parcel, using the P3 equilibrium split: the deposit residue remains at its own
solidus, the child at its liquidus, and both retain the source composition. At `xi === 1`, change
the whole parcel to `PH.melt` in place, with bond state cleared; do not require a spare slot for
a whole-parcel phase change. Do not transfer the entire host parcel merely because it crosses
`meltHold`.

The child goes through the existing `PTMELT.transit()` in the same G5 pass. Do not add a second
rise law, bypass the chamber, change slab eligibility, or admit uncooled deposits to the melt
path. A partial split refused by a full pool is deferred and counted once; every source field,
including the certificate, remains unchanged.

## 4. M1 — books and state

Add a distinct gross source-phase debit `thr.depMelt` (and a transfer count `tx.depMelt`). Keep
`thr.seg` and `thr.whole` as mantle-source terms. The deposit identity becomes:

```
invM[dep] = thr.landCr + thr.eroCr - thr.eroDep - thr.deepDep - thr.depMelt
```

The mobile-melt book gains the matching source:

```
thr.seg + thr.whole + thr.depMelt - thr.freeze
    = thr.intrDr + mobile-melt parcel mass
```

Use one source-aware pool operation so an equilibrium split updates both books once. A
whole-parcel deposit change debits `thr.depMelt` without incrementing mantle `thr.whole`.
No split or whole-phase change changes `S.ledger` or `S.hLedger`; the existing P3.1 latent
identity must remain true. Include the latch in the pool field list, reset/initialization and
complete-state fingerprint, and include the new counters in reset and fingerprint. Re-run the
pool field-ownership check so compaction cannot strand the latch.

## 5. M2 — acceptance

The implementation is not accepted by a single live exposure count. It must pass all of these:

- Controlled shallow/deep fixtures: `y = 30 km` remains ineligible for G5, `y > 30 km` alone
  is insufficient without the latch, cooling sets the latch only after `xi < meltHold`, and a
  cooled deposit can later remelt at depth.
- A newly landed hot parcel cannot route during its first G5 opportunity, including a fixture
  that moves it past 30 km before G6 cooling. A deposit that is already cold remains harmless
  until it is both certified, buried and at/above the mobile threshold.
- Partial and whole transfers conserve mass, enthalpy, composition and melt; pool-full partial
  splits defer without state changes; the melt child follows the ordinary transit/intrusion
  path.
- Both deposit and melt source identities close, alongside the global mass, enthalpy and P3.1
  latent-heat identities. The replay fingerprint is deterministic and contains the latch and
  counters.
- The integrated P4 configuration stays within the existing frame gate, has no unbooked
  deposit debit or capacity refusal, and reports live remelt mass/time alongside `thr.deepDep`.
  Run more than one long-lived seed before generalizing a zero or non-zero result.

One M1-specific reading has to be kept apart from M0's raw one. M0 measures every buried deposit
at the threshold; M1's route admits only material the `depQuenched` certificate allows, and M0's
temperature-against-local-field column shows why that difference is not a formality: the samples
that reach the threshold carry an enthalpy excess over the node field they sit in, which is the
signature of hot material buried before the shallow quench rather than of sediment warmed by the
mantle it was carried into. The latch exists to exclude exactly that material, so the number M2
reports is the **certified** exposure — and a zero there keeps the branch deferred exactly as a
raw zero would, while the latch itself still lands, because a route without it would send
un-quenched ejecta back into the chambers.

The decision is read from both M0 readings together. Eligible exposure anywhere in the matrix is
the case M1 was written for. Zero exposure across the matrix, with the closest approach still far
from the threshold, is the case that closes the route as unreachable under the current thermal
state and constants — that is a finding about `meltHold`, the solidus curve and where deposits are
carried, not a queue item, and it belongs in `roadmap.md` as a measured deferral. Zero exposure
with a population sitting at the line is the case that needs a thermal-state discussion before
any routing code is written.

If the canonical and seed-matrix runs contain no eligible exposure, keep the runtime feature
deferred rather than shipping an unexercised routing branch. The measurement harness and this
routing specification remain useful without enabling the physics.
