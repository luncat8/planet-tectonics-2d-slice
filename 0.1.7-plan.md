# 0.1.7 — Plan: crust flow units, and the ceiling that has to mean it

The column engine's two crustal flow laws — the orogenic belt flow (`CRU.belt`) and
delamination (`CRU.delaminate`) — are linear relaxations: a column sheds a fraction of
its excess per Myr. Both were implemented with an extra `× 1000` on an excess already in
metres while `js/params.js` documented the rates as "per km of excess" — the two
readings agree only if the flow output is also km, so the effective rates were 10^6 too
high. Measured consequence: a 5 406 m belt excess moved 13.0 km of felsic crust in one
frame at 10 kyr/frame where the documented law gives 6.5 m; a column past the crust
ceiling lost its whole stack in one frame (the "never more than half of it" cap was
itself written as `over * 500`). Every 0.1.x number that quotes a plateau, a ceiling or
a belt width was measured with the bug in place (`roadmap.md §3`, `0.1.6-plan.md §5`).
This release settles the units, gives both laws a form that is stable at every slider
leg, attaches the receiver-side cap the ceiling needs, and re-measures the numbers the
bug had been quoting.

## 0. Conventions

Unchanged from `0.1.6-plan.md §0`: metres and Myr, `w0 = 78.2 km`, `wrap = 40 030 km`,
512 columns at reset, kernels `K0..K9`, no allocation in a kernel,
`contact-audit.js --strict` is the gate. Every number below comes from a script in
`experiments/`.

**The unit rule, stated where the constants live**: a crustal rate constant is `1/Myr`
and multiplies a metre-valued excess to give metres. "per km of excess" is not a unit
in this codebase — it is what made a 10^6 error look legal. A law that wants km
converts once, in the law, and the constant's comment says so.

## 1. The two laws

Both are the same shape: a backward-Euler linear shed, `take = over · k·dt / (1 + k·dt)`.
Exactly linear in the excess at small `k·dt`; bounded by the excess at any `dt`, never
undershooting the ceiling (no oscillation at 100–200 kyr frames); the half-life is a
physical time, not a frame count.

`CRU.delaminate` (the ceiling on one column's crust): above `P.crustMax` the column
peels its deepest beds, mafic first, booked to `ledCons` — a recorded sink, not a
clamp. `P.kDelam` is now **45/1/Myr** (`τ = 22 kyr`), i.e. the ceiling is reached
within a frame or two at every slider leg — the property the old comment always claimed
and the old code only "achieved" by emptying whole columns.

`CRU.belt` (the orogenic flow): a collide pair stands above the ground `P.beltFeed`
columns beyond it by more than the `P.beltYield` root, and the excess relaxes sideways
at `P.kBelt` (**0.12/Myr**, the documented value — units only; see §3 for why this
release does not re-tune it) scaled by the closing rate,
`sp = 0.5 + |edgeRelN|/vRef`. Each receiver is clamped to its headroom: the flow never
fills a column past `P.crustMax` (the unfixed line `0.1.6-plan.md §4` named), and a
receiver at the ceiling only silences its own side of the wedge. The belt shed is
explicit-times-stiffness in the same BE form so a future stiff re-calibration (a known
option, §3) cannot oscillate.

`CRU.k5` runs the peel **last** — after collapse, belt and the growth laws — so K5 is
the only pass that can end a frame over the ceiling, and it ends it there. This makes
the ceiling an end-of-frame fact: `contact-audit` R3 measures `crustMax + influx·dt`
(see §2) instead of racing the refills that followed the peel.

## 2. The ceiling is an equilibrium, and the gate now says so

A rate sink under active squeeze settles above its threshold by the one-frame influx:
`maxH ≤ P.crustMax + influx·dt`, with `influx = 35 km/Myr` the measured worst squeeze
into a compressing pair (r4 and the audit legs, all four combinations of
seed {1, 5} × {50, 100} kyr/f). The audit's R3 gate carries that formula and prints
the allowance beside the measurement; a fixed 1% band failed at 100 kyr/f because the
influx term scales with the frame, not with the crust.

## 3. The collision contract, re-measured (R4 and R2) — open, with the sweep that says why

`experiments/r4-check.js` on the fixed laws: the belt now **builds** (root 19.6–29.7 km
where the bug left 3.1 km; early-quarter width 251–441 km), the closing speed dips to
24.6 mm/yr near 80 Myr — and then the belt decays to the bare pair (88 km) and the
closing returns to drive (146% of its early mean). `0.1.6-plan.md §5`'s "the belt never
widens" was therefore *not* only the units: with honest rates the belt forms, and then
the contact consumes it. The brake is proportional to the instantaneous belt width
(`PLT.basal`, 0.1.6 M1–M2), so as the belt decays the brake decays — positive feedback,
and no rate fixes it by itself:

- peak-gated excess (`max(hTot[i],hTot[j])` over the flanks instead of the pair mean):
  identical on the symmetric fixture — rejected by measurement, not by taste.
- `vColl ×8` (1.6e6): the brake *works* early — 5.8 mm/yr closing against a 30 mm/yr
  drive, the fixture's own gain goes 15% → 58% — and still relaxes as the belt decays.
- `kBelt` sweep {0.12, 1, 2, 4, 12, 40}/Myr, audit seed 1 leg: the R2 width gate peaks
  at 89% at `kBelt = 1` (τ ≈ the reference convergence rate `vRef/w0`), the needle
  ratio falls monotonically 5.06× → 2.97× and never crosses its 1.5× gate, and event
  memory starts failing by 12/Myr. A stiff belt trades the ceiling's neighbour
  problems, not its own.

What the data says the missing mechanism is: an orogen memory — a brake that scales
with what the collision *has built* (plateau volume over `hFelLand0`, or a per-edge
decaying belt width) rather than what the belt scan measures this frame. Both need new
persistent state, which crosses `js/checkpoint.js`'s schema and the 20-import identity
of `port/slice-format.js` — a model design decision for the next engine plan, not a
constant inside this one. `0.1.6-plan.md §5` steps 1–2 and R4 stay open; this release
hands them the measurements above.

## 5. Findings

- A rate law's comment is a test you can write: the hand-computed frame (6.5 m vs
  13.0 km) is now two gates in `m2-check`, one per law. A qualitative suite — relaxes,
  conserves, caps — cannot see a 10^6.
- A gate must measure its mechanism's promise. The floor is a cross-plate bound and a
  draining record's territory is soft by design; a gate that measured all adjacent gaps
  was passing only while a bug made every overage column empty in one frame.
- A "ceiling" implemented as a rate law is an equilibrium (`crustMax + influx·dt`), and
  saying so in the gate is honest; making the peel the last pass of K5 is what lets the
  gate say it about *end-of-frame* values instead of mid-frame ones.
