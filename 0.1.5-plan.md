# 0.1.5 — Plan: contact mechanics (smooth, convergent-limited plate interaction)

Implements the contact rework that 0.1.0 left open. Physics source: `0.1.0-design.md`
(§4.2 kinematics, §4.3 crust cycle, §4.4 column update) and the vendored
`archive/reference/geotectonics-0.1.5-final-design.md` (§4.3 overlaps, §6.3 forces).
The finished 0.1.0 plan is archived in `archive/0.1.0-plan.md`; its remaining features
(toy eruptives, deposits, save/load) are re-planned in `0.2.0-plan.md` and land after
this one, because they hang vents, deposits and extrusions on the crust geometry that
this plan makes well-behaved.

Self-contained: a fork can execute this plan with the design document and this file.
Runtime rules and workflow are `AGENTS.md`: classic `<script>` tags, no build, tabs,
no allocation in the frame loop, `module.exports` guarded for node, worklog and
reports in `archive/`, checks under `experiments/`.

## 0. Conventions

- One unit system: m, Myr, m/Myr, 1/Myr (design §9). The only yr quantities are the two
  sliders and they are converted in `sim.js`.
- Every kernel is `(state, dtGeo, t, Tm)`, headless, allocation-free and a no-op at
  `dtGeo = 0`. Entities move only through mark + gather; a kernel never reads a value
  its own pass has written.
- **Layers are thicknesses, and thickness is volume / the column's own width.** Every
  flux between two columns is computed in volume and divided by the receiver's width
  (`COL.collapseMove` is the reference implementation). K4 is the one exception and it
  says so: K4 turns every layer into a volume on entry (`layTh *= oldW`, `colW = 1`)
  and back into a thickness on exit, so a birth or a consumption inside it never has to
  know a width.
- All new state is fixed-capacity typed arrays allocated in `state.js`; all tunables go
  to `params.js` with the units in the comment.
- The measurement loop for this plan is `node experiments/contact-audit.js [frames]
  [seed] [kyrPerFrame] --strict`, which gates §1 and exits 1 on a violation. Every
  number in §1 is a `params.js` constant, so the gate, the plan and the tuning cannot
  quote different values.
- A phase is done when its gate script prints ALL PASS and the numbers are recorded in
  `archive/0.1.5-worklog.md`.

## 1. The contract

The section is the product; a plate boundary that changes the picture faster than the
geology can is a defect, not a feature. 0.1.5 makes six promises, each measured on a
2048-point raster of the drawn surface at every frame:

- **R1 No event is louder than the background.** On the frame a record is born or
  drained, the drawn surface at that site may move no more than the rest of that same
  frame multiplied by `P.evDzK` (1.25). The pairing is per frame, not per site class:
  a quiet frame's worst move is a diagnostic, an event frame's is a gate. Baseline:
  events moved the surface 4657 m in one frame while quiet frames moved at most 276 m
  (16.9x), and 91 of 93 topology events repeated at a site that had already flipped.
- **R2 Contacts build a belt, not a needle.** A convergent boundary thickens a run of
  at least `P.beltCols` (4) of the 6 columns around the pair, and the peak of the belt
  is not more than `P.beltPeak` (1.5) times the thickness of its flanks. Baseline: the
  collision at t = 4.5 Myr put 11.8 km of crust on one column in a single frame and
  diluted its neighbour by 9.3 km in the same frame.
- **R3 The crust has a ceiling.** `hTot` stays under `P.crustMax` (80 km) everywhere, and
  the excess leaves through a recorded sink (`CRU.delaminate` peels the deepest beds
  and books them in `ledCons`). No clamp, no mass-deleting deletion. Baseline: 161 km,
  and the 500 Myr regression run reported 80 km only because erosion was running.
- **R4 Convergence is absorbed.** A mature continental contact comes to rest: the belt
  holds a root of `P.beltYield` (3 km) before it flows sideways, the flow is
  proportional to the excess, and a C–C contact slower than `P.vSuture` (3 mm/yr) for
  `P.evAge` (2 Myr) sutures its two plates, after which the pair is one plate and
  cannot close any faster. Orogeny is asymptotic instead of unbounded.
- **R5 Stacks stay ordered and never run out of beds.** Beds are inserted at their
  stratigraphic rank (`P.LITH_RANK`, sill below mafic below felsic below volcanic below
  sediment), so no kernel can invert a stack by accident, and a full stack consolidates
  instead of growing without bound: 0 inverted column-frames and 0 column-frames at
  `P.layerCap`. Baseline: 135 883 inverted column-frames and 66 923 at `layerCap`.
- **R6 Everything is still exact.** Per-lithology mass closes to 1e-9 over a run with
  every kernel enabled, `dtGeo = 0` is a no-op, and the same seed and step are bitwise
  deterministic. Every transfer is either a move *inside* the crust (no ledger entry,
  because the mass is already in `now`) or an explicit `ledProd` / `ledCons` /
  `ledMixIn` / `ledMixOut` entry. A kernel that drops or invents volume is a defect
  even when the picture looks right: see the negative-bed rule in §2.

Two more properties are reported rather than gated, because a threshold on them would
be a threshold on taste:

- **The event rate** (`P.evRate`, 2 per 1000 frames is the 0.1.0 baseline). What is
  gated is `P.evGap` (40 frames, 2 Myr at 50 kyr/frame): no site may change its
  topology twice inside the event memory. A boundary that re-opens after 13–20 Myr is
  a Wilson cycle, and a rate cap would buy quiet by forbidding geology.
- **The worst quiet-frame move**, with its `dh` and `dw` at the same place. It is the
  yardstick R1 is measured against, and the place a new artefact shows up first.

## 2. Mechanisms

Five kernels and two rules. The first rule is what makes the rest work:

> **A record may only appear or disappear when the territory it owns is worth nothing
> to the picture** — a birth happens in a gap that has been opening for millions of
> years, a death happens where the record has already shrunk to a sliver. Nothing else
> may change the number of columns.

The second is the ledger's:

> **A capacity limits what a basin *takes*, never more.** A clamp that hides a pile-up
> is a defect; so is a "capacity" that takes more than arrived and leaves a negative
> thickness behind. Every limiter in the model — `P.sedCap`, `P.chamberCap`,
> `P.layerCap`, `P.crustMax` — bounds an arrival, never manufactures a departure.

### M0 — The audit as a gate

`experiments/contact-audit.js` measures, and `--strict` turns §1 into checked gates:
the R1 pair, the R2 belt width and peak, the R3 ceiling, the `P.evGap` repeat count,
stack inversions, at-cap column-frames, the share of the crust held by draining records
(`P.sliverMax`, 2%), and buffer finiteness. The audit matches records across frames by
position *and* width, treats a record that turned draining as matched to the record it
replaced, and reports `unmatched` and `rematched` so a quiet frame's worst `dh` can be
read as a real move rather than a re-partition.

### M1 — Quiet topology events

**(a) Consumption converts, it does not delete.** When a column is consumed at a
subduction margin, its stack goes into the ribbon as before, then the *record* is left
in place: empty (`colNL = 0`), `colGhost = 1`, on the overriding plate, its territory
kept. Deleting it re-partitioned a full column of territory between the two neighbours
in one frame, and since thickness is volume / width that is a 20–35% loss of crust — a
multi-kilometre pop for both of them. The sliver holds its slot until the trench has
closed both its gaps to the floor, and it is *drawn* as the line its two real
neighbours already make across it (`SURF.morph`), so neither the consumption frame nor
the sliver's life moves the section. A sliver that has just been drained is exempt for
`P.tauSliver` (4 Myr): it was standing on its own ground, a trench several km below
that line, and snapping it to the line is a 5 km step in one frame.

- The consumed crust is still ledgered (into the ribbon, or `ledCons` when there is no
  ribbon yet). A sliver is never a mass source.
- The margin keeps half of every sediment bed the loser carried, as the accretionary
  prism, booked through the same volume path as everything else.
- `P.wMin = P.gFloor * P.w0` is the retirement rule: a sliver whose two gaps are both
  on the floor is holding `2 * wMin` of territory, and handing that back moves each
  neighbour by a few per cent of its own width.

**(b) A floor on contact gaps, carried by the plate.** After K3 transport, a pair of
records of *different plates* may not be closer than `P.gFloor * w0` (0.05, ~3.9 km).
The correction is a *plate* correction: each plate is displaced rigidly by the largest
overlap it is holding, half of it each way, momentum-neutral. Nudging the two records
apart in place instead takes the difference out of their outer neighbours' gaps every
frame until the outermost column of the chain has no width, and no width means no
thickness. A plate with a contact on both sides is pushed both ways, so the passes
repeat until no pair is inside the floor, bounded by `P.floorPass` (16). A ring of
contacts can over-constrain a rigid correction — a plate held between two equal
overlaps has nowhere to move — so the floor is a bound, not an equality:
`P.floorTol` (1% of `gFloor`) is the residual the check allows, and it is 25x smaller
than the residual a fixed three passes leaves (measured on seed 5 at 500 Myr: 3 passes
7.5% inside the floor, 8 passes 0.8%, 16 passes 0.05%).

**(c) Events need a sustained state and a real speed.** `COL.intents` keys off the
classified edge, which the hysteresis deliberately holds at speeds far under `epsHi`; a
boundary that hovers at 0.1 mm/yr therefore spawns and kills a record every few frames.
Both topology intents are gated on the classifier's own entry terms: `|edgeRelN| >
P.epsHi` and `edgeAge > P.evAge`. A boundary that never exceeds the entry threshold is
a neutral contact and has no business changing the topology. A consuming pair must also
be *at the floor* — that is the state a subduction is in — while a C–C edge consumes
nothing at all, because there is nowhere for the crust to go: the pair squeezes against
the floor instead, which is mass-exact shortening, and sutures as plates.

### M2 — Collision builds a belt

**(a) No consumption at a continental contact.** C–C collision removes nothing: the
reference's "thinner felsic column disappears" is a 2D-grid artefact (a fixed cell has
to be emptied) and it is what forces the whole stack onto one record. Here the crust
has nowhere to subduct, so both records stay, squeeze, and suture as plates through
`COL.events`. `COL.consume` keeps the oceanic path only.

**(b) The belt flows sideways once it is a wedge.** `CRU.belt` runs after the column
update, for every C–C edge whose four neighbours are real records. The pair's mean
thickness minus the mean of the columns two to either side, less a root of
`P.beltYield` (3 km), is the excess: the crust stands at its critical taper before it
flows, and without the root the flow flattens the boundary until no belt is left. The
flux is `P.kBelt` (0.12 /Myr per km of excess) scaled by the convergence rate, capped
by the frame, and it is a *move inside the crust* through `COL.collapseMove`, so the
ledger has nothing to say about it. Half of each column's share goes to the column
beside the pair and half to the one beyond, so a belt widens instead of raising a wall
two columns wide.

**(c) Merge by lithology, not by stacking.** Every operation that puts rock into a
stack goes through one primitive, `COL.insertVol(st, c, lith, vol, age, flags)`:
the bed is placed above the first bed from the bottom whose `P.LITH_RANK` is above
`lith`'s, thickened into a neighbour of the same rock and flags rather than added, and
at `P.layerCap` the stack is consolidated first. `COL.consume`, `COL.collapseMove`,
`CRU.arcGrowth`, `CRU.lipGrowth`, `MAG.spill` and the K6 deposit all use it. The mass
is a volume in, a volume out; the order is a property of the operation, so no kernel
can invert a stack by accident, and a stack that will not consolidate books its
overflow as consumed rather than losing it.

**(d) Delamination caps the crust.** Above `P.crustMax` the column sheds its deepest
beds (mafic basement first) at `P.kDelam` (0.45 /Myr) of the excess, never more than
half of it in one frame, and the volume is booked in `ledCons`. That is the reference's
eclogite drip: a recorded sink, not a clamp, and the reason a belt stops instead of
growing forever.

### M3 — Convergence is absorbed

The feed rate must fall as the belt grows, otherwise the flow only decides how fast the
inevitable happens. Three mechanisms, in the order they act:

1. **The belt widens** (M2b), so the thickening is spread over `P.beltCols` columns
   instead of raising one. The peak over its flanks falls to ~1.3–1.44x.
2. **The excess is capped** (M2d), so a contact cannot hold more root than the ceiling
   allows; a pair at the ceiling stops thickening no matter how fast it converges.
3. **The plates suture** (R4): a C–C edge that has been slower than `P.vSuture`
   (3 mm/yr) for `P.evAge` (2 Myr) merges its two plates, after which the pair is one
   plate, the boundary is gone, and the single plate's speed is set by the mantle
   drive. A split corridor re-opens it, so a Wilson cycle is still possible — just not
   at the frame rate.

The arrest is therefore geometric rather than a speed limit on `plU`: a plate that has
sutured cannot close against itself. This is the honest reading of the design's "the
collision resistance is the normal-speed damper" in 1D, and it costs no extra term in
the plate solve.

### M4 — Subduction-side smoothing

The M4 paths feed a slab from a squeezed column, so the last frames before a
consumption are the ones that move the surface. With M1 in place:

- `SLAB.appendStack` anchors the ribbon at the *edge* (the midpoint of the consumed
  pair's gap), not at `colX[j] +/- 0.4 * w0`; the trench stops jittering by half a
  column.
- A consuming pair must be at the floor gap before the stack moves, so the crust that
  enters the ribbon is taken from a column that is already a sliver, and the surface
  above it has already sunk to the trench depth at the convergence rate.
- `SLAB.remove` and node truncation keep their explicit `ledCons` entries; the water
  ledger is unchanged.

### M5 — The surface kernel's own capacity

K6 gives a basin a capacity (`P.sedCap`, 4 km) and spills the excess out of it, because
a trench is the lowest ground on the planet and would otherwise collect every grain the
margin erodes for as long as the planet runs. The spill is bounded by the load that
arrived: `shed = min(hSed + th - P.sedCap, th)`. A margin whose prism already stands
over the capacity — subduction hands it half of every sediment bed it consumes and
nothing takes it back — would otherwise deposit a *negative* bed, which is a hole in
the stack that every later sum is short by and that is booked nowhere. It surfaces
hundreds of frames later, as a per-lithology ledger error of 1e-3 at the moment the
column is finally consumed and the hole is deleted.

## 5. Rejected alternatives (and why)

- **Keep the deletion, rate-limit the width.** A per-column width lag needs a third
  account for territory in transit and breaks the property that `SUM(thickness*width)`
  is exactly the integral of what is drawn (design §1.3). The sliver does the same job
  with real geology.
- **A hard thickness clamp.** Hiding the pile-up instead of draining it: the crust
  volume has to go somewhere and the ledger must say where.
- **Consuming slowly instead of at once.** Transferring a stack over N frames just
  spreads the same total dilution over N frames; the neighbours still end up thinner
  than they should be. Territory is the thing to move slowly.
- **A larger `rContact`.** Waiting longer before consuming means the columns are more
  squeezed when it happens, so the pop is *bigger*; the audit baseline shows the
  0.6 w0 trigger already diluting neighbours by 22–35%.
- **A convergence speed limit on `plU` (M3 as first written).** Displacing a plate to
  hold a contact adds a second actuator to the plate solve, and it fights the one it
  is supposed to represent: a sutured pair already cannot close against itself, and a
  speed clamp has no way to say *which* pair is arrested. The geometric route costs no
  term in the solve and is the same physics read in 1D.
- **Letting the consumed record's edge history walk to the new boundary.** The surviving
  boundary is classified from the geometry every frame by `PLT.classify`, so the
  hand-off is overwritten before anything reads it; the only way to observe it is a
  fixture that calls `COL.k4` directly, which is a check that cannot fail in the
  product. The boundary that takes a sliver's place starts fresh, and has to earn
  `P.evAge` again — which is what keeps a trench from running at the frame rate.
