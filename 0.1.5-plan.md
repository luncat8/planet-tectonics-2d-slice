# 0.1.5 — Plan: contact mechanics (smooth, convergent-limited plate interaction)

Implements the contact rework that 0.1.0 left open. Physics source: `0.1.0-design.md`
(§4.2 kinematics, §4.3 crust cycle, §4.4 column update) and the vendored
`archive/reference/geotectonics-0.1.5-final-design.md` (§4.3 overlaps, §6.3 forces).
The finished 0.1.0 plan is archived in `archive/0.1.0-plan.md`; its remaining features
(toy eruptives, deposits, save/load) are re-planned in `0.2.0-plan.md` and land after
this one, because they hang vents, deposits and extrusions on the crust geometry that
this plan makes well-behaved.

Self-contained: a fork can execute this plan with the design document and this file.
Runtime rules and workflow are `AGENTS.md`: classic `<script>` tags, no build, tabs,
no allocation in the frame loop, `module.exports` guarded for node, worklog and
reports in `archive/`, checks under `experiments/`.

## 0. Conventions

- One unit system: m, Myr, m/Myr, 1/Myr (design §9). The only yr quantities are the two
  sliders and they are converted in `sim.js`.
- Every kernel is `(state, dtGeo, t, Tm)`, headless, allocation-free and a no-op at
  `dtGeo = 0`. Entities move only through mark + gather; a kernel never reads a value
  its own pass has written.
- All new state is fixed-capacity typed arrays allocated in `state.js`; all tunables go
  to `params.js` with the units in the comment.
- The measurement loop for this plan is `node experiments/contact-audit.js`
  (`experiments/logs/0.1.0-M4-contact-audit.txt` is the starting point). Everything this
  plan changes is judged on that output plus the existing harnesses.
- A phase is done when its gate script prints ALL PASS and the numbers are recorded in
  `archive/0.1.5-worklog.md`.

## 1. The contract

The section is the product; a plate boundary that changes the picture faster than the
geology can is a defect, not a feature. 0.1.5 makes five promises, each measurable on
the 50 kyr/frame default that the page loads with:

- **R1 No event is louder than the background.** On the frame a column is born or
  consumed, the drawn surface may move no more than on a frame where nothing happens.
  Baseline: events move the surface 4657 m in one frame, quiet frames at most 276 m
  (16.9x), and 91 of 93 topology events repeat at a site that had already flipped.
- **R2 Contacts build a belt, not a needle.** A convergent boundary thickens a run of
  at least 4 columns; the peak of the belt is not more than ~1.5x the thickness of its
  flanks. Baseline: the collision at t = 4.5 Myr puts 11.8 km of crust on one column in
  a single frame and dilutes its neighbour by 9.3 km in the same frame.
- **R3 The crust has a ceiling.** `hTot` stays under ~80 km everywhere and under
  `hDelam` in the mean; the excess is a recorded sink. Baseline: 161 km, and the
  500 Myr regression run reports 80 km only because erosion is running.
- **R4 Convergence is absorbed.** A mature continental contact comes to rest: the
  closing speed at a C–C edge decays as the belt thickens, so orogeny is asymptotic
  instead of unbounded.
- **R5 Stacks stay ordered.** Sediment, tephra and lava above felsic above mafic, and
  every layer operation preserves that order. Baseline: 135 883 column-frames with an
  inverted bed (mafic above felsic or above sediment) and 66 923 column-frames at
  `layerCap`, i.e. the bedding churns.
- **R6 Everything is still exact.** Per-lithology mass closes to 1e-9 over a run with
  every kernel enabled, `dtGeo = 0` is a no-op, and the same seed and step are bitwise
  deterministic. This part already holds (see `experiments/ledger-check.js`, M4.4) and
  must not regress: every new transfer is either a move inside the crust, or explicit
  production, consumption or a mix account.

## 2. Mechanisms

Five kernels and one rule. The rule is what makes the rest work:

> **A record may only appear or disappear when the territory it owns is worth nothing
> to the picture** — a birth happens in a gap that has been opening for millions of
> years, a death happens where the record has already shrunk to a sliver. Nothing else
> may change the number of columns.

### M0 — The audit as a gate (0.5 d)

`experiments/contact-audit.js` already reports the numbers; turn the ones that must
hold into a checked mode (`--strict`, exit code 1 on violation) and add it to the suite
list in the worklog and to the acceptance pass:

- worst single-frame surface move on a quiet frame (target: report, no gate)
- worst single-frame surface move on a birth/death frame <= quiet worst * 1.25
- max `hTot` <= `P.hDelam * 1.2`
- belt run around the thickest contact >= 4 columns
- zero repeated birth/death sites, <= 2 topology events per 1000 frames and per planet
- stack inversions 0, at-cap columns 0

Done: `contact-audit.js --strict` fails on the 0.1.0 baseline with exactly the five
violations above and passes after M1–M4.

### M1 — Quiet topology events (2 d)

Two independent changes, both in `js/columns.js` and `js/state.js`.

**(a) Consumption converts, it does not delete.** When a column is consumed at a
subduction margin, transfer its stack to the ribbon exactly as today, then clear the
record instead of deleting it: `hTot = 0` (the arc factory regrows it: `trenchDist`
already marks it, `CRU.arcGrowth` adds felsic to it, the half-sediment prism rule
covers the rest), keep its position and hand it to the overriding plate. The record is
now a trench/forearc sliver whose width shrinks as the convergence closes its two gaps.

- It dies only when `colW < P.wMin` (`0.01 * w0`): at that point the territory it leaves
  is under 1% of a column, so the neighbours' mass-preserving width change is under
  0.5% of their own width — the surface moves by metres, not kilometres.
- The margin keeps its own territory while the sliver lives, so nothing dilutes it.
- The consumed crust is still ledgered into the ribbon; the sliver is *not* a mass
  source.
- `colPlate[sliver] = plate of the overriding side`, so the boundary moves oceanward
  and the next event consumes the next oceanic column. The trench is a chain of
  shrinking slivers, which is what a trench is.

**(b) A floor on contact gaps.** After K3 transport, push a different-plate pair apart
when its gap drops below `P.gFloor * w0` (`0.05`): half the correction to each column,
momentum-neutral, then `S.widths()`. This is the geometric form of the reference's
collision resistance: the contact carries the stress instead of letting two records
interpenetrate and swap in the sort. It also guarantees a sliver dies at `P.wMin`
rather than crossing its neighbour.

**(c) Events need a sustained state and a real speed.** `COL.intents` keys off the
classified edge, which the hysteresis deliberately holds at speeds far under `epsHi`;
a boundary that hovers at 0.1 mm/yr therefore spawns and kills a column every few
frames. Gate both intents on the same terms the classifier uses to *enter* a state:
`|edgeRelN| > P.epsHi` and `edgeAge > P.evAge` (`2 Myr`). A boundary that never exceeds
the entry threshold is a neutral contact and has no business changing the topology.

Done: `contact-audit.js` reports zero repeated birth/death sites, <= 2 events per
1000 frames, and a birth/death frame whose surface move is within 1.25x of a quiet
frame. `m2-check.js` fixtures for sparse gaps, the seam and `colCap` births stay green.

### M2 — Collision builds a belt (3 d)

**(a) No consumption at a continental contact.** C–C collision removes nothing: the
reference's "thinner felsic column disappears" is a 2D-grid artefact (a fixed cell has
to be emptied) and it is what forces the whole stack onto one record. Here the crust
has nowhere to subduct, so both records stay, squeeze (their widths shrink and the K4
volume rescale thickens them, which is exactly mass-exact shortening), and suture as
plates through the existing `COL.events` path. `COL.consume` keeps the oceanic path
only; the C–C branch becomes the merge described in (c).

**(b) Crustal flow spreads the belt.** Generalise `CRU.collapse` into the orogenic
flow of design §4.4: the same mass-exact bed-level diffusion, with a
thickness-dependent mobility and a contact-localised boost.

```
hEx = max(hFel_i, hFel_j) - P.flowThr        (flowThr 28 km)
k   = P.kCollapse + P.kFlow * hEx / (P.hCollapse - P.flowThr)   (kFlow 1.2 /Myr)
k   = min(k, P.flowDtMax / dt)               (flowDtMax 0.08: explicit-diffusion cap)
belt: faces within P.beltCols (3) of a C-C edge use the boosted mobility,
      everywhere else the flow starts at hCollapse
face = k * dt * w0 * w0 * dh / gap           (the design's Laplacian, floored at faceGapMin)
```

Measured on the prototype: the peak falls from 161 km to 76 km and 53 columns sit
between 55 and 68 km, i.e. a belt instead of a needle, at unchanged frame cost.

**(c) Merge by lithology, not by stacking.** When a pair does merge (a squeezed
C–C pair whose thicknesses the flow has already equalised, or the oceanic path), the
survivor's stack must stay ordered. Add one primitive and use it everywhere:

```
COL.insertVol(c, lith, volume, age, flags)
  position = the first bed from the bottom whose density rank is above `lith`'s
             (maf 0 < fel 1 < sed/tephra/lava/sill 2)
  merge with an adjacent bed of the same lithology, else shift the tail and insert
```

`COL.consume`, `COL.collapseMove`, `CRU.addLayer` (arc, LIP, tephra), `MAG.spill` and
the new flow all go through it. The mass is a volume in, a volume out; the order is a
property of the operation, so no kernel can invert a stack by accident.

**(d) Delamination caps the crust.** Above `P.hDelam` (68 km) the column sheds its
deepest beds (mafic basement first, then lower felsic) to a new `ledDelam[]` account,
at `P.kDelam` (0.02) of the excess per frame. That is the reference's eclogite drip: a
recorded sink, not a clamp, and the reason a belt stops at ~70 km instead of growing
forever. `S.mass()` and the ledger check both include `ledDelam` (it is a `ledCons`
entry, kept separately only so the HUD can attribute it).

Done: a prescribed two-plate convergence for 200 Myr leaves a belt of >= 4 columns
above 55 km, peak <= 80 km, `ledDelam > 0`, per-lithology balance < 1e-9, and no
column above `P.hDelam` for more than 1 Myr. `m2-check.js`'s collision fixture is
rewritten to assert a belt (peak within 1.5x of the flanks) instead of "the winner
thickens".

### M3 — Convergence is absorbed (1.5 d)

The feed rate must fall as the belt grows, otherwise the flow only decides how fast the
inevitable happens. After K2's solve, for every C–C edge:

```
hBelt = mean hTot of the four columns around the edge
lock  = smoothstep(hBelt, P.hLock0 45 km, P.hLock1 62 km)
allow = P.vRef * (1 - (1 - P.vLock 0.12) * lock)      closing speed limit
if relN < -allow:  move both plates' plU by half the difference (momentum-neutral),
                   then recompute colU and ext
```

Two relaxation passes per frame, so a plate touching two contacts settles. The contact
therefore stiffens with the belt exactly as the reference's collision resistance does,
but as an exact limit on the relative speed instead of a force that has to win against
the mantle drive. It is also the honest reading of the design's "the collision
resistance is the normal-speed damper".

Done: a prescribed convergence fixture shows `|relN|` at a mature contact decaying
below `vSuture` (3 mm/yr) within 50 Myr while the belt keeps widening; the arrest does
not fire below `hLock0` (a young contact stays fast); plate speeds remain within
`vMax` and the plate solve's unit tests are unchanged.

### M4 — Subduction-side smoothing (1 d)

The M4 paths feed a slab from a squeezed column, so the last frames before consumption
are the ones that move the surface. With M1 in place:

- `SLAB.appendStack` anchors the ribbon at the *edge* position (the midpoint of the
  consumed pair's gap), not at `colX[j] +/- 0.4 * w0`; the trench stops jittering by
  half a column.
- A consuming pair must be at the floor gap (M1b) before the stack moves, so the crust
  that enters the ribbon is taken from a column that is already a sliver, and the
  surface above it has already sunk to the trench depth at the convergence rate.
- `SLAB.remove` and node truncation keep their explicit `ledCons` entries; the water
  ledger is unchanged.

Done: no frame in a 200 Myr run removes more than 25% of a column's crust, and the
world-sampled surface move during a subduction event is within the R1 budget.

   `section-check`, `m2-check`, `m3-check`, `ledger-check`, `raster-bench`, `smoke`.

## 5. Rejected alternatives (and why)

- **Keep the deletion, rate-limit the width.** A per-column width lag needs a third
  account for territory in transit and breaks the property that `SUM(thickness*width)`
  is exactly the integral of what is drawn (design §1.3). The sliver does the same job
  with real geology.
- **A hard thickness clamp.** Hiding the pile-up instead of draining it: the crust
  volume has to go somewhere and the ledger must say where.
- **Consuming slowly instead of at once.** Transferring a stack over N frames just
  spreads the same total dilution over N frames; the neighbours still end up thinner
  than they should be. Territory is the thing to move slowly.
- **A larger `rContact`.** Waiting longer before consuming means the columns are more
  squeezed when it happens, so the pop is *bigger*; the audit baseline shows the
  0.6 w0 trigger already diluting neighbours by 22–35%.
