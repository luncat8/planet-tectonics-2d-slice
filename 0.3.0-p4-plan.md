# 0.3.0-P4 — Plan: contact, recycling and surface material transport

This plan continues the particle engine of `0.3.0-plan.md` and `0.3.0-p3-plan.md` at the
point P3.5 closed: the conserved melt-to-plate path is accepted, so the mechanical and
surface questions that P3 deliberately left out can be asked with a closed ledger behind
them. P4 is the plan's §8 P4 milestone, split into the sequence its own gate implies:

| milestone | owns | fixture |
|---|---|---|
| P4.0 | inter-plate contact and underthrusting: a lid-scale contact kernel, the no-penetration response, and the two regimes (subduction / arrest) | `experiments/pt-p4-contact.js` |
| P4.1 | slab sinking and deep recycling: a detached slab sinks, and its transfer into a persistent deep inventory is booked, not sunk | `experiments/pt-p4-slab.js` |
| P4.2 | arc melt: water leaves the slab, depresses the wedge's solidus, and the P3 melt path erupts at the arc | `experiments/pt-p4-arc.js` |
| P4.3 | particle-based weathering, erosion and sedimentation, with the surface law chosen by a comparison harness first | `experiments/pt-p4-erosion.js`, `experiments/pt-p4-transport-sweep.js` |
| P4.4 | integrated acceptance and the tuning freeze | `experiments/pt-p4-check.js` |

P4 does not claim a collision solve, a global subduction census, a climate-driven erosion
model or a sedimentary basin simulator. It claims what its fixtures gate: plates that meet
do not pass through each other, a slab that sinks is accounted for, arc melt follows the
slab, and eroded material is transferred, never invented.

## 1. Contracts before equations

### 1.1 What contact is, and what it is not

P2.3's orogen term (`kOrogen`, `zOrogenMax`) is a kinematic uplift proxy: it reads two
surface columns whose plate velocities converge and adds relief. It is not a contact solve
and must not be cited as one. P4 adds the mechanical response it was standing in for, and
the proxy stays as the *relief* consequence of a convergence P4.0 has already resolved --
the uplift is not the evidence that contact happened.

The engine's material model has one uniform density, so there is no crustal buoyancy to
separate oceanic from continental crust. Temperature is the only density proxy the buoyancy
law itself uses (`RaK * dT/dx`), so P4 uses it the same way: the colder side of a contact is
the denser side. A contrast across the contact is therefore what decides between the two
regimes, not an absolute label the model does not carry.

### 1.2 Conservation

The contact kernel (P4.0) changes velocities and positions only. It never touches mass,
enthalpy, composition, melt fraction, phase or id, so the P3 ledgers close bitwise across
it by construction and a fixture asserts that rather than assuming it.

Momentum is not a conserved quantity in this engine and P4 does not make it one: the mantle
advection itself (grid.js `advect`) moves markers by an interpolated flow field. The
arrest regime's brake is a balanced impulse pair between the two clusters (their combined
momentum is unchanged, which the fixture measures); the subduction regime's brake holds the
overriding plate only, and the unbalanced impulse is reported as a named diagnostic, because
the reaction a real override carries is mantle traction the one-way Stokes coupling does not
model.

Deep recycling (P4.1) is the first transfer that leaves the plate population, and it is the
ledger's first new line since P3.0: a named deep inventory, mass and enthalpy booked into it
by a matched debit/credit pair, with throughput reported apart from stock exactly as P3.2
and P3.3 do. A slab that reaches the CMB is a transfer, not a sink.

### 1.3 Determinism and state

Everything the contact kernel writes per frame is either a cluster-level accumulator that
the frame recomputes anyway (`csVX`/`csVY`, the brake's correction) or workspace that the
frame rebuilds (the per-column contact table, the bending-band flags). The only new
persistent state is the cumulative diagnostic counters, and they are in the state
fingerprint (state.js `hash`) like every other counter, so a replayed run replays them.

The kernel no-ops at `dtGeo = 0` like every other geological kernel: a paused frame reports
the contact table, it does not brake, bend or push anything.

### 1.4 Scope: the lid

P4.0's contact pass reads the lid -- strong markers (cluster members) above `P.yCrust`,
which is the same band the crust relief terms already sample. Inter-cluster contact below
that band is the slab's business (P4.1) and is deliberately not resolved here; the plan's
gate is about plates, and a plate is a lid cluster. The pass is O(markers + columns) and
allocates nothing: the contact table is one preallocated `nx`-wide block of small arrays.

## 2. Kernel order

P3 put phase equilibrium ahead of the crust pass inside G4. P4 extends G4 with the contact
steps between the rigid fit and the projection, because the brake has to correct the
cluster's rigid velocity *before* the projection moves markers with it, and the bending band
has to move markers *instead of* the projection:

```
G4:  phase equilibrium (phase.js)
     strength, damage, anneal            (solid.js strength)
     clusters, bonds, failure            (solid.js clusters)
     rigid fit, slab pull, ridge push    (solid.js kinematics)
     contact: detect, brake, bend        (contact.js)
     rigid projection                    (solid.js project)
     contact: no-penetration guard       (contact.js)
```

`solid.js`'s `crust()` keeps its name and its meaning (the whole G4) and gains the two
contact calls; its `kinematics` is split into the fit and the projection so the brake can sit
between them. No other kernel changes order. P4.1 adds the slab pass after G4 (it needs the
contact result), P4.2 adds the water source inside G5, and P4.3 adds the surface transport
inside G7.

## 3. Milestones

### P4.0 — Contact and underthrusting

A contact is a pair of adjacent surface columns held by different clusters whose velocities
close on each other above `P.contactYield`. The pass finds them from the same per-column
shallowest-crust-marker table the relief terms use, and answers in one of two regimes:

- **subduction** -- the colder side is denser than the warmer side by at least
  `P.contactdT`. The colder plate is the subducting one: it keeps its velocity and its
  leading band bends down along `P.contactDip` beneath the override, whose approach is
  held. The band's velocity blends from the plate's rigid velocity at its inner edge to the
  slab velocity at the trench, so the bend is a rotation of the velocity, not a stretch, and
  the slab's speed is the plate's own.
- **arrest** -- no usable contrast. Both clusters take a mass-weighted share of the
  correction that cancels the closing (a balanced impulse pair), nothing bends, and the
  existing kinematic orogen term is what raises the belt.

A position-level guard is the last step: inter-cluster crust markers closer than
`P.contactHard` of a cell are pushed apart along their separation. It is the discrete
statement of the plan's gate -- colliding plates do not interpenetrate -- and it is counted,
so the report can say how often the velocity-level response left work for it. One sweep is
not always enough (separating p from q can push p into r), so it sweeps while it finds work,
bounded by `P.ctSweep`, and publishes what the last sweep left as a named residual in
`S.d.resid`: "zero hard overlaps" is measured against the pass's own table, inside the pass,
because a frame's later kernels move the parcels the table names. The push lands a pair on the
radius to within an ulp, so the test squares the radius with a rounding slop -- without it the
solver chases its own rounding forever.

Fixtures (`experiments/pt-p4-contact.js`):

- a controlled two-plate convergence leg, both regimes, driven through the real `crust()`
  entry: the closing rate at the contact falls to zero, the override is held, the colder
  plate's band ends up below the override's base and the override's markers stay above it,
  and the same leg with the kernel disabled interpenetrates (the defect, measured, so the
  fix is not asserted against nothing). The override's lid needs a root of its own: a rootless
  surface lid carries no slab pull, drifts away from the trench, and leaves the hold nothing to
  hold -- so the override deepens away from the boundary columns, with a gap at the columns
  either side of the contact, and both legs' temperatures are equilibrated into the grid before
  the first frame reads them;
- mass, enthalpy, composition and melt inventories bitwise unchanged across contact frames,
  and the arrest brake's impulse pair leaves the two clusters' combined momentum unchanged;
- the live shipped planet: contacts found, worst closing rate, absorbed shortening, bending
  markers and guard pushes reported, the hard-overlap count against a kernel-off control
  leg, the bending cap answered at every site it reaches, the residual published per frame and
  re-read at the end of the leg against a freshly built table, the domain still holding every
  parcel the guard pushed, the ledger still closing, and the frame cost.

Gate: colliding plates do not interpenetrate (zero hard overlaps on the live leg and on the
controlled legs), a subducting band's material ends up below the override's base while the
override's markers stay above it, and the P3 suite stays green with the kernel shipped on.

### P4.1 — Slab sinking and deep recycling

A slab segment that has bent below `P.yCrust` is no longer held by the contact pass. It
sinks under its own negative buoyancy (the same temperature-is-density proxy, as a downward
drive rather than the horizontal slab pull of P2.2), and when it reaches the deep inventory
boundary it is transferred: mass, enthalpy, melt and composition debit the parcel and credit
a fixed-capacity deep record set, with matched counters, and the record set is state (in the
fingerprint) and in the inventory (counted once). A full record set defers, never sinks.
This is the plan's §1.2 line: P4 books deep recycling as a transfer into a persistent deep
inventory.

The population the pass drives is the decision that matters, and it is measured, not assumed:
solid parcels below `P.yCrust` that are colder than their row mean by `P.slabdT` and colder
than `P.slabT` in absolute terms, **excluding** parcels still attached to a cluster of plate
size (`P.plateMin`) -- attached lithosphere is the plates' business, not a slab's, and counting
it against the row mean drains the deep mantle, which is cold relative to its own row and is
most of the world's mass. The contrast alone is not a ceiling; the absolute one is what keeps
the pass from eating the planet. The transfer check sits inside that population, so only
material the pass would have driven is ever recycled. The pass moves parcels, never velocities:
a slab's descent has to survive the next frame's `gatherVel`.

### P4.2 — Arc melt from the slab

The slab carries water down (P4.1's transfer books what survives). In the `'cool'` initial
condition, the shallowest cold surface skin (rows up to `P.yWet`, where `T <= 0.22`) starts
with bound water `P.slabW` (0.005); with `meltWW 20` that depresses the solidus by 0.10, which
leaves a cold lid or a cold slab (`T <= P.slabT = 0.45`) well below its own solidus at every
depth (`Ts >= 0.68`), so a wet slab never melts on its own. Once a detached cold slab segment
sinks into the dehydration window (`P.yArcMin..P.yArcMax`, `250..600 km`), `arc.js` at the head
of G5 releases `min(P.kArc * dt, P.arcFrac)` of its water mass into the warm, unsegregated
mantle wedge above it (`P.yCham..P.yArcMin` within `P.arcSpan`, up to `P.arcTop` candidates per
column ordered from smallest solidus gap `Ts - T` so once the hottest parcel reaches
`P.meltHold` the next takes the rest). There the transferred water depresses the wedge's
solidus through the existing `meltWW` term of the P3.1 law -- no new melt law -- and re-reading
`(T, melt)` from `H` via `phase.js`'s `byH` books `m * dT` in `S.ledger` so the P3.1 latent
identity `hLedger - ledger = meltLat * (invLTot - invL0)` holds across the release. The P3.2
segregation, P3.3 conduit (opening as explosive once `chW / chM > wBlast`) and P3.4 eruption
path then run unchanged, so an arc is a *consequence* of the slab's geometry, not a new
eruption model, and whatever water remains in the slab below `P.yArcMax` rides it down to
`P.yDeep` for P4.1's deep records. The release rule (depth window, rate, fraction) is
calibrated against a fixture (`experiments/pt-p4-arc.js`) that moves a dipping slab under a
fixed wedge and measures where melt appears.

### P4.3 — Erosion and sedimentation

The surface law is chosen by comparison before anything lands: a runoff/stream-power
transport rule against a conservative hillslope (slope-dependent diffusion) rule, both as
particle transfers on the same controlled relief fixture
(`experiments/pt-p4-transport-sweep.js`), with the shipped one the measurement picks.
Whatever lands must move material downhill, deposit it, and close the global inventory:
`zh` stays a profile (P2.3's smoothing proxy, unchanged) and the sediment stock is parcels
and deposits that the P3 inventory already counts.

### P4.4 — Acceptance and the freeze

`experiments/pt-p4-check.js` runs the real pipeline over the P4 legs (contact, slab, arc,
erosion) at the demonstration clock and a range of slider settings, delegates the owner
harnesses as children with their counts quoted, and freezes the P4 constants the way P3.5
froze the reserve: each line above its own measured worst case, the frame budget at the
demonstration clock, and the whole P3 acceptance still passing with P4 shipped on.

## 4. Decisions the experiments must settle

- the contact yield, the subduction/arrest contrast, the slab dip, the bending band's reach
  and the guard's hard radius. P4.0 ships measured candidates; P4.4 freezes them. The guard's
  sweep budget is settled: the residue is 0 at `P.ctSweep` 3 and raising it only grew the push
  count, so the third sweep is margin and not a fix;
- whether the contact table should carry a plate-size threshold. It does not, by design, so
  1- and 2-marker clusters -- lid shrapnel from the shipped failure law, one of them at
  312 cm/yr -- generate sites and most of the guard's pushes, which makes "absorbed
  shortening" fragment-inflated on the live leg. P4.4 decides, next to `contactYield`;
- whether the braking should also correct a cluster's rotation (a plate pressed at one end
  still converges at the other). P4.0 corrects translation only and records the residual;
- the deep record capacity and the release depth window. The capacity is not binding (6 of
  `P.deepCap` 32 records on the live leg, 0 refusals, so the deferral path is only exercised by
  a fixture that fills the set on purpose); the release depth window (`P.yArcMin..P.yArcMax`,
  `250..600 km`, above which is the cold lid/forearc and below which the slab carries its
  remaining water to `P.yDeep`), rate (`P.kArc 0.25 /Myr`, `P.arcFrac 0.25`) and lateral span
  (`P.arcSpan 125 km`, `P.arcTop 4`) are calibrated by `pt-p4-arc.js` and frozen at P4.4;
- the surface law (P4.3), by the comparison harness, not by preference;
- whether a detached slab should weld back or keep sinking: settled -- attached lithosphere
  keeps welding and is excluded from the slab population, detached cold material sinks, and
  nothing in the pass can pull a parcel back out of a cluster it has left.

## 5. Interfaces this plan must keep

- **One global per new file** (`0.3.0-p3-plan.md` §6): `js/pt/contact.js` (`PTCONTACT`),
  `js/pt/slab.js` (`PTSLAB`) and `js/pt/arc.js` (`PTARC`) each export a single global on an
  IIFE.
- **`index.html` / `particles.html` stay byte-identical mirrors** and `experiments/pt-ui.js`
  keeps gating them: a P4 HUD line lands with the page, the mirror and the fixture in one
  change, reusing the existing ids.
- **The state stays capturable**: the P3 fingerprint covers phase, id, enthalpy,
  composition and melt, and P4's cumulative contact, slab and arc counters join it; a save
  records `partCap` and refuses a different one. P4.1's deep records are state the same way.
- **No new physics inside a frozen kernel**: the P3 melt, phase and flight kernels keep
  their meaning. P4.2 feeds the existing solidus depression; it does not add a second melt
  law, and P4.3's sediment is the P3 deposit phase, not a renderer-only buffer.
