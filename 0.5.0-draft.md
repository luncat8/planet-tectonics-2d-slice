# 0.5.0 — Draft: two FFT-free particle convection engines

Reference record of the request that opens this line of work, in the words it was asked in.
The plan itself is `0.5.0-plan.md`; this file is here so a later session can see what was
asked, the way `0.3.0-draft.md` and `0.3.2-draft.md` serve theirs.

## What was asked

Plan two more particle convection engines, without FFT:

1. magma liquid on a cell grid, crust is particles (with or without links between
   particles);
2. only particles: magma without links between particles, crust with links between
   particles for some strength, and emulate melt and cracks.

Important decisions:

- particles smooth or not;
- particle size:
  - same or different (by material or density);
  - constant or changed — an earlier experiment suggested hot melted particles may be
    smaller to flow better, because they get more room to move and other geometrical
    advantages, but this may be slower on GPU than same size. Think, or make it optional:
    size depends on heat (cold solid bigger);
  - note: we have a log vertical scale, so same size on canvas means an emulated bigger
    size in the deep. This may be good or not.
- these questions may be hard to solve by calculation and may just need options to choose
  and adjust in the GUI, and see how it behaves: convection, melting, eruption,
  unpressurize.
- do we need Box2D or not — what will be faster and good-looking. Another experiment
  showed convection works well in Box2D if circles have temperature and a lift force, but
  it is not settled whether that is OK, or whether it can be made faster on GPU and kept
  stable. Movement speed seems not fast, so probably no substeps or difficult math to
  avoid overshoot / collision failure are needed — but not sure.

Purpose of these engines: faster performance and visual gameplay. No need to be
over-precise — just keep all the behavior that is possible.

## Constraints carried into the plan

- The request is for a plan (a design document) first; a fork or reimplementation must be
  possible from the plan alone, with no reference to any chat.
- The existing engines keep their own tracks and pages: the column engine
  (`columns.html`, 0.1.x/0.2.x) and the FFT particle engine (`index.html` /
  `particles.html`, 0.3.x). The new engines are additive, one page each.
- The repository's standing rules apply: file:// friendly, classic script tags, no build,
  no modules, guarded `module.exports`, single-tab indentation, preallocated typed
  arrays, no allocation in the frame loop, vendored libraries as local files, one global
  per new file.
- Every number the plan asserts must be reproducible from a script in `experiments/`.
  The Box2D cost curve is already such a number (`experiments/planck-smoke.js`).

## What the plan had to answer, therefore

1. what each engine's state is: which material lives on the grid and which lives in
   particles, and who owns mass at every moment;
2. what replaces the spectral Stokes solve, and what "good enough, not over-precise"
   means as a gate rather than a hope;
3. the request's named decisions — smooth or not, size policy (same/different, constant/
   heat-dependent, the log-scale consequence), Box2D or not, substeps or not — each with a
   default, the GUI option that exposes it, and the measurement that can overturn it;
4. how the four named behaviors (convection, melting, eruption, unpressurize) become
   acceptance scenarios both engines are gated on;
5. what it costs per frame, from measurements, next to the FFT engine's own budget.
