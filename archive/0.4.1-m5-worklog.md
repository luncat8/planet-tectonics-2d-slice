# Milestone M5 Worklog (step 1): The Coupling Envelope

**Milestone:** 0.4.1 §8.3 (`pgt-coupling` v1) and §8.1's join key — the first of the two steps
M5 splits into. Witness: `experiments/coupling.js`, 42 checks, recorded in
`experiments/logs/0.4.1-coupling.txt`.

---

## 1. What step 1 is, and what it deliberately is not

Step 1 is the **envelope and the arithmetic in front of it**: what a coupling message is, what
it may not be, how a section column finds the interval that covers it, and what an import would
cost. Nothing in `js/coupling.js` mutates section state — `deltas` is a read-only measurement of
what a C4 reconcile would have in front of it. Applying the reconcile and booking its ledger
lines (`reconciled` / `diverged` / `fresh` / `retired`) is step 2, and the §8.2 ladder, the
paused-clock handshake and the §8.5 observation log come after that. The plan's M5 gate is not
met by this commit; the protocol's two halves and their arithmetic are.

## 2. What landed

### 2.1 `js/coupling.js` (global `COUP`)

- **The message**: `{ format, version, pathChecksum, tMyr, epochMa, sea, plates[], crust[],
  trenches[], ledger, checksum }`, exactly §8.3's shape. One canonical body, one checksum
  (`fnvText` over the canonical JSON — the pack's own hash, `js/deposit-core.js`), six
  significant digits on every number, `validate` for every rule §8.3 implies, `json`/`parse` for
  the wire and `matches` for the section's guard (a message for another line is refused, not
  blended).
- **`fromPack`**: derives a message from a verified cut. Plate intervals split where the plate
  id, its boundary type or its polarity changes and carry the run's mean `vt` and `|vp|`; crust
  is one entry per pack sample (the cut was taken at that resolution and merging neighbours that
  agree in the last digit buys nothing); trenches are the subduct samples; the ledger is
  `Σ thickness × width` per lithology, scaled the way the pack's arc is.
- **The section's half**: `joinTo` joins a column to the crust interval whose midpoint is within
  `JOIN_SPACING` (2) local spacings, on a periodic ring; `deltas` reports the matched/unmatched
  columns, per-field deltas, the grow/shrink volumes, the age and fertility moves; `clockOk`
  enforces §8.3's cadence rule (the section may not run ahead of the last message by more than
  one cadence).

### 2.2 `experiments/coupling.js` (42 checks)

- **A** (11 checks): a message derived from the pinned cut is the cut's own numbers — clock,
  epoch, sea, crust table, 24 plate intervals, 4 trenches, ledger, JSON round trip, quantisation
  idempotence.
- **B** (19 checks): refusals — foreign format, future version, bad path checksum, negative
  clock, no sea, a plate table that does not advance or leaves a gap, two tables ending at
  different arcs, negative thickness or age, damage above one, boundary and polarity codes out
  of range, trenches off the cut or unsorted, a negative ledger, an edited body that keeps the
  old checksum, a wire message with no checksum at all, and the guard's refusal of another cut.
- **C** (4 checks): a 20-message sequence at the plan's 5 Myr cadence from a world advanced and
  re-cut every cadence — every message validates, is its own cut, and the sequence advances the
  clock one cadence a step (120.5 → 220.5 Myr).
- **D** (8 checks): the section's half — a self-message books nothing beyond the pack's own
  integer metre, every column joins a whole-ring message, the clock rule holds across the
  sequence, the reconcile the sequence would book stays smaller than the crust it corrects, a
  message whose spans are wider than the join radius leaves those columns unjoined, a hard reset
  import keeps the ledger the message carried, and the import cost is measured (§9.5).

### 2.3 Corrections the coupling needed from M3's export (`js/section-seed.js`)

- **`sKm` is the cut's own arc**: cumulative column widths from the first column's boundary,
  never `j*w0` (the nominal lattice, which a transported ring no longer sits on) and never
  `S.colX` (an unwrapped ring coordinate whose origin drifts as the ring turns). `cellKm` is the
  widest span the ring carries, which is what the pack's own "last sample within a cell of the
  end" rule needs.
- **The host class is applied to the pack's own numbers** (`pack.hFelM` etc., already rounded),
  so a cut cannot say "thick continental" at 44 999.6 m while its own carried sample reads
  45 000.
- **The clock is asked for when a cut is written** (`simClock()`), not captured at file load.
  This also removes the harness-loader artefact where `js/section-seed.js`'s file-scope `SIM` was
  not the world's (a cut written under the loader carried `tMyr 0`).
- **`experiments/checkpoint.js` §D** now splits the self round-trip into D1 (the lattice-matched
  round trip, which is what the plan's "identical carried fields" can mean), D2 (the spin-up
  bound against a freshly laid section, measured with the engine's own `S.mass()`) and D3 (the
  re-cut of the widened ring the engine grows, where the gate is the mass identity and the
  convexity of the resample, not equality). 50 checks, recorded in
  `experiments/logs/0.4.1-checkpoint.txt`.

## 4. Decisions

- **Arc, not `colX`**, on both sides of the wire (module header). Cumulative widths are the only
  arc a periodic ring has.
- **The join key is a distance, not a nearest neighbour**: a message that tiles its own ring
  joins every column however it is dragged, because the nearest interval is never more than half
  a spacing away; the rule bites on messages with spans wider than the radius. That is the §8.1
  rule working, not a hole in it.
- **`deltas` measures, the next step books.** Keeping the measurement read-only is what lets the
  sequence harness run 20 imports without the section being mutated by its own gate.
- **One crust entry per sample** — the message keeps the cut's resolution instead of inventing a
  merge rule the pack did not have.

## 5. Next step suggestion

Step 2 of M5: add `COUP.apply(st, msg, opts)` that performs the C4 reconcile the `deltas`
measurement already describes (matched columns take the message's interval table, the ledger
books `reconciled` / `diverged` / `fresh` / `retired`, an unmatched column is `fresh` and its
mass booked as such), extend `experiments/coupling.js` §D to assert the 20-import mass identity
closes to 1e-9 and that beds stay on matched columns, and then wire the envelope into the page's
Slice panel as a paste-a-message action so the protocol has a non-live transport. The ladder
(§8.2) and the observation log (§8.5) follow once that identity holds.
