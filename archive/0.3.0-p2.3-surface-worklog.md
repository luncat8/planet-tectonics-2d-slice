# 0.3.0 P2.3 — surface relief and topographic coupling worklog

Follows `0.3.0-p2.2-slab-worklog.md`, which left the horizon ruler-flat at `y = 0`: the
separable FFT + Thomas Stokes solve needs a flat coordinate top (`psi = 0`, `v = 0` at
`eta = 0`), `keepInside` reflects markers at `yMin = 4.14 km`, and `render.js` painted `SKY`
for every screen row above `y = 0`. The plan's answer (added in that commit as §4.5 and
milestone P2.3) was a periodic zero-mean 1D elevation profile `S.zh[i]` coupled to a terrain
warp in the view and a ridge-push term in the plates.

This milestone implemented it, and the review that came with it found two defects in P2.2's
crust pass.

## 1. P2.2 review: two defects found and fixed

### 1.1 Sub-threshold markers became one-marker "plates"

`solid.js` numbers a cluster id for every *candidate* marker -- the `mu`-based filter it uses
so the load integral still covers markers that are near, but below, the plate threshold. Since
`S.cl[p] >= 0` is what `d.lid` and the raster count as plate, a lone soft marker with no
partners was counted as a plate.

Measured on the shipped `cool` run (`scratch/singleton.js`):

| t/Myr | clusters `csN` | of which singletons | weak members | `d.lid` |
|-------|----------------|---------------------|--------------|---------|
| 40 | 50 | 49 | 49 | 17.4% |
| 200 | 204 | 192 | 178 | 9.5% |
| 240 | 250 | 227 | 214 | 7.0% |

so **86-91% of clusters were spurious** and the HUD's lid fraction read about an eighth high.

Fix: three sentinels in `clusters()` -- `-1` below the candidate threshold, `-3` load-only
candidate, `-2` plate-capable -- with membership tested by the *same* expression `pair()` uses
to decide a bond (`d >= clusterMin`), and the `-3`s returned to `-1` after the pass. The load
integral still visits them, so the failure law is unchanged.

### 1.2 Ridge push and the plate gate

The same pass gained the P2.3 term: `csP[c] = sum_p m * (-dzh/dx)` over the cluster's members,
added to `csVX[c]` as `clamp(kPush * csP/csM, +-vPushMax)`, applied to *every* cluster (an
unbroken ring has no free trench end, but it does have a slope to slide down). A flat profile
gives exactly zero, so P2.2's slab-pull unit gates are untouched.

## 2. The surface profile (`js/pt/fluid.js` G7, `js/pt/solid.js`, `js/pt/render.js`)

`S.zh[i]` (km above the reference sea level, zero horizontal mean) relaxes over `tauSurf` to

```
z_raw[i] = z_crust[i] + flex( z_therm[i] + z_dyn[i] )
```

- **`z_therm`**: the upper-column temperature anomaly against each node row's own horizontal
  mean, integrated to `yIso = 150 km`, with `kIso = alpha * dT = 0.09` km per (T * km).
- **`z_dyn`**: `kDyn * yIso * du/dx` from the shallowest cell row's convergence;
  incompressibility makes the vertical flow at the compensation depth `-(du/dx) * y`. The
  plan's form on the vertical velocity at node row 1 is unusable: that row is inside the welded
  lid (`v = 0.03-0.4 km/Myr` against `uMax 20`), so the term is read from `u` instead.
- **`z_crust`**: `kLid * (hLid - flex(hLid)) - kRift * rft`, the welded-lid thickness anomaly
  against its own flexural average (the flexural response itself, so it is not filtered again)
  minus the `mu`-weighted shallow rift damage.

`zh` is drawn by a per-column warp in `render.js` (`zVis = kRelief * clip(zh, +-zVisMax)`,
damped `exp(-y / yTaper)`, converted through `1/J(eta)`), with an ocean band under the
reference sea level and the identical displacement applied to the markers. The `relief` switch
in the bar turns the whole warp off, sea included.

Two bugs the fixture caught, both invisible to the P2.1/P2.2 checks:

1. **the crust term was flexure-filtered twice**, which applies the plate's stiffness twice and
   cancelled the load: a 14 km welded band read `-0.13 km` instead of `+1.0 km`. Now only the
   thermal and dynamic part is filtered.
2. **`flex()` leaves its scratch equal to its output** (the `[1,2,1]/4` stencil is in-place),
   so `hLid - tmp` was exactly zero -- the whole thickness half of the term was dead. The
   kernel now copies the raw field first (`hRaw`).

## 3. Measured results

### 3.1 The live run (`experiments/pt-surface.js`, 250 Myr)

| quantity | value |
|----------|-------|
| mean elevation | `1.1e-16 km` (machine precision) |
| deepest 5% of columns ("trenches") | `-7.9 km` |
| hottest 5% of columns ("ridge/plume swells") | `+1.5 km` |
| coldest 5% ("abyssal plains") | `-7.7 km` |
| most divergent 5% ("dynamic domes") | `+1.7 km` |
| correlation with the upper-column thermal integral | `r = 0.93` |
| profile range | `-10.4 .. +7.7 km` |

The correlation is `1.00` at the start (thermal only), `0.48` at 120 Myr and `0.93` at 250 Myr:
while the lid is thin the dynamic term is a large share of the profile, which is physical and
is why the fixture gates the ordering rather than a fixed share.

`kDyn` is the profile's one calibrated number. At `1.0` the deepest trench read `-16 km`
because the thermal and dynamic terms correlate at a convergent margin and charge the same
column twice (thermal `-6.1`, dynamic `-10.1` at the deepest columns of a 250 Myr run);
`kDyn = 0.3` puts the deepest trench at `-8.7 km`, inside the plan's `-4..-8` band.

### 3.2 The hand-built states (the law, not the pipeline)

Each term is compared column by column against an independent spelling of §4.5:

| state | result |
|-------|--------|
| one hot column, `dT = 0.12` through `yIso` | `zh = +0.45 km` at the column, `-0.01 km` 8 columns away, matching the formula to `1.5e-15 km` |
| a convergence pattern, flat T | the deepest column is the most convergent (`dzh/dx = -0.017 /Myr`), matching to `3.3e-15 km` |
| a 3-column welded band with a damaged middle | band `+1.0 km`, seam `-1.2 km`, `+-1.3 km` moats either side, matching to `5.1e-15 km` |
| flat, quiet planet | exactly `0` |

### 3.3 The view

- the ocean band exists only below the reference sea level: `4870` water pixels, `0` of them
  above `y = 0`;
- the drawn rock top sits on the profile within a raster row in **all 320** columns;
- with `relief` off the horizon is flat at `y = 0` and there is no water at all;
- a marker on a swell is drawn `7` rows up and one in a basin `15` rows down, matching the warp
  law `kRelief * zh * exp(-y/yTaper) / J(y)` to `0.12 px` (including the `zVisMax` clip).

### 3.4 Ridge push

| case | result |
|------|--------|
| flat planet | `0.00e+0 km/Myr` |
| slope `-dzh/dx = 0.002` | exactly `kPush * 0.002 = 6 km/Myr` |
| mountain-front slope | capped at `vPushMax = 15 km/Myr` |
| P2.2's slab-pull units | unchanged (`pt-crust.js` 31 checks, `pt-drift.js` 5 checks) |

On a live run (`scratch/pushprobe.js`, 300 Myr) a plate's *mass-weighted mean* slope is
`0.0010-0.0042`, because the flanks of the swells a plate spans largely cancel inside one
cluster. That puts the push at `3.1-12.4 km/Myr` (`0.3-1.2 cm/yr`), under the `15` cap, which
is what the cap is for: a plate with a genuinely one-sided tilt. Mean plate drift over
`pt-drift.js`'s four samples rises from `1.60` to **`2.39 cm/yr`** with the push added
(`4.6x` the `0.52 cm/yr` of net flow under the plates), so the surface loop closes with the
slab pull rather than replacing it.

### 3.5 The price

`pt-check.js`'s shipped-config frame reads `11.7 ms`, the same as the recorded P2.2 number;
the isolated A/B on the `'rb'` Ra 1e6 config measured `13.83` (HEAD) against `14.67` (with
P2.3) ms/frame, i.e. **+0.84 ms** for the surface kernel, the stipple warp and the ridge push
together. The kernel alone is **0.33 ms/frame** (measured per kernel: transfer 3.56, flow 0.59,
move 4.53, crust 5.87, surface 0.33). Neither the kernel nor the warped raster and stipple
construct a typed array over 400 frames (`pt-surface.js`, with the typed-array constructors
wrapped and the ArrayBuffer delta checked).
