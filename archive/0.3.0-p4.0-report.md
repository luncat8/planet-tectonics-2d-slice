# 0.3.0 P4.0 — the contact kernel

P2.3's orogen term reads two surface columns whose plate velocities converge and raises a belt.
It is a kinematic proxy: nothing stops the two plates from ending up in the same ground, so the
convergence it reads is never resolved. P4.0 is the mechanical response that proxy was standing
in for, at the lid scale the plan's §1.4 scopes it to. One new file,
`js/pt/contact.js`, and the hooks that let it sit between the rigid fit and the projection.

Primary gate: `experiments/pt-p4-contact.js`, **ALL PASS (22 checks)** — the two controlled
regimes, the kernel-off control on the same fixture, the four ledgers bitwise across the
kernel's own calls, the arrest impulse pair, and the live planet census. Full serial release
validation with the kernel shipped on: **15 processes, 604 quoted checks (596 distinct)**, all
green. Logs are `experiments/logs/0.3.0-p4.0-*.txt`; the pre-change baseline of the same tree
with `P.contact` off is `experiments/logs/0.3.0-p4.0-base-*.txt`.

Momentum is not conserved engine-wide — the mantle advection itself moves markers by an
interpolated flow — and the subduction hold is deliberately unbalanced, because the reaction a
real override carries is mantle traction the one-way Stokes coupling does not model. The hold's
unbalanced impulse is therefore a named diagnostic, `S.contactHold`, booked per held side and
mixed into the fingerprint, rather than hidden. The arrest brake, whose balance *is* a property
of a single frame, is measured and closes exactly.

## The two regimes

A contact is a pair of adjacent surface columns held by different clusters whose velocities
close above `P.contactYield`, read from the same per-column shallowest-crust-marker table the
relief terms use. The material model has one density, so temperature is the only density proxy
the buoyancy law itself uses, and it is the **contrast** across the contact — not a label the
model does not carry — that decides whether the denser side can sink:

- **subduction**, `|dT| ≥ contactdT` (0.08): the colder plate keeps its velocity and its leading
  band bends down along `contactDip` (35°) beneath the override, whose approach is held. The
  band's velocity blends from the plate's rigid velocity at its inner edge to the slab velocity
  at the trench, so the bend is a rotation of the velocity, not a stretch, and the slab's speed
  is the convergence rate the contact measured.
- **arrest**, `|dT| < contactdT`: both clusters take a mass-weighted share of the correction
  that cancels the closing — one balanced impulse pair — and nothing bends. The convergence the
  pass locked up is booked in `S.ctConv`, which is now the orogen term's source at a resolved
  boundary: a locked boundary that keeps converging is a boundary that keeps thickening, and its
  belt is not the arc's.

## Shipped live result at 300 Myr (seed 1, 50 kyr/frame)

- 20 contacts in the worst frame, worst closing 1.949/Myr;
- 10,657 hold-frames and 166,112 bending markers over 6,000 frames;
- 12,821 km of absorbed shortening;
- 873 guard pushes on 650 frames (10.8%) — the velocity-level response is sufficient on the
  controlled legs and insufficient on a minority of live frames, where the lid has fragmented
  into many clusters whose fronts jitter. The guard separates every pair it finds; its scope is
  the table the pass builds, the lid band above `yCrust`, so the count is a lower bound;
- mass residual 0.00e+0, no refusals, no NaN;
- the pass itself: 0.152 ms/frame, 1.57% of the 9.65 ms frame, allocating nothing.
