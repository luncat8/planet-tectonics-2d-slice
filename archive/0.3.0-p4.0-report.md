# 0.3.0 P4.0 — the contact kernel

P2.3's orogen term reads two surface columns whose plate velocities converge and raises a belt.
It is a kinematic proxy: nothing stops the two plates from ending up in the same ground, so the
convergence it reads is never resolved. P4.0 is the mechanical response that proxy was standing
in for, at the lid scale the plan's §1.4 scopes it to. One new file,
`js/pt/contact.js`, and the hooks that let it sit between the rigid fit and the projection.

Primary gate: `experiments/pt-p4-contact.js`, **ALL PASS (31 checks)** — the two controlled
regimes, the kernel-off control on the same fixture, the four ledgers bitwise across the
kernel's own calls, the arrest impulse pair, the subduction hold's unbalanced impulse, the
bending cap, the domain, the guard's own residue and the live planet census. Full serial release
validation with the kernel shipped on: **15 processes, 604 quoted checks (596 distinct)**, all
green. Logs are `experiments/logs/0.3.0-p4.0-*.txt`; the pre-change baseline of the same tree
with `P.contact` off is `experiments/logs/0.3.0-p4.0-base-*.txt`.

The milestone was reviewed after it landed; `archive/0.3.0-p4.0-review-worklog.md` is that
record — seven defects in the kernel, an override that had no root and therefore nothing for the
hold to hold, and the residue hunt that decided what "zero hard overlaps" is measured against.
The numbers below are the reviewed ones.

Momentum is not conserved engine-wide — the mantle advection itself moves markers by an
interpolated flow — and the subduction hold is deliberately unbalanced, because the reaction a
real override carries is mantle traction the one-way Stokes coupling does not model. The hold's
unbalanced impulse is therefore a named, signed diagnostic, `S.contactHold` (Σ `csM·Δvx` over the
brake's two sides, so an arrest pair cancels and books nothing), mixed into the fingerprint. The
arrest brake, whose balance *is* a property of a single frame, is measured and closes exactly.

## The two regimes

A contact is a pair of adjacent surface columns held by different clusters whose velocities
close above `P.contactYield`, read from the same per-column shallowest-crust-marker table the
relief terms use. The material model has one density, so temperature is the only density proxy
the buoyancy law itself uses, and it is the **contrast** across the contact — not a label the
model does not carry — that decides whether the denser side can sink:

- **subduction**, `|dT| ≥ contactdT` (0.08): the colder plate keeps its velocity and its leading
  band bends down along `contactDip` (35°) beneath the override, whose approach is held. The
  band's velocity blends from the plate's rigid velocity at its inner edge to the slab velocity
  at the trench, and the slab's speed is the **subducting plate's own** trench-normal approach —
  the band belongs to one plate, so the closing of the pair stretched it.
- **arrest**, `|dT| < contactdT`: both clusters take a mass-weighted share of the correction
  that cancels the closing — one balanced impulse pair — and nothing bends. The convergence the
  pass locked up is booked in `S.ctConv`, which is now the orogen term's source at a resolved
  boundary: a locked boundary that keeps converging is a boundary that keeps thickening, and its
  belt is not the arc's.

## The guard, and what its residue means

The guard is the discrete statement of the plan's no-interpenetration gate: every inter-cluster
pair in the frame's table closer than `P.contactHard` of a cell is pushed apart along its own
separation, half each, so neither plate is preferred. One sweep is not always enough — where
several pairs share a marker, separating p from q can push p into r — so it sweeps while it finds
work, bounded by `P.ctSweep` (3), and publishes what the last sweep left as `S.d.resid`.

Two measurements decided the details. A sweep budget scan found that more sweeping did not clear
the live residue (3 pairs survived 10 sweeps *and* the push count grew), and an in-situ dump of a
residual frame showed why: the pairs were sitting **on** the radius, `d/hard = 1.000`. The push
lands a pair on the radius to within an ulp and a strict test then finds it still inside, so the
solver chased its own rounding forever. `sweep()` squares its radius with a rounding slop
(`hard² · (1 − 1e−12)`) and the fixture's independent count uses the same slop; the push count
fell from 2,870 to 1,318 — 1,552 of the pushes this milestone first reported were the guard
re-separating pairs it had already separated — and the residue went to **0 on every frame**. Two
sweeps are what the live planet needs; the third is a no-op that costs nothing measurable and is
kept as margin.

The residue is read inside `guard()`, where the table is live, because a frame's later kernels
move the parcels the table names: `pool.js remove()` compacts the tail into the hole it made, and
a wholly molten parcel leaves for the melt pass. An end-of-frame re-read is not this number, and
the fixture says so.

## Shipped live result at 300 Myr (seed 1, 50 kyr/frame, `P.slab` off)

- 26 contacts in the worst frame, worst closing 2.053/Myr;
- 20,061 hold-frames and 346,095 bending markers over 6,000 frames;
- 30,775 km of absorbed shortening;
- 1,318 guard pushes on 922 frames (15.4%) — the velocity-level response is sufficient on the
  controlled legs and insufficient on a minority of live frames, where the lid has fragmented
  into many clusters whose fronts jitter. The guard separates every pair it finds; its scope is
  the table the pass builds, the lid band above `yCrust`, so the count is a lower bound;
- 0 unbent sites against `ctHinge` 32, worst residual 0, booked impulse −5.21e+9 against a plate
  momentum of 3.00e+6;
- mass residual 0.00e+0, 0 parcels outside the domain, no refusals, no NaN;
- the pass itself: 0.157 ms/frame, 1.36% of the 11.55 ms frame, allocating nothing.

The sites the pass answers include 1- and 2-marker clusters — lid shrapnel from the shipped
failure law, one of them at 312 cm/yr — as well as plates. Whether the table should require
`P.plateMin` is a P4.4 freeze question next to `contactYield` and `contactHard`, recorded with
its measurement in the review worklog rather than changed mid-milestone.
