# 0.1.8 — Plan: collision arrest and event-stable orogens

The column engine has a rate-stable crust ceiling, but continent–continent convergence is
not arrested by the current instantaneous belt scan. The brake fades as its measured belt
shrinks, even after the collision has built and redistributed an orogen. This release
chooses and tests a built-orogen measure before it changes the plate force.

M0 has run (§3): **no candidate measure is selected, and the reason is measured rather than
fitted** — the prescribed fixture stops building after its first squeeze, so the arrest
contract would be a shape test on a stopped machine. Accumulation (M1) therefore comes
before the brake, and R4 is restated in §2 to say so.

M1 has since landed and responds (the C-C conveyor retires records and the orogen keeps
growing), and M2 has selected its brake by measurement: **D, the accumulated boundary
shortening**, frozen at `k = 2e5`, meets the arrest contract on seeds 1 and 5 at both frame
rates (§5 M2 outcome, `archive/0.1.8-m2-worklog.md`). D is landed in the engine as
`S.edgeShort` (§4, §5 M2 landing) and the M3 matrix has run: R4 is **MET** on all four
seed/rate rows, three strict audit legs are 11/11 and one (`5000/5/100`) reads 8/11 on a
re-rolled trajectory (§5 M3, `archive/0.1.8-worklog.md`).

The previous engine plans are `0.1.6-plan.md` (§5, the R4 fixture) and `0.1.7-plan.md`
(§2, the dt-aware ceiling; §3, the collision contract re-measured). The corrected
contact-audit baseline and review findings are in `archive/0.1.7-review-followup.md`. M0's
full log is `experiments/logs/0.1.8-m0.txt`.

## 1. Current evidence and problem statement

`beltFel`. With honest, stack-built crust the belt does form — the corrected R4 fixture
reaches 378–400 km of width and 23.9 km of root — but the contact then consumes or

belt 378 → 400 km (the width must not be decaying while the drive continues), closing

remains **R4 NOT MET**; a speed limit is not an acceptable substitute for a physical brake.

## 2. Contracts

1. **R1 — Events stay quiet.** The largest drawn-surface movement within the actual birth or
   death site is no more than `P.evDzK` times the largest movement elsewhere on that same
   frame. The site comes from the event bucket, never from a `NaN` or a proxy index.
2. **R2 — A collision is a belt, not a needle.** Define the needle by the local shape of the
   collision pair, not its height against distant terrain:
   - For each non-ghost collide pair `i,j=i+1`, let `peak = max(hTot[i],hTot[j])` and
     `shoulder = max(hTot[i-1],hTot[j+1])`, where each shoulder slot is read as the nearest
     **real** column: a draining record holds no ground (`hTot ~ 0`, retired by its trench
     within a few frames) and is stepped over, bounded to `P.beltFeed + 1` columns each way;
     a slot with no real column reads 0, which keeps the old reading for a pair with no
     ground beside it. The pair is a needle only when it rises above **both** immediate
     shoulders: require `peak / shoulder <= P.beltPeak` (1.5). The shoulder is real ground
     of any kind: there is no felsic or plate-ownership test, so a pair whose higher
     shoulder is the opposing plate's thinner margin is still held to the same ratio. That
     is deliberate — the clause then measures the profile, and whether a belt should be
     defined against same-plate or same-crust ground is a physics/definition question for
     a later plan, not a threshold to move. Measured instance, seed 5/50, `x 9811.8 km`: a
     welding felsic margin (`hTot` 13.2 -> 17.9 km, `hFel` 10.2 -> 14.7 over five frames)
     against a felsic block beside a mafic plateau reads 2.02 / 1.75 / 1.56 for exactly
     three frames and falls below 1.5 on the fourth; `COL.beltAt` already reports 5
     columns and 380 km of belt at the same sample and the contiguous-width clause passes
     there. The alternative shoulder filters were measured and do not help: a felsic-only
     shoulder reads 8.5 km at that site (4.3x, worse), and a shoulder restricted to the
     peak's own plate leaves the 18.1 km read unchanged. The red is therefore a real
     onset transient of the specified shape, not a ghost or a draining record, and the
     gate keeps it red until a later plan decides the definition. Probe:
     `scratch/probe-peek.js`. This is a measurement correction of the same named
     quantity, not a threshold: skipping a draining record only raises the shoulder and the
     shoulder only lowers the ratio, so it cannot turn a passing pair red.
     (`experiments/contact-audit.js --detail` reports the raw-slot reading beside the
     selected one, and the section it reads is `scratch/probe-peek.js`.)
   - On built samples (`peak >= outerFlank + P.beltRoot`), inspect the six cells `i-2..i+3`
     above `outerFlank + P.beltRise`. Require a **contiguous** run of at least `P.beltCols`
     (4) in at least 90% of built collision samples. `P.beltRoot`, `P.beltRise`, `P.beltCols`
     and the 90% allowance keep their existing values; contiguity is the specified shape.
   - Keep the former `peak / outerFlank` ratio and non-contiguous four-of-six count in strict
     reports as legacy comparison fields, not as the R2 decision. The outer-flank metric can
     reject a broad elevated/twin-crest belt even when the pair is below its immediate
     shoulders; it measures relief contrast, not needle shape.

   This is the R2 profile audit, not a redefinition of the accumulated belt used by physics:
   `COL.beltAt` continues to provide the shared contiguous outer-flank width for the crustal
   flow and plate-force terms, and rendering continues to show the actual profile. The new
   local-needle and contiguous-six-cell criteria must be named and computed identically in
   `contact-audit.js` and `orogen-measure.js`; do not change `COL.beltAt`, the drawing or the
   force law to make a red audit green.

   Legacy evidence is retained for comparison: in the prior strict legs, excluding flanks
   that are not continental on both sides reduced the worst outer-flank ratio from 4.91–6.64
   to **1.46–1.87** (gate 1.5), while either-flank, pair-versus-neighbours and
   nearest-continental variants stayed at 1.60–5.46. The current logs preserve the old
   arithmetic; future R2 logs report both old and selected measurements.
3. **R3 — The ceiling remains honest.** `maxH <= crustMax + 35 km/Myr × dt` on both frame
   rates; receiver headroom and the foundering ledger remain exact. The column separation
   floor must measure the **circular** distance between two records: a pair that crossed
   inside the frame is a few hundred metres apart, not a full wrap apart, and a floor that
   misses it lets `COL.k4` hand the overtaken record collapsed territory whose conserved
   volume spikes the thickness over the ceiling in one frame (measured before the fix:
   an 80 km record at 136 km, 86.8 km after delamination). Any future floor-like gap read
   keeps the short-way correction.
4. **R4 — The collision is arrested by its orogen.** A continent–continent contact must keep
   accommodating convergence after the separation floor: for as long as the drive keeps the
   pair converging, the pair shortens mass-exact and the orogen it builds (belt width, root,
   excess volume) keeps responding, at 50 and 100 kyr/frame. Only on such a fixture does the
   arrest test apply: the closing speed in the final quarter is at most 75% of the
   first-quarter mean, the brake-off control remains above 90% of its early speed, and the
   collision remains measurable for the whole run. Report the instantaneous belt, the
   accumulated orogen measure and the force each frame; do not hide a growing or stale
   memory behind one final number. If the prescribed fixture stops building after the first
   squeeze (M0), the arrest numbers are not a verdict on any measure — the missing physics
   is accumulation, and a velocity clamp is not a fix.
5. **R5 — Events do not flicker.** No same-site birth/death reversal occurs within
   `P.evGap` frames. Measure at 50 and 100 kyr/frame on seeds 1 and 5. "Same site" is
   measured the way the kernel binds events, i.e. on the `COL.beltAt` site buckets
   (2.5 `w0` wide), which re-detect a reversal once a seam's relative motion changes
   sign at the same x. The kernel's own anti-repeat promise is the shorter `P.evAge`
   window (2 Myr of simulated time); the diagnosis reports both counts. Measured in the
   current legs: the gate window holds 6 reversals (2 of 383 events at 3000/1/100, 4 of
   390 at 5000/5/100) and **0 fall inside `P.evAge`** — every one is 2.2–3.9 Myr of
   simulated time apart, i.e. the boundary genuinely reversed after the kernel's memory
   expired. Five are a `consume` trench sliver followed by a `rift` birth at the same scar
   (`scratch/probe-r5d.js`), one is a C-C conveyor retirement followed by a `rift`; the
   birth lands 3–77 km from the death along the same site bucket, and the records drift
   apart again afterwards. A residual repeat that
   sits outside `P.evAge` is therefore a documented limit of the frame-window gate, not a
   kernel edit target. Do not widen `P.evGap`, `P.evAge`, or the bucket size.
6. **Mass and replay stay deterministic.** The per-lithology ledger closes to the existing
   tolerances, repeated runs hash identically, and save/load resumes the new state bitwise.

R1, R2, R3 and R5 are gates alongside R4, not optional regressions to revisit after the
brake works.

## 3. The model decision (M0): measured outcome

`experiments/orogen-measure.js` ran the two candidates and the incumbent through one brake
hook on the same fixture, with coefficients swept, fit on seed 1 and checked on seed 5:

```text
m = k * W * fb * (-edgeRelN) / vRef      W: instant COL.beltW | A Vex/beltRise | B edge memory
```

- **A — plateau excess volume.** `Vex = sum(max(0, hFel[c] - hFelLand0) * colW[c])` over the
  contact catchment (bounded by `beltMaxCols`, stopped at a draining or ghost column),
  divided by `beltRise` so it reads in the same km units as a belt width. Derived from the
  stacks; no new state.
- **B — decaying edge memory.** A bounded per-edge scalar holding the largest belt width the
  edge has built, fast to build (tauUp 1 Myr) and slow to forget (tauDown 50 Myr).

Result at 4000 frames, 50 kyr/frame, seed 1 (log: `experiments/logs/0.1.8-m0.txt`):

| candidate | strongest row that stays live | live at | late/early | belt | R2 |
| --------- | ----------------------------- | ------- | ---------- | ---- | -- |
| instant   | k 2e6 (absorbs 84% of control) | 4000 | 111% | 400 km | no |
| A         | k 1e6 (absorbs 88% of control) | 4000 | 117% | 400 km | no |
| B         | k 2e6 (absorbs 84% of control) | 4000 | 109% | 400 km | no |

No coefficient of any candidate meets the arrest contract on seed 1; the smallest late/early
ratios reachable at all are 33–35%, and every such row has already stopped being a collision
(619–659 live frames, peak height cut to 50–54 km). The strongest rows that do stay live
absorb 84–88% of the control closing and still end at 109–117% of their own first quarter —
slower, but never arrested. **Selection: none.**

The reason is the fixture, and it is measured, not fitted:

- The pair gap reaches the `P.gFloor * P.w0` = 9.38 km separation floor at frame 47 and then
  does not move: the reported closing is 32.7 mm/yr, the real gap motion after the floor is
  **0.000 mm/yr** (filtered average; raw first differences are pair renumbering, not
  motion). Column count 512 → 513, plate widths 20015/20015 → 19981/20049 km, belt
  380 → 400 km and root 5.9 → 3.0 km, then flat. Nothing is consumed, so nothing keeps
  shortening: the drive is absorbed by pushing whole plates back out.
- A fed collision (a ribbon continent with an ocean trench feeding it, brake off) does not
  accumulate either: peak 35.0 → 25.4 km, margin ends at 44.4 km against the 35 km
  reference, `Vex` 3.31e9 m² — the incoming crust spreads into a lower, wider margin.
- The def planet holds a continental collision for only ~40–50 Myr: `scratch/probe-accum.js`
  finds 1–2 collide edges at t 10–40 Myr and none from t 50 to 150 Myr, and the 4000-frame
  M0 run last sees one at t 25 Myr. R4's 200 Myr fixture prescribes a state the engine never
  reaches on its own.

What the sweep does say, and what M1 must not waste: the authority to arrest is one constant
away — A at k 1e6 absorbs 88% of the control closing (4.1 → 4.8 mm/yr against 39.0) with
R1/R3/R5 intact — and every stronger row sutures the collision below `P.vSuture` and ends
the measurement. So the brake is not the missing piece; the orogen that keeps growing is,
and "late <= 75% of early" is a shape test on a machine that has already stopped.

C1 — letting the floored pair keep shortening — has since been measured
(`scratch/m1-c1.txt`): it absorbs the 9.38 km tail of the first squeeze and nothing more,
so §5 M1 specifies the conveyor instead of the floor change alone.

## 4. Ownership and serialization

The M1 mechanism and the M2 choice determine the schema delta:

- A derived inventory must specify its finite catchment and have no history-dependent
  hidden cache. If cached for cost, the cache must be reproducible after reset, checkpoint
  load and topology changes.
- A persistent edge value belongs to the edge, not to a plate ID or a raster index that
  can be reassigned. Column remeshing, birth, consumption, wrap and polarity changes need
  explicit transfer/retirement rules, checked against the `COL.fields` gather. (B's 0.1.8
  probe cleared its per-edge slot on every re-hand; a chosen memory needs the same proof.)
- A new checkpoint array/scalar changes the column checkpoint record table and requires a
  checkpoint version bump plus corrupt/old-version refusal tests. Save/load must preserve
  the memory and the next frame hash.
- The v1 slice-pack fields are frozen in `port/slice-format.js`. If the chosen state must
  cross a globe/section cut or C4 import to reproduce the force, define a versioned format
  change and coordinate the byte-identical `port/` counterpart and its `PORT.json` manifest.
  Do not silently add a field to v1 or invent a second codec. If the memory is local-only,
  state explicitly what a cut/import assumes and ledger that assumption.

What D's landing owns, under these rules:

- `S.edgeShort` (Float64Array, metres) is **one scalar per boundary**: the pair of adjacent
  records of different plates. It is cleared when the pair stops existing — the records stop
  being neighbours (`COL.transport`'s sort test, `COL.k4`'s hand-off), a newborn takes the
  slot (`COL.rift`), or the two sides become one plate (`PLT.classify`'s same-plate branch,
  which is where a suture lands). It is **not** cleared by a boundary state change: a braked
  collision that drops below `epsLo` reads neutral for a while, and resetting the memory
  there would release the brake and re-start the collision from zero, an oscillation.
- It is **not** gathered with `COL.fields`: `COL.transport` carries it by hand with the same
  neighbour test, and `COL.k4` snapshots it with `histAge`/`histSlow` and hands it through
  `COL.handEdge` in both survival branches. The increment (`COL.released`) is booked at the
  conveyor retirement on the surviving boundary: the pair's gap after K4 minus the retired
  pair's gap before it (`COL.histGap`), one released column of territory.
- It is a checkpoint array (checkpoint `VERSION` 3 → 4; a version-3 file is refused by its
  version word, `experiments/checkpoint.js`). It is **not** a slice-pack field: `port/`
  stays byte-identical and v1 is frozen. A cut starts it at zero because the pack has no
  field for it, and a C4 import (`js/coupling.js` `syncEdges`) restarts it at zero on every
  boundary: the boundaries written there are the globe's, and the brake rebuilds from the
  section's own retirements. No mass rides on it, so the ledger is unaffected.
- `experiments/m2-check.js` gates the booking (the retired pair's released territory lands
  on the surviving boundary and nowhere else) and, in every `invariants()` pass, that it
  sits only on live inter-plate boundaries.

### M1 — Accumulation (the new first milestone)

The C-C contact must keep accommodating convergence after the floor. Today the floor is a
bound on the pair: the drive pushes the plates into each other, `COL.floor` pushes whole
plates back out, and the pair's territory never shrinks. `COL.intents`' crush path (intent
2) only fires for a column whose *two* gaps are at the floor — the squeeze-from-both-sides
case — so an ordinary C-C pair reaches no consuming state at all, and its comment ("a C-C
edge consumes nothing at all … the pair squeezes against the floor instead") describes
shortening the geometry never performs.

**C1 has been measured in `scratch/probe-shorten.js` (log: `scratch/m1-c1.txt`) and it is
not enough on its own.** Letting a closing collide pair keep its overlap down to a crush
floor does absorb shortening (spacing 78 → 3.91 km, the two boundary records thinning and
thickening mass-exact), but the *budget* is small: the pair's spacing is only `gFloor*w0`
= 9.38 km wide when the floor engages, so the last 12% of the squeeze is all C1 adds —
Vex 2.27 → 2.41e9 m² (+6%), belt 400 → 395 km, and R4's arithmetic is unchanged (76% on
the 2000-frame control, 78% brake-off). The fixture's entire orogen is *one squeeze*: the
drive closes the contact spacing from a normal column spacing (~78 km) down to the floor,
and those 34 km of width per boundary record are the whole 2.3e9 m² budget. The floor only
denies the tail of it.

Two measured hazards for whatever replaces it:

- The exemption must be keyed on the pair's own closing motion (`colU[i] > colU[j]`), not
  on the previous frame's edge class: the sort re-hands the edge, and with an edge-keyed
  test the pair crossed and produced a *second* collide edge at frame 52, then reset to the
  separation floor.
- The crush floor must exceed one frame of convergence (1.5 km at 50 kyr/frame, 3.0 at
  100 kyr), or the records cross between frames even though the floor corrects afterwards.
  At a 0.78 km floor the fixture crossed; at 3.91 km it sat still and stable for 300 frames.

Deliverable: a mass-exact **conveyor**. At the crush floor with sustained convergence the
boundary records have absorbed all the shortening they can, so one of them must be retired
and its stack accreted into the orogen (a move, not the ledger's consumed sink), which lets
the contact advance one column and the cycle repeat. One step releases a whole column of
territory (~78 km of shortening, ~2.7e9 m² of crust budget — as much as the initial squeeze
again) at a rate of roughly one per 52 frames at this drive; that is the growth the brake
measure needs over R4's 200 Myr. Sub-candidates to measure against each other before one is
kept:

- **C1a — hold the pair, consume the thinner record.** The thinner of the two boundary
  records is retired (index tie-break) and its volume split between its plate-mate and the
  other plate's boundary record, which is exactly the territory each gains; the stack keeps
  its volume and the plates keep their identity.
- **C1b — intent 2 widened.** The same retirement, entered through `COL.intents`' crush
  path (intent 2) so the existing `COL.k4` machinery owns it.

Gates, all measured on the prescribed fixture and on the def planet:

- the filtered real gap motion equals the reported closing speed (M0's 0.000 mm/yr is the
  bug being fixed);
- the pair's `hTot`, the belt width, the root and the excess volume keep responding to the
  drive for the whole run, at 50 and 100 kyr/frame — a collision that stops building is a
  failure even if its numbers look calm;
- mass-exact: the ledger closes, `S.widths` stays positive, and where `bedMin`, `layerCap`
  or `crustMax` bind the material goes to the existing backlog/ledger, not to a pop; the
  accreted record's volume is a move inside the crust (no `ledCons` sink);
- the retirement rate stays clear of the event gates: one step per ~52 frames at 50
  kyr/frame against `P.evGap` = 40, measured, with the R1 site movement and R5 reversals
  reported per step;
- R1/R2/R3/R5 unchanged on the four strict audit legs; frame cost inside the existing
  budget (M0 rows run 1.4–1.7 ms/frame).

measurement before it changes a hot path:

  file should stop implying it does.
