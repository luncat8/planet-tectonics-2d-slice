# 0.3.2 M5 — the material correspondence argument: new deposits, not the control's three in different clothing

M4 §7 leaves one question and one instrument missing. The deposit-scoped bound is the first row of
the family that removes the held-back concentration without emptying the surface pipeline or
saturating the melt pool, and it delivers two transfers where the control delivers three, with none
of the control's three material ids among them. Whether those two are the control's three in
different clothing — the defect displaced — or new deposits the ordinary pipeline runs is not a
question a count can answer. M5 builds the instrument that can, answers it, and walks into a second
mover on the way.

Banks: `experiments/logs/0.3.2-M5-baseline-trace-600.json` (control, five ids) and
`experiments/logs/0.3.2-M5-frb025-dep-trace-600.json` (candidate, six). Specification:
`0.3.2-plan.md` §M5. Assertions: the M5 section of `experiments/pt-heat-tail-check.js`.

## 1. The instrument, and what it costs

`--trace-id a,b,c` on `experiments/pt-heat-tail.js`. For each named material id, from the frame it
first appears to the frame it last does: its admission (frame, site, depth, mass, phase); its burial
and heating history on a 200-frame spine with every frame that does something to it in full (at or
above its own solidus − 0.05, molten, capped by the bound, held back by the field support,
delivered, a phase change, the last frame); every act of the bound on it (the equilibrium
temperature the delivery would have reached, the two sides of the bound at that parcel, what was
withheld); and its routing fate, with its extremes and the frames that set them. Two things ride
along: `hottest` now carries the material id, because the outlier this release is about was banked
as a temperature, a frame and a place — enough to report, not enough to trace; and the mesh repair's
counters (`movedSum`, `movedMax`, `movedFrames`, `redeals`) are banked from here on, because §5
needs them at the population and not at six parcels.

Neutrality is measured, not claimed. The traced control reproduces **6191250240167390** and the
traced candidate **1808048552219233** — the fingerprints of their own untraced worlds — and every
diagnostic the family was read on is the banked one field for field: the transfers down to their
gains and shares, `heldSamples`/`heldMassTime`/`heldMeltTime`/`heldGapMax`,
`spreadCapped`/`spreadGiven`, `mantleRoutes`, `depTx`, `depMelt`, `refuse`, `intakeWallWorst`,
`minT`, `maxT` and the census. The short cells agree as well: `baseline 0 20` is 5534361194548428
traced and untraced, `frb 0.25 20 --scope dep` is 8159759613119221, `frb 0.25 20 --scope solid` is
2818612275077783. The trace costs 0.004 ms/frame on the control (11.696 untraced, 11.700 traced).

`circ Dx` is the shorter way round the 16000 km wrap):

| 217799 | f11217 x10919 y−1.5 m0.085 ph2 | f11248 x15625 y−1.8 m0.195 ph3 | 4706 | +31 | 48 / 164 | 0 / 1 |

Every id the two runs share is admitted at a different site, by 3537 to 7928 km, most in a different
phase, with a different mass and a different lifetime — 55 frames of airborne material in the
control against 884 frames of buried deposit in the candidate, for the id the candidate delivered
first. The admission *frames* stay within 34 of each other, because the counter keeps step with the
eruption cadence while the material it names does not. That is precisely what makes an id match look
available here.

The divergence precedes every traced admission: the earliest is frame 3846/3847 and the two runs
already disagree about what that id is. Ids come from `S.nextId`, a split's child is a new id and a
merge survivor takes the smaller one, so once the deliveries differ the same material carries
different ids in the two runs. For a bound that acts on every delivery, that means condition 5's
material branch — "the same material ids routing with field support" — cannot be met by *any* row of
this family: the only candidate that keeps the ids is one that changes nothing. The correspondence
branch is the only one open, and it has to be argued from what each run's own material does.

Two caveats belong to the instrument itself. Within one run a traced id is a lineage, not a fixed
body of material: a merge survivor takes the smaller id, and the traced masses run 0.075 → 0.603 km²
and 3.076 → 50.000 km² (the pool's own cap) as a result. And §5 shows the mesh repair moves a parcel
without touching its id. Neither disturbs the readings used here — admission, delivery, caps — and
both are why the argument reads sites and histories rather than following one body of rock.

## 3. The candidate's two transfers are ordinary pipeline deposits

| id | admitted | routed / held | hottest | delivered | shed into host | T / solidus / field |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 124861 | f7725 x3437.5 y−1.20 m0.031 ph3 | 133 / 132 | f9929 T 0.9694 | f9930 x4071.8 y139.39 | 0.5044 into 1.2667 | 0.9693 / 0.9096 / 0.9297 |

Both are the protected class of event: deposit material admitted at the surface, buried, held at the
routing threshold by the field support until its own field reaches its own solidus, then routed —
0.0597 and 0.0223 of T above solidus, field above solidus in both cases, zero refusals. Neither is a
tail transfer. Both live to the end of the run (10.65 km² at y 17.9 km, and 22.35 km² at y 26.2 km
after wrapping the domain).

The bound's own act on them, read at the material: **332 caps on 124861** withholding −55.8339
km²·H in total, 316 of them on the cold side — there the bound warms the parcel back toward its own
field — with a largest single act of 0.5882; **25 caps on 217799** withholding +9.1384 km²·H, 18 of
them on the hot side, largest 3.5313. Both parcels end the run above their own fields, by 0.2508 at
y 63.6 km and 0.5311 at y 8.1 km: the first is the bound's own width, the second is wider than it,
because the bound limits what a *delivery* may do and not what burial and advection do afterwards.
Neither approaches the control's worst held gap of 1.3277 or its deposit maximum of 2.2671.

One coincidence deserves naming, because it is what makes a count look like a correspondence. The
candidate's first transfer is admitted at x 3437.5 and delivered at x 4071.8; the control's third is
admitted at x 3330.3 and delivered at x 4061.2 — 107 km apart at admission, 10.6 km apart at
delivery, the same volcanic province in both worlds. The frames (7725 and 9930 against 10188 and
10660), the depths (139.4 against 49.1 km), the admission phases (deposit against airborne) and the
masses say it is the same belt doing its ordinary work twice, not the same event.

## 5. What the trace walked into: the mesh repair moves material

The control's 42152 does not descend smoothly. Between frames 9348 and 9349 it is at (x 7434, y
237.0) and then at (x 7125, y 80.7) — 309 km laterally and 156 km up in one 50 kyr frame, with its
mass, composition and heat continuous through the move. That is `donor()` in `js/pt/grid.js`:
`F.transfer` calls `G.reseed` when a node's deposited weight falls below `M.cover`, and reseed's
donor pull takes a marker from a bucket that has a spare and places it on the hole, assigning the
hole's coordinates and leaving id, mass, composition and heat alone. The search runs in square rings
of radius 2, 8 then 32 nodes, and `dx` is 62.5 km, so the widest ring reaches 2000 km; `G.redeal`'s
global lattice permutation sits behind it as the last resort.

Counted over the traced parcels: the control's 42152 is relocated **10 times in 1484 sampled
frames**, by up to **737 km in depth** and 376 km laterally — its relocations take it 289 → 551 →
1265 → 448 → 295 → 239 km, and after its delivery 237 → 81 → 101 → 81 → 49 → 8 km, with advection
in between; 165372 twice and 194306 twice; the two short-lived airborne ids never. In the candidate,
124861 twice (75 km laterally, 70 km in depth) and 217799 once (245 km laterally).

At the population, now banked in both worlds:

| | control | candidate |
|---|---|---|
| markers relocated, 600 Myr | 13,234 | 13,439 |
| frames with a relocation | 5,209 (43%) | 5,126 (43%) |
| most in one frame | 12 | 17 |
| global lattice re-deals | 0 | 0 |

Three readings follow. It is routine, not exceptional: 1.1 markers moved per frame on average, 2.5
on the frames where it fires, and never once the global fallback. It is not what the deposit-scoped
bound changes: the candidate moves 1.5% *more* markers than the control, so the 98× fall in the
held-back concentration is not the repair firing less. And it is a non-local heat mover whose
side-effect is visible in the trace — one parcel's widest departure from its own field is reached
three frames after a relocation that lifted it 156 km, into a field of 0.175 from one of 0.76: hot
material placed in a cold neighbourhood keeps its heat and only afterwards relaxes toward the field
it was dropped into.

The repair is not a defect. It exists because a node no marker reaches keeps its last frame's value,
and a stale patch is a buoyancy source that does not exist — measured, leaving holes stale drove a
run at Ra 1e6 into a grid-scale instability. What is new is that its heat side-effect is large per
parcel, routine in aggregate, and had never been counted.

## 6. What M5 says

The correspondence argument, in the only form that is well-posed for a candidate that changes the
delivery at all:

- The candidate's two transfers are **new deposits the ordinary pipeline runs** — different
  material, admitted thousands of km from anything the control admits under those ids, delivered
  field-supported within 0.06 of T of their own solidus after 132 and 28 held frames. The bound did
  not displace the defect into two smaller events.
- The defect is **absent rather than moved**: the candidate's deposit phase never exceeds T 1.1136
  against the control's 2.2671, its worst held T − field gap is 0.2503 — the bound's own width —
  against 1.3277, and its held-back concentration is 694.73 against 68,140.14 km²·Myr.
- The protected **class** of event survives: surface deposit, burial, heating, field-supported
  routing, zero refusals, a live surface pipeline (landCr 980,363 km², 28 melt parcels, 1766
  deposits against the control's 967,824 km², 38 and 1350). But the **channel runs thin**: `depMelt`
  1.2838 km² against 9.7302 km², a factor of 7.6, and the control's flux is dominated by one event
  (7.4359 km² from a 50.00 km² host) for which the candidate has no counterpart — its largest host
  is 5.24 km².

So the row is **still not shown eligible**, and the ground is now a measurement instead of a count:
it removes the tail and keeps the class, at a seventh of the deposit→melt flux. Whether that seventh
is the bound's doing or the divergence's is not separable from one world per row — with n = 1, a
single large event decides the ratio.

One gate moves in the row's favour, and M4 §7 had it wrong. The M4 bank read 21.589 ms/frame with
`--watch` on (the cold path, the band table and the per-sample gain and share readings) and so
recorded `gates.frame` false; the same cell with the trace instead of the watch instruments reads
**15.768 ms/frame and passes**. Neither bank separates the row's own cost from its instruments, but
the row is not over the frame-time gate on this host because of itself.

## 7. Next step

1. **Separate the bound from the divergence.** Run the control and `frb 0.25 --scope dep` on two or
   three further seeds (same 50 kyr/frame, 600 Myr) and read `depTx`, `depMelt`, the host masses and
   `heldMassTime` as distributions rather than as one world each. If the candidate's deposit→melt
   flux is a seventh of the control's on every seed, the thinning is the bound's and the row stays
   rejected on a measured cost; if it varies with the world, the 600 Myr cell was reading one large
   event and the row's standing is open again. This is the only question that decides eligibility,
   and it is two runs per seed on machinery that already exists — four to six cells, about twenty
   minutes, and the trace is not needed for it.
2. **Cross the held-back concentration with the repair.** The counters are banked; what is missing
   is the join — for every held routing sample, whether that parcel had been relocated within the
   last N frames, and how much of `heldMassTime` sits on recently moved material. If a large share
   of the control's 68,140 km²·Myr is on parcels the repair dropped into cold neighbourhoods, then
   part of the concentration is coverage repair and not melt routing at all, and a bound on
   deliveries is the wrong instrument for it.
