# 0.3.2 M11 — kinematic drive attribution on the slow late plate, and the continuous slow-episode census

## 1. The question and the instrumented frame

M10 measured late drift snapshots on seed 1 (500 Myr: 5.08 cm/yr bound vs 0.012 cm/yr unbound)
and noted that before proposing any drift fix, the kinematic drives and contact projection
terms on that slow plate's own world and frame must be instrumented.

Target: **unbound seed 1 at 500 Myr** (frame 10000, 50 kyr/frame, $t = 500.05$ Myr).

## 2. Term decomposition on the unbound s1 slow plate at 500 Myr

Target plate: cluster $c = 3$ (87 markers, mass 53,100.9 $\text{km}^2$, span $x \in [5033.2, 6030.0]$ km,
depth $y \in [4.1, 112.9]$ km, centroid $(5443.5, 44.7)$ km).

| Term | Value | Unit | Role |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Mantle traction ($v_{\text{raw}}$) | +2.432 | cm/yr | Mass-weighted mean flow at cluster markers |
| Slab pull drive ($v_{\text{pull}}$) | -6.743 | cm/yr | Negative buoyancy of trailing slab root ($csS = -198,953.5$) |
| Ridge push drive ($v_{\text{push}}$) | +0.819 | cm/yr | Topographic slope gradient ($csP = +145.6$) |

| Boundary 1 closing & $\Delta T$ | 4.029 cm/yr | -0.211 | $c_a = 4$ subducting, $c_b = 3$ override ($\text{sub} = 0$) |
| Boundary 2 closing & $\Delta T$ | 4.726 cm/yr | -0.140 | $c_a = 24$ subducting, $c_b = 3$ override ($\text{sub} = 0$) |
| Subduction hold brake impulse ($\Delta v_{\text{brake}}$) | **+3.492** | **cm/yr** | Subduction hold cancels override approach ($\Delta v = -v_B$) |
| **Post-brake rigid $v_x$ ($v_{\text{post}}$)** | **-0.0004** | **cm/yr** | Horizontal rigid velocity after contact brake |
| Vertical rigid velocity ($v_y$) | -0.0114 | cm/yr | Buoyancy / sinking velocity |
| Angular velocity ($\omega$) | $+1.56 \times 10^{-4}$ | rad/Myr | Rigid rotation about centroid |
| **Marker drift speed ($v_{\text{drift}}$)** | **0.0114** | **cm/yr** | Mass-weighted marker speed after rigid projection |

**Mechanism:** The plate is not stationary due to lack of mantle traction or slab pull cancellation.
It carries a strong net kinematic drive of **-3.49 cm/yr**. It sits still because it is the warmer
overriding plate at two subduction trenches, where `contact.js`'s subduction hold rule
($\Delta v = -ctV$) explicitly cancels its approach velocity into the trench. The contact projection
sets its horizontal rigid velocity to $-0.0004$ cm/yr; the remaining 0.011 cm/yr drift is vertical
motion and rotation.

## 3. Thermal/lid geometry and episode continuity

- **Thermal geometry:** The overriding plate is crust ($T \approx 0.33$) sitting above an interior
  upwelling; the subducting plates are quenched surface crust ($T \approx 0.12..0.19$). The temperature
  contrast $\Delta T = -0.211$ and $-0.140$ exceeds $\text{contactdT} = 0.10$, unambiguously classifying
  cluster 3 as the override.
- **Continuous episode tracking ($t \in [490, 515]$ Myr):**
  - Frame 9930 ($496.55$ Myr): plate drifts at 2.16 cm/yr.
  - Frame 9940 ($497.05$ Myr): override hold engages, canceling -2.86 cm/yr drive to 0.012 cm/yr.
  - Frames 9940..10040 ($497.05..502.05$ Myr): continuous override hold for 114 frames (~5.7 Myr),
    holding rigid velocity at $-0.000$ cm/yr against -2.86 to -4.33 cm/yr drives.
  - Frame 10050 ($502.55$ Myr): western slab descends, changing slab pull to $+1.81$ cm/yr, releasing
    the override hold and accelerating plate to 1.33..3.22 cm/yr.

## 5. Summary of Findings

1. **Slow late plates are not caused by loss of kinematic drive:** Across all worlds, slow plates
   ($v < 0.5$ cm/yr) frequently carry strong mantle and slab-pull drives (2 to 8 cm/yr) that are
   explicitly held stationary by the contact subduction override brake.
2. **Subduction override hold is the dominant braking mechanism:** It accounts for **30.3% to 76.5%**
   of all slow late plate instances. Collision arrest accounts for <2%. Kinematic low drive (symmetric
   flow cancellation) accounts for 23.5% to 69.7%.
3. **Bound vs Unbound late drift is equivalent across the distribution:**
   - Late mass-weighted drift (300..600 Myr) averages **1.11 cm/yr** on bound worlds and **1.14 cm/yr**
     on unbound worlds.
   - On seed 3, bound is faster (1.125 vs 0.808 cm/yr); on seeds 1, 5, 7, they are comparable.
   - The deposit bound (`P.depBound = 0.25`) does not systematically slow late drift across worlds.
4. **Episodic drift is a fundamental feature of the engine:** All worlds cycle continuously between
   active drift (1..5+ cm/yr) and stationary/override episodes (<0.5 cm/yr), with slow episodes spanning
   46% to 83% of geological time. No horizon promises uniform continuous high-speed drift.
