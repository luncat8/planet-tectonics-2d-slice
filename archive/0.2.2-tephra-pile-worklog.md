# 0.2.2 — tephra piles until vent death

Owner: `archive/0.2.1-melt-supply-worklog.md` §8 — "whether tephra should pile before
`writeBack`, or write-back wait for the vent's death" is the last thing between the
section and the design's two visible eruption styles. Settled: **write-back waits for
the vent's death.** Landed: K7 still writes lava; tephra waits for `venDeath` →
`finish`. `P.kMelt` 20 builds the cone and is **not** committed (R2/R3).

## 0. The two options, measured

M2's contract wrote frozen mass to the column every K7 and kept `toyH` as a drawing of
that rock (`"the retained solid shape owns no mass"`). A hooked live arc
(kPlumeMelt = 0, kMelt ×10000, 800 frames) lost **144 cells² to `erode` and 43 to
`bury`**, 0 to reset/rehome, and never held more than 7 cells² in the box. Peak height
1.2 px. K6 was shaving a surface tephra bed the tectonic clock had just been handed.

`kEro` is small on a flat 1 km plateau (~0.03 m / 50 kyr), but arc columns are steep:
`1 + 2·slope/slopeRef` with `slopeRef = 0.01` is a 10–20× multiplier, and every frame's
write-back puts a fresh volcanic top on the stack. The live pile is then one frame of
tephra, which is an apron, not a cone.

Waiting for death means the box owns the mass while the vent is alive (`venLava` /
`venTephra` still sit in `S.mass()` as mafic melt; `toyFz` is not counted twice). K6
`erode` is a no-op at `venEdV = 0`. `bury` skips an unwritten pile. `finish` already
froze, landed, and wrote; a dead vent still leaks no state.

Piling one frame then writing is the option the 0.2.1 table already falsified: it is
what K7 was doing.

## 1. What the live arc does with that rule

Same isolation (kPlumeMelt = 0, seed 1, 50 kyr/frame, 3000 frames = 150 Myr):

| kMelt | melt/water | max pile cells² | H px | W px | deaths |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0.002 (was) | 6e-5 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| 0.2 | 6e-3 | 0.24 | 0.09 | 17 | 25 |
| 2 | 6e-2 | 4.3 | 1.6 | 17 | 96 |
| **20** | **0.63** | **124** | **9.1** | **27** | **43** |

20 is the petrological decade (0.2.1 §3's ×10000 row) and the smallest value whose
live arc cone matches erupt-bench's explosive reference (10.02 × 27 at 150 cells²) on
width and is 1 px short on height — supply, not the write-back rule. 2 stays an apron.

`experiments/tephra-pile.js` gates that row **without committing it**: it sets `kMelt = 20`
and `kPlumeMelt = 0` for the run. 27.00 × 9.12 px, 124.5 cells², tephra beds 1.53e7 m2
after death, no live write-back, ledger 1.57e-14.

## 2. Why 20 is not committed

`acceptance.js` at kMelt 20 with the new write-back rule: 47 / 49. The two reds are
strict contact legs, not the toy:

- `3000/1/100` R2: 2438 / 2724 built-collision frames have a four-column run (90%
  required). 14 frames short on a new trajectory.
- `5000/5/50` R3: thickest column 81.9 km against 80 + 1.8 km of one-frame influx.
  Delayed write-back delivers the uneroded pile as one bed, so the same melt makes a
  taller spike than 0.2.1's per-K7 write (which K6 shaved as it arrived).

0.2.1's rule stands: a melt constant that moves R2/R3 is not landed from this patch.
`P.kMelt` stays 2e-3. The live default arc is still supply-starved; the plume shield is
still the one design-scale edifice. The toy now *can* hold an explosive cone, which is
what 0.2.1 §8 asked.

## 3. Contract change

| was (M2) | now |
| --- | --- |
| K7 `step` + `writeBack` both lithologies | K7 writes **lava** only; tephra waits for `finish` |
| tephra queues empty every frame | tephra queues hold until `venDeath` → `finish` |
| probe beds during a live episode | probe beds after death; the live cone is the box |
| K6 shaves the drawing to match the stack | K6 shaves only a *written* (`venEdV > 0`) edifice; `bury` skips `venEdV = 0` |

Prescribed fixtures that call `writeBack` themselves (M2.1, M2.2, M2.8, `active16`)
are unchanged. `edifice-bench` M2.3 now asserts the live hold, then `MAG.venDeath`,
then the beds. M2.3b is the K6-vs-live-pile fixture.
