## M2.2 — K3 transport, K4 contacts and cadence events (2026-09-26)

**Shipped.** `COL.transport` moves each column by its solved plate velocity, wraps it,
then stable-sorts `(x, old index)` and gathers *every* per-column field and the full
96-slot stack through preallocated typed buffers. It carries deposits/vents through the
inverse permutation; a changed neighbour invalidates edge hysteresis. The pre-move width
travels with the record. K4 scans sorted gaps/contacts into intents, consumes before
births, and applies transfers in **volume units** before recalculating widths and stack
thicknesses. Pure translation also rescales stacks by old/new width, so plate-boundary
motion cannot silently change mass. The final topology is sorted; plate counts/anchors,
caches, slope/profile, reciprocal vents and boundary histories are rebuilt. A consumed
edge owner hands its history to the new left neighbour when the ordered plate pair survives.

Opening > `2*rGap*w0` gives one midpoint packet on the left plate (including the seam):
continental donor sets with mean felsic thickness ≥ `hRiftBreakup` each contribute
1/(K+1) of felsic/sediment layer volumes and all ore potentials; otherwise the mantle
sources a new mafic stack. The newborn starts at age 0/damage 0.6. A closing O–C/O–O
contact < `rContact*w0` sinks the subducting stack except half its sediment, which goes
to an overriding-margin prism; C–C transfers the whole thinner-felsic stack. Deposits
follow retained horizons (or lose their owner for a sunk bed), and vents follow their
surviving margin if it is free. At full capacity, skips are atomic; consumed slots can
be recycled by same-frame births. The 1-Myr event hook after K4 can suture a C–C
contact after 20 consecutive Myr of slow convergence (`edgeSlow`, separate from the
boundary's total `edgeAge`) or split a damaged corridor with ≥24 cells per daughter. K3/K4 and
events run at zero geological time without changing tectonic state.

**Pitfalls fixed during validation.** A newborn needs `colW=1` while its temporary
layer thicknesses represent volumes, otherwise cross-lith compaction records zero in the
mix ledger. Clearing only the old `dead[0..n)` range left a stale flag in the first
appended slot after a prior consume, so a later second birth was produced but silently
excluded from the gather; clear the whole fixed buffer. The event hook is invoked as an
unbound function by `SIM.step`, so it must call `COL.plates()` rather than `this.plates()`.
The old M1 raster fixture's unowned 50 m bed is allowed to subduct now; it disables
K3/K4 during its 100k-frame *storage* check, while M2 checks real moving beds and
mass independently.

**Validation** (outputs in `experiments/logs/0.1.0-*.txt`):

## M2.2 review — column widths, birth inheritance, hard contact (2026-09-26)

**Shipped.** Reviewing the M2.2 commit against the design found three mechanics that are
right for the reference's fixed-area grid and wrong for variable-width columns:

- **Column width is the territory the column owns**: midpoint of the left gap to midpoint of
  the right gap (`S.widths`), not `x[i+1] - x[i]`. The left-anchored width stretched only the
  *right* neighbour of an opening gap and gave a newborn's whole territory to its left parent.
  With trapezoid widths `SUM(colW) = wrap` for any spacing, `SUM(thickness*width)` is exactly
  the integral of what the renderer draws (it paints the columns as nodes of a piecewise-linear
  profile), and an opening gap thins **both** margins by the same factor.
- **A birth inherits the material of the territory it takes** (`COL.inherit`) instead of a
  `1/(K+1)` share of 2K donors. The reference fills an *empty* fixed cell from donors; here the
  gap is already owned by the two columns beside it and has been stretched thin by the opening.
  Taking `1/(K+1)` from each of 2K donors packed 1.5 columns of crust into a half-width cell —
  a 69 km spike with +6 km of relief at every rift axis, where the design asks for a rift
  valley. Now both margins and the newborn land at the same thickness: the uniform rift fixture
  stretches 35 km to 27.87 km on both margins *and* the axis, and the axis sits 788 m **below**
  sea level (inland +400 m) — a valley that floods, as a rift should. Ore potentials are
  concentrations, so a birth averages its two parents and stays in 0..1 (the donor share summed
  six columns of potential into one, up to 3.0).
- **Hard contact resolves below the hysteresis gate.** `epsHi`/`epsLo` own the boundary *state*,
  but a pair that already overlaps (`gap < rContact*w0`) and is still closing (`relN < 0`) must
  be classified by polarity: `PLT.classify` promotes it to subduct and `COL.intents` consumes on
  `relN < 0` rather than `relN < -epsHi`. Otherwise a slow overlap interpenetrates forever —
  measured 0.25 km of width carrying 2.1 km of crust, since the volume-conserving stack has to
  go somewhere. `COL.lidFan` bands became the same asymmetric cells (`a = x - gapLeft/2`,
  `b = a + colW`): centring a band of width `colW` on `x` makes neighbours overlap, and an
  overlap of one part in 1e13 already breaks the convex blend (`Tf` undershot `-lithCold`).

**Validation.** `m2-check.js` grew the rift fixtures (newborn = mean of its margins, mantle
source = `hMafNew * colW[birth]` to 0.00e+0 relative, ore in range, slow overlap consumed) and
stayed green; all six harnesses green.

## M2.3 — dynamic surface response (2026-09-26)

**Shipped.** New `js/crust.js` (13th script, after `plates.js`) holds **K5**; `SURF.k6` holds
the single profile recompute. `SIM.k[5] = CRU.k5`, `SIM.k[6] = SURF.k6`, and the two
`SURF.profile()` calls inside `COL.k4` are gone, so the profile is built once per frame from
the final state instead of twice from a half-finished one. K5 is allocation-free: both stencils
gather into the preallocated `CRU.face` and apply afterwards, so no column reads a value this
same pass wrote.

- **Age and damage.** `colAge += dt`; `damage += dt*(kDam*ext+/extRef/strength - kHeal*damage)`
  clamped to [0,1]. `strength = clamp(strBase + strFelK*ss(hFel,10,35 km) +
  strAgeK*ss(age,20,200 Myr), strBase, strMax)/Tm` — thick old crust on a cold planet resists
  damage, which is what makes rift corridors localize late. Only `ext > 0` damages;
  `kDamT*|relT|` stays deferred (no transverse velocity in 1D).
- **zDyn.** One exponential pull toward the source, `z += (target - z)*(1 - exp(-dt/tauDyn))`,
  `target = -zTrench` at `trenchDist === 1` and 0 elsewhere. The reference's `zDyn -= zTrench`
  per frame would make trench depth a function of the frame step. Plume swell is M4.
- **Flexure.** Periodic face flux `F_i = kFlex*dt*w0^2*(z[i+1]-z[i])/gap`, applied as
  `z[i] += (F_i - F[i-1])/colW[i]`. At uniform spacing (`gap = w0`) that is exactly the
  reference's `kFlex*dt*SUM(z_j - z_i)`, so `kFlex` keeps its 1/Myr meaning; the fluxes
  telescope, so `SUM(colW*zDyn)` moves only through source and relaxation. `gap` is floored at
  `faceGapMin*w0` — a squeezed contact would otherwise ask for a step 200x smaller than the
  frame provides (a naive `1/gap^2` coefficient went unstable as columns closed).
- **Collapse.** The same face-flux form on `hFel`, gated on `max(hFel_i, hFel_j) > hCollapse`,
  moving **real beds** through the new `COL.collapseMove(from, to, volume)` and `COL.removeAt`:
  the volume is peeled off the donor's topmost felsic beds (beds above ride down, deposits
  follow their horizon) and thickens the receiver's top felsic bed, or pushes one. Volume units
  are the point — the two columns have different widths, so one volume is two thicknesses.

**Scope decision.** Design §4.4 puts collapse in K5; the plan had it in M4.3. It ships in M2.3
and M4.3 is now "arc enrichment only", because a section with no thickness sink is geologically
unusable: shortening conserves volume while it removes area, and in 1D *all* of a boundary's
shortening lands on one column (there is no out-of-plane spread). Measured over 1 Gyr at
100 kyr/frame, seed 5: the peak column reaches **946 km of crust and 153 km of relief with no
K5 at all, 142 km / 17 km with collapse on**. At 500 Myr the planet is 597 columns, 9 plates,
median relief -4.6 km (a normal ocean floor), 12 columns above 100 km of crust, top relief
22 km. Collapse is a real cap but a slow one and stays the only one until M3: at 17 km of
relief the knee law removes ~3 km/Myr, at 100 km ~620 km/Myr. Do not "fix" the residual
exaggeration by capping thickness — that is erosion's job.

**Split-corridor rule fixed (exposed by the new damage source).** With damage accumulating, the
M2.2 split ("first cell with damage >= splitDamage, both sides >= minPlateCells") fragmented the
planet to `plateCap`: measured 32 plates from 8, 449 of 583 columns above the threshold, 1000 km
orogens, 437 km peak crust at 500 Myr. In 1D every single cell is a cut, so the reference's rule
("removing the corridor leaves >= 2 bodies of >= minPlateCells") has to be read as the
contiguous **intact** runs — which is self-limiting exactly as in 2D, where a wide damaged zone
disconnects nothing. `COL.splitScan` now finds the first corridor whose intact bodies are both
>= `minPlateCells`, splits at the corridor midpoint (nearest-component assignment) and drops the
*whole* corridor to 0.5 so the rift stays a weak line that cannot re-split; a plate owning the
whole wrap is a closed ring and never splits. Same run afterwards: **9 plates, 167 km peak
crust, 22 km relief**.

**Also shipped (M2.2 carry-forward): bilinear `Tf` sampling in the body raster.** Design §7 step
4 always asked for a bilinear fan sample; M1 shipped nearest-node. The fan tightens with depth,
so the mantle rows on screen sit in rings of 8-16 nodes: one node is 1067-2135 screen pixels
wide and the real field carries node-to-node jumps of ~3 of the 8 colour bins — thousand-pixel
stripes. Sampling is cell-centred (`fx = wx*invP + cnt - 0.5`, periodic). Because a ring owns a
*contiguous run* of screen rows and its colour at a given x is constant across the run, the pass
now samples and shades each ring once per screen column and stamps the run, which pays for the
interpolation several times over: default window **3.90 -> 2.94 ms**, overview **4.18 -> 2.97 ms**
(min of 15x40; interleaved same-process A/B: 2.96 bilinear+cache vs 4.17 committed). Negative
control with nearest-node sampling: the tent fixture reports 3-bin steps and 0 lit pixels past
half a pitch, against 1 bin and 267 px with bilinear.

**Pitfalls fixed during validation.**
- Face-flux **direction**: `F_i > 0` is inflow into column *i* across its right face, so
  collapse moves material from the thicker `i+1` to the thinner `i`. Reversed, it feeds
  thick -> thick and runs away exponentially (measured 2.87e6 km of crust in 1200 frames).
- Face-flux **units**: the flux is a volume (m^2 of section) but `COL.push` takes a thickness;
  pushing m^2 as m added >1500 km per frame. `collapseMove` converts at both ends
  (`peel = volume/colW[from]`, `grow = peel*colW[from]/colW[to]`).
- Kernels and event hooks are invoked as bare function references, so `this` is undefined in
  strict mode: `CRU.k5`/`SURF.k6`/`COL.events` must name their module (`CRU.`, `COL.splitScan`).
- An exact exponential relaxation combined with an explicit diffusion leaves the steady state
  mildly frame-rate dependent (the effective rate is `(1-e^-dt/tau)/dt = 1/tau - dt/2tau^2`): a
  running trench settles 0.49% deeper at 50 kyr/frame than at 200 kyr/frame. Inside the plan's
  +-10% rule for prescribed frame rates, so it is asserted as such rather than hidden.
- The M2.0 event-order fixture restored the K5 slot with a hard-coded `SIM.add(5, null)` — its
  value when the fixture was written. That silently disabled the column update for every fixture
  below it, long runs included (the 500 Myr line read 946 km and 8 plates: exactly the no-K5
  numbers). Restore slots by saved value.
