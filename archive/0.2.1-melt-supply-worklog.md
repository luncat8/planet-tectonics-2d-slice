# 0.2.1 — melt supply: the calibration 0.2.0 M5 measured and deferred

Owner: `archive/0.2.0-m5-worklog.md` §9's first open item — "a design-scale cone needs ~50×
the section's melt production; that is a `kMelt`/`kPlumeMelt` decision with a crust-budget
consequence, and it belongs to the next engine plan". Taken here. Landed:
**`P.kPlumeMelt` 2e4 → 2e6**, `P.kMelt` unchanged, on a measurement that also corrects the
"~50×" estimate and the "crust-budget" fear behind it.

## 0. The scale the melt has to reach

Everything is bounded by one derived number: a toy cell is one default-window pixel,
`P.toyCellM2 = toyCellX × toyCellY ≈ 5.80e5 m2`, which is 5.80 km3 at the committed
`venKm3M2` (1e5 m2 per km3). The box is 48 × 32 cells. So the design's 10–30 px edifice
(0.1.0-design.md §1.5) is 50–450 cells2 of pile, i.e. **3e7–2.6e8 m2 delivered to one
column** — and a column's chamber holds `VchM2 = 5e5 m2`, under one cell. Supply, not the
chamber, decides the edifice: the vent drains up to 4.3e5 m2 per 30 min frame, so a fed
column empties as fast as it fills and the pile grows at the supply rate.

At the committed constants the whole section made 2.87e6 m2 of plume melt and 3.4e4 m2 of
arc melt in 150 Myr (0.2 km3/Myr), and the best-fed chamber reached exactly `VbirthM2`.
That is the gap M5 measured from the other side.

## 1. What the two sources actually are (measured, not assumed)

**Arc.** `js/magma.js` `MAG.arc` converts released ribbon water at
`kMelt × rel × (wedgeT0 + Tf − Tc)`, so its yield per m2 of water is `kMelt × temp`.
A hook on the kernel over 31 416 water-releasing ribbon nodes (seed 1, 50 kyr/frame)
measured that factor directly: min 0.153, median 0.441, max 0.500, water-weighted mean
**0.381** — the wedge temperature is healthy, so neither `wedgeT0` nor `Tc` is throttling
the arc. Nor is the factory range: 80.3% of the released water falls within the kernel's
300 km of an arc-qualifying column (37.0% within 50 km), and the in-range yield 8.58e-4
matches the formula's `kMelt × temp` = 7.62e-4. The arc's ceiling is its **water supply**:
4.94e7 m2 released per 150 Myr.

**Plume.** `MAG.plume` adds `kPlumeMelt × plmStr × (0.8 + 0.4 plmStr) × dt` to the plume's
own column, so `kPlumeMelt` is a straight m2/Myr rate and 2e4 was 0.2 km3/Myr at a
normalised head — 20 km3/Myr after this change, still ~1e3× below a modest real head
(Hawaii is ~1e5 km3/Myr). It is not one of 0.1.0-design.md §9's constants, so it carries no
published figure to defend.

Both feed `MAG.add`, which books `ledProd[maf]` — the crust-budget side of the ledger.

| 1000 | 2.5 | 6.27e-2 | 6.8e5 / 1.4e6 | 112 / 103 | 77.2 / 63.5 | 10.8 / 15.9 | 0.6 / 0.8 | 17 / 17 | 0.020 / 0.021 |

At ×10000 — a melt/water yield of 0.63, i.e. within an order of magnitude of the
petrological water-multiplication factor — the arc's edifice stock is still only
9.7e6 / 1.1e7 m2, 30× short of the 3e7 m2 a 10 px edifice needs, its pile is still ≤1.7 px
tall, and its duty cycle has saturated at ~90%, which is exactly the vent birth/death
lifecycle 0.2.0 M1 built disappearing into a permanently erupting arc. No `kMelt` value
buys an arc cone, so none is landed.

**Why (verified in code, not inferred).** `ERUPT.land` books a landed packet into `toyH`
*and* `toyFz` and queues it in `S.venTephra[v]`; `ERUPT.writeBack` drains that queue into
the column every K7 — 0.2.0 M2's own contract: "landing queues tephra … the retained solid
shape owns no mass". Explosive tephra therefore never piles in the box; it becomes beds in
the owning column. Effusive lava is the molten fraction, which stays in the box until it
cools below `Tsol`, so a plume shield piles and an arc strato does not. The arc's 17-px-wide
apron is that apron and nothing more.

That also states a limit of the acceptance instrument: `experiments/erupt-bench.js` drives
`ERUPT.step` directly and never calls `writeBack`, so its explosive leg's H 10.02 / width 27
is the box alone. §2.1's "one explosive run builds a 10–30 px edifice" is proven for the
box, and is not what a live arc vent shows on the section. Whether tephra should pile before
write-back (or write-back wait for the vent's death) is toy physics, i.e. 0.3.0-P3's
territory, not a melt constant.

## 4. The crust-budget consequence, measured

Melt was never the crust's problem, which is why the deferral's premise ("a crust-budget
consequence") turned out to be the cheap part:

| | before (2e4) | after (2e6) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| melt's share of all mafic production | 0.001% / 0.004% | 0.155% / 0.277% |
| total mafic production, 150 Myr leg | 1.567e11 m2 | unchanged to 3 digits |
| thickest column against `crustMax` 80 km | 80.4 / 80.1 km | 80.3 / 80.3 km |
| delamination | 7.6e10 / 4.1e10 m2 | 6.3e10 / 7.4e10 m2 (different trajectories) |
| chamber spill to sills | 0 | 0 |
| K9 red frames, every frame of all 32 sweep legs | 0 | 0 |
| worst per-lithology ledger drift | 1.0e-14 / 2.0e-14 | 1.4e-14 / 1.5e-14 |

The ceiling, the delamination and the ledger all sit where they were: 100× more melt is
still under half a percent of what the section already produces as mafic rock (mostly rift
volcanics, `js/crust.js`), so nothing else in the budget had to move. The 0.2.0 M5 estimate
of "~50× the melt production" was wrong in both directions — the plume needed 100× and the
arc cannot be fixed at any multiplier.

## 5. A latent red the landing exposed: `COL.floor`'s settle test

Landing the new rate turned acceptance §2.7 red: 118 K9 red frames on the 100 kyr/frame,
500 Myr leg, every one of them the `gap` invariant, standing from frame 3237 — an ordinary
contact held at 9.156–9.260 km against its 9.382 km floor for tens of frames at a time.

**It is not the melt.** `ERUPT.place` → `COL.insertVol` writes into the column's *layer
stack*; it never touches a width or an x, so no amount of melt can move a column face. And
the same red reproduces at the *old* constant on a different seed: `kPlumeMelt` 2e4, seed 4,
5000 frames → 90 red frames, worst minGap 9.153 km, identical signature. The melt change
only re-routes which trajectory walks into it (seed 1 now red, seed 4 no longer).

The cause is `COL.floor`'s exit test, and it had two halves:

- **No tolerance.** The correction is a max-push Jacobi sweep over plates, so a ring of
  squeezed plates converges geometrically and then spins. A per-pass trace taken during the
  diagnosis (a throwaway probe, not a committed harness) read 2.696, 1.034, 1.010, 0.911,
  0.708, 0.684, 0.519 … km of residual, a ratio of ~0.87 per pass — and the loop's exit test
  was `d >= min` exactly, so it never settled. The reproducible version of that is
  `node experiments/floor-bench.js 5000 0 16`, which takes the tolerance out of the solver
  and the diagnostic together: **68.1–78.7% of frames run the whole 16-pass budget**, and
  every leg fails by design (log `0.2.1-floor-bench-tol0.txt`). `P.floorTol`'s own comment
  says it is "× gFloor a ring of contacts may leave uncorrected (see COL.floor)", but only
  `SIM.diag` read it: the diagnostic was applying a tolerance the solver never knew about.
- **A cap sized for the spin.** With the budget burned that way, a genuine squeeze got 16
  passes where it needs up to 27 (`floor-bench.js` measures the distribution), so the frame
  ended inside its own floor and stayed there until plate advection relieved the ring.

The fix is one comparison and a cap: `COL.floor` now settles at `min × (1 − P.floorTol)` —
the tolerance its own diagnostic applies — and `floorPass` goes 16 → 64. `COL.floor` also
records the passes it used (`COL.floorPasses`) so the budget is measurable, and the new
`experiments/floor-bench.js` gates it over three legs / 15 000 frames: **no settle reached
the cap, worst case 27 passes against 64**, and the worst gap the solve leaves is
9.2883 km against the 9.2883 km the diagnostic allows — solver and diagnostic now agree by
construction, where before the solver chased exact equality the diagnostic never demanded.
21–25% of frames settle in one pass and 34–47% in two, at 2.06–2.38 ms/frame including the
per-frame diag sweep, so the larger cap costs nothing and the sweep no longer spends its
budget on contact rings that are already legal.

Afterwards both 100 kyr legs are green over 5000 frames each — seed 1, which the landing had
turned red on 118 frames, and seed 4, which was red on 90 at the *old* constant — as is the
50 kyr control: 0 red frames and no exhausted settle in 15 000 frames
(`0.2.1-floor-bench.txt`).

## 6. Gates

`node experiments/acceptance.js` at the landed constants (`kPlumeMelt` 2e6, `floorPass` 64,
the `COL.floor` tolerance): **ALL PASS (48 checks)**, exit 0, no red
(`experiments/logs/0.2.1-acceptance.txt`). It runs every standing suite and the four strict
contact legs and quotes each one's own total; the four legs are also kept standalone with
their R1/R2/R3 detail (`0.2.1-contact-audit-<f>-<s>-<k>-.txt`, 13 checks each). The two
stability legs read 0 K9 red frames over 50 000 and 5 000 frames with ledger drift 1.07e-13
and 1.46e-14, determinism holds at three clock/zoom settings, and the body raster is
2.17–3.24 ms at five presets.

`experiments/floor-bench.js` (new, 11 checks) gates the solver directly: no settle exhausts
the pass budget and no contact finishes a frame inside its floor.

The cheap suites were also run individually before the acceptance pass: erupt-bench 25,
edifice-bench 73, vent-bench 38, ledger-check 19, m3-check 22, scale-check 21, smoke 75,
view-check 119, section-check 18, port-check 15, core-log 33 — all green, so no fixture
pinned the old plume rate.

## 8. Open, and next

- **The arc builds beds, not topography** (§3): a live arc vent shows a 17-px apron at any
  melt rate, and `erupt-bench`'s explosive cone is the box without write-back. Whether
  tephra should pile before it is written back is a toy-physics question for 0.3.0-P3, and
  it is the last thing standing between the section and the design's "two visible eruption
  styles".
- **Duty cycle at the landed rate is 57–69%.** A plume vent feeds on most frames, which is
  watchable but leaves little quiet interval for the birth/death cycle to read against. If
  a viewer wants distinct episodes the knob is `Vbirth`/`tauVent`, not the melt rate.
- **`kMelt` is now a known-uncalibrated constant.** Its yield (6.05e-5 m2 of melt per m2 of
  water) is four orders of magnitude below the petrological factor, and §3 shows that
  fixing it would change the arc's duty cycle without changing its look — so it belongs
  with the tephra question, not before it.
- **`COL.floor` is a Jacobi sweep with a pass budget.** It now settles on the diagnostic's
  tolerance, but a ring of squeezed plates still converges at ~0.87 per pass; if a future
  change adds plate-boundary states or a longer ring, `experiments/floor-bench.js`'s pass
  histogram is the thing to re-measure rather than the cap to raise.
- 0.2.x stays closed; this is a patch on the column engine's constants plus one latent
  solver fix, not a new milestone of it. The roadmap's next engine work is still 0.3.x
  (particle engine P3+) or 0.4.0's exchange.
