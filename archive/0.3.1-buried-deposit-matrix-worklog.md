# 0.3.1 M0 — the buried-deposit remelt matrix

The first M0 worklog (`0.3.1-buried-deposit-remelt-worklog.md`) measured seed 1 and closed on a
question: one zero is one sample, and the 30 km rule might be a shallow quench mask rather than a
deep bound. This worklog carries the matrix that answers it: eight seeds over 300 Myr at the
shipped 50 kyr/frame clock, plus a clock group that probes the same seeds at 500 and 10 kyr/frame
and one 600 Myr leg. Everything below is report-only measurement; `params.js` is untouched and
the shipped simulation only changed in that G5 is now sampled, never in what it does.

## 1. What was measured and how

`experiments/pt-buried-deposit-melt.js` (v2) runs the ordinary P4 stack and, on every G5 call,
samples the buried deposits it finds: parcels in phase `dep` below the shallow cooling mask, each
weighted by `S.subDt` so sub-stepping clocks integrate mass-time at wall time. Per sample it
records depth, melt fraction xi against the `meltHold` threshold, the local margin, the local
field temperature, and the host mass it sits on. A parcel is **eligible** when xi >= `meltHold`
(= 0.1) while buried deeper than `depositCoolDepth` (= 30 km): exactly the material M1's route
would have to move.

Single leg:

    node experiments/pt-buried-deposit-melt.js [liveMyr] [seed]

Cell matrix, one line per cell, optional per-cell duration, `--norepeat` to drop the repeat leg:

    node experiments/pt-buried-deposit-melt.js 300 --cells 1:50,2:50,...,8:50
    node experiments/pt-buried-deposit-melt.js 300 --cells 6:500,6:10,5:50:600,3:10

Logs: `experiments/logs/0.3.1-buried-deposit-seeds.txt` (16 checks, ALL PASS, 892.8 s) and
`experiments/logs/0.3.1-buried-deposit-clocks.txt` (12 checks, ALL PASS, 1358.3 s). Both repeat
their first cell and require it bitwise, so a cell is not carrying state from its neighbour.

One reading was added to the harness for this matrix and is now required by `0.3.1-plan.md` §2:
`T ... vs field ...`, the node temperature of the field cell the sampled parcel is embedded in
(`grid.sampleAt`). M0's own count cannot be read without it, for the reason in §4.

Second, exposure grows with duration much faster than the buried stock does: the 600 Myr leg

its closest sample is a fully molten parcel at 74 km depth in a host whose field temperature is
0.288.

## 4. What the field column adds

Read the two temperature columns together and the eligible samples are not what a "remelt" story
would predict. Their own T exceeds the local field T at every closest approach, and the largest
gaps are at the largest exposures: seed 5's 600 Myr sample sits at T 2.267 in a 0.288 field; the
seed-6 and seed-8 samples sit at ~0.99 in fields of 0.72. A parcel genuinely warmed by the matter
it was buried in would sit at its host's temperature, not 2 to 7 times above it.

`experiments/scratch/hpeaks-1-300.txt` (git-ignored probe) shows the same separation without the
deposit wrapper: on seed 1 over 300 Myr the world's maximum parcel enthalpy is frozen at 1.3850
through 250 Myr in a light, fully molten parcel while the node field's own maximum temperature
never exceeds 1.0047, then climbs to 1.6381. The mechanism is in the deposit operator itself:
`grid.scatterT` hands each arriving parcel a share `cw = mu_k / W_k` of the node's temperature
measure and `gatherDT` reads it back the same way, so the marker-to-grid pair is the adjoint of
the deposit. Heat therefore arrives per unit of node measure, and a light parcel landing in a
heavy node takes a large specific increment. That is exactly what makes the intake equal to the
operator's own wall flux (`sum_p m_p a_pk = mu_k`, gated by `pt-p4-check.js`), so it is not a
weighting bug to be renamed away — but it does mean a hot, un-quenched parcel can park in the
deposit population and sit there.

Both readings point the same way for M1: the deposits M0 counts as eligible are, at least in
part, hot material buried before the 30 km mask could reach it, not sediment warmed by its host.
The `depQuenched` latch M1 specifies — a positive-time shallow cooling pass that equilibrated
below `meltHold`, surviving later depth warming — is therefore not a formality around the route;
it is the filter that separates the two populations. The number to quote after M1 lands is the
**certified** exposure: eligible *and* latched. A zero there is a real zero, and it would defer
the route as cleanly as a raw zero would.

## 5. Verdict

Branch 1 of `0.3.1-plan.md` §5, re-confirmed under the fixed harness: eligible exposure exists,
and it exists on ordinary clocks and durations. M1 is indicated, not deferred. The measurement
also leaves the 0.3.0 gates untouched — every cell above closes mass and enthalpy books at 0
refusals, so the route can land beside them without reopening them.

its local node field, which is the reading the M1 worklog hands to the next measurement.
