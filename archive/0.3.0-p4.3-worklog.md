# 0.3.0 P4.3 worklog — weathering, erosion and sedimentation

Law chosen by measurement: `experiments/pt-p4-transport-sweep.js` (**37 checks**), gate for the
shipped pass: `experiments/pt-p4-erosion.js` (**29 checks**). Logs are
`experiments/logs/0.3.0-p4.3-*.txt`; the paste-back summary is `archive/0.3.0-p4.3-report.md`.

## What shipped for P4.3

One new file, `js/pt/erode.js` (`PTERODE`, one global on an IIFE, node require exports
`run`/`route`/`law`/`move`), at G7's tail behind `F.surface`.

`route` rebuilds the terrain the law reads and the population it erodes, from scratch, in four
walks of `nx` and two of the pool: the face slope `erS[i] = (zh[i+1] - zh[i]) / dx` (the wrap face
at the seam); the receiver `erR[i]`, the steeper of the two lower neighbours with the right one
winning an exact tie, so a whole-cell shift of the initial condition shifts the drainage with it;
the drainage area `erA` by peeling the columns with no inflow in topological order (each column
starts at its own `dx`); the deposition site `erSite[i]` -- the first reach on the path whose own
gradient has fallen under `eroSlopeDep`, or the sink the path ends in; and the donor buckets
(`erTop`/`erList`, a counting sort) of solid parcels inside the `eroBand`-deep weathering band,
with each site's own deposit parcel in `erDep` (the lowest slot wins, so the seat is a function of
the pool's order and not of the walk's luck).

`law` is one walk of the demand table: `E = kStream * sqrt(A) * |slope|` km/Myr, capped at
`eroMax`, a frame's demand `E * dt * dx` km2, and **zero for a sink** -- a lake floor is where the
drainage ends, so it is a site and never a source.

`move` is one walk of that table. Each column's demand is served from its own shallowest in-band
solid donor (one take per donor per frame), taking
`min(demand, eroTake * m, m - eroMinM)` and crediting it into the site's deposit seat through
`POOL.carry` -- the same transaction shape as the rest of the pool: the receiving parcel keeps the
mass-weighted total enthalpy, melts and composition of the pair, or, when the site has no deposit
yet, a fresh deposit-phase parcel is admitted at `x = site * M.dx`, `y = -zh[site]` and the
donor's intensive state is copied into it. The receiving parcel is then re-read through
`PHASE.byH` at its own depth and composition and the sensible change is booked in `S.ledger`, so
the P3.1 latent identity stays true across the pass. Unmet demand is accumulated in `S.eroShort`
and named on the frame in `d.eroWant`; a full pool defers. The pass never writes `S.zh`.

`js/pt/params.js` gained the P4.3 block (`ero: true`, `eroBand: 120`, `eroTake: 0.25`,
`eroMinM: 50`, `kStream: 0.3`, `eroMax: 0.2`, `eroSlopeDep: 1e-4`). `js/pt/state.js` gained the
transaction and throughput counters (`tx.ero`, `thr.eroDr`/`eroCr`/`eroDep`), the per-frame
diagnostics (`d.ero`/`eroM`/`eroWant`/`eroNew`), the cumulative `S.eroShort`, and the workspace the
pass runs in (`erS`/`erR`/`erA`/`erD`/`erSite`/`erDep`/`erIn`/`erStack`, `erTop` of `nx + 1`,
`erList` of the pool cap -- all allocated at init, nothing in the frame). `js/pt/pool.js` gained
`carry` (the erode transfer, described above) and `'erList'` in `WORK`. `js/pt/sim.js` runs
`ERODE.run` at G7's tail; `index.html` and `particles.html` each load `js/pt/erode.js` after
`flight.js` (still byte-identical); `js/pt/ui.js` prints the HUD's `erosion` line.

## The law, decided by measurement

The sweep drives both candidates through the shipped pass's own `route`/`move` (only the demand
table differs) on one controlled relief fixture built in `experiments/pt-p4-lib.js`
(`relief()`: two divides, two basins at 150..156 and 230..240, every flank monotone, so a column's
steepest descent is its only descent), and fixed its decision rule before reading a number:

- **D1 conservation** -- both candidates must close mass, enthalpy, felsic, water and melt across
  a hand-called frame, hold the P3.1 latent identity and move the world's fingerprint;
- **D2 downhill** -- no law may land material outside its own receiver chain or on a column
  higher than its source. Both candidates pass, so D2 is a contract and not a discriminator;
- **D3 basin capture** -- at *equal work* (both laws throttled to the same mass per frame), at
  least three quarters of the moved mass must settle inside the fixture's basins;
- **D4 transport reach** -- at equal work, at least half must settle more than one column away;
- **D5 budget and effect** -- the shipped pass must stay under 1 ms/frame of its own work on the
  live planet and meet no refusal, and the effect must be a real redistribution and not a
  circulation.

| reading (equal work, 400 frames a leg) | runoff / stream power | conservative hillslope |
|---|---|---|
| mass conservation across one frame | closes (`dM`, `dH`, `dF`, `dW` all 0.00e+0; latent shift 6.9e-17) | closes (shift 6.0e-19) |
| D2 violations | 0 up, 0 off-chain | 0 up, 0 off-chain |
| **D3** lands in the basins | **100.0%** | **1.5%** |
| **D4** lands > 1 column away | **96.2%** | **0.0%** |
| in place | 1.2% | 0.0% |
| mean hop | 40.86 columns | 1.00 columns |
| net redistribution / moved (rate A) | 3029.69 / 3066.00 = **98.8%** | 255.00 / 3066.00 = **8.3%** |
| sediment parcels at the end | 18 (both basins) | 252 (one per donor) |
| shipped-pass cost on the fixture | 0.355 ms/frame | 0.394 ms/frame |

Both candidates were run twice, at each other's yield (rate A = the hillslope law's own fixture
yield, rate B = the shipped coefficient), and every reading is the same at both: the verdict is a
property of the law, not of the level it was read at.

Live legs, 8 Myr each at each law's own coefficient with the background frozen
(`experiments/logs/0.3.0-p4.3-sweep.txt`): runoff moved 322.2 km² and concentrated 318.1 km² of it
(98.7%); hillslope moved 1056.0 km² (327.8% of the runoff's throughput) and concentrated only
207.8 km² (19.7%), because every hop is one column and neighbouring columns exchange material in
both directions -- a diffusive law's *net* effect is its divergence, not its flux. That is the
decision: **runoff/stream power ships**, `E = kStream * sqrt(A) * |slope|` capped at `eroMax`, each
column settling at the end of its own flow path.

## Why the pass reads the way it does

- **A sink gets no demand.** A local minimum is where the drainage ends; if it eroded, the basin
  floor would be a source of the material it just received and the "sediment ends in the basins"
  claim would be about a churn. It also keeps a narrow pit (whose own outgoing face can be steep)
  from eroding itself.
- **One take per donor per frame.** The take is capped at `eroTake` of the parcel and floored at
  `eroMinM`, so a frame bites at most a quarter of the surface parcel of a column and never
  reduces one below 50 km². Measured on the fixture: 254 donors, worst take 0.1% of a parcel per
  frame -- the band thins from the top instead of being drained.
- **The seat is the site's own node.** `place()` uses `site * M.dx`, not the column centre: the
  column map (`Math.round(x / dx)`) would round a centre-seated parcel into the *next* column and
  the site would lose the material it just admitted. The seat sits on `y = -zh[site]`, the same
  datum `flight.js` lands ejecta on, so P3.4's merge search (`mergeTarget`, 40 km, composition-
  gated) finds an erosion pile and merges into it rather than stacking a second one (gated in §C).
- **What leaves a donor is mass, so the sensible booking must include it.** A donor that loses `d`
  km² at temperature `T0` changes its sensible total `m*T` by `-d*T0` even though `T` is
  intensive and unchanged; the receiver's total moves by `m'*T' - m*T`. Two checks catch a
  booking that ignores the mass term: the P3.1 latent identity on the fixture (shift 6.9e-17) and
  the live leg, where the pass books **-1.05e+5 km²·H over 400 frames (mean -1.08 per transfer)
  against an identity residual of 6.9e-9** -- 13 orders of magnitude of leverage on the ledger's
  own sign and magnitude, which is what makes "the ledger moved" a measurement rather than a
  restatement.
- **The deposit phase has two sources now, and its identity says so.** `thr.eroCr` is credited
  into deposit parcels by construction (every site seat is a deposit) and `thr.eroDep` counts what
  the pass took back *out* of a deposit donor, so
  `invM[dep] = thr.landCr + thr.eroCr - thr.eroDep` exactly. That is the P3.5 deposit identity
  with P4.3 shipped on; without the new counter the early P3.5 run read 4304.5 km² of deposit
  against 2477.8 km² of landings and could only be read as a failure.

## Traps the harness work found (all measurement, no physics)

1. **`scaled(a, b)` is a relative difference between two readings of one quantity; it is not a
   drift against a stock.** Gating `|book error| / |stock|` yields ≈1 by construction and fails
   for a pass that is perfectly closed: the enthalpy and latent books are *residuals of sums of
   order the intake*, so they must be barred against `|hLedger|` and printed that way.
2. **A per-column mass sum cannot resolve the transfer it is asked about.** A fixture column holds
   ~1.6e5 km² of rock, so a naive running sum resolves only ~1e-8 km² while the transfer is
   ~1e-2 km². Every per-column reading in the gate is Neumaier-compensated (`colMass`), which is
   what makes "the loss equals the gain" a 1e-9-relative check and not a statement about rounding.
3. **`S.invN`/`S.invM` are only fresh after `POOL.inventoryComp(S)`.** Two of the gate's own
   checks and three of the sweep's read stale censuses until the inventory is recomputed; the
   sediment-stock claims must read it on both sides of the leg.
4. **A slot index is dead the moment a merge closes it.** `pool.remove` zeroes the vacated slot by
   design ("nothing lives past `S.n`"), so a landing check must read the seat's id *before*
   `FLIGHT.land`, or it compares against 0.
5. **The pass's cost is its own arithmetic, not the on/off frame difference.** Turning the pass on
   also admits sediment parcels the crust pass then carries: 0.42 ms/frame of the pass's own work
   against 9.6-17% on the frame it lives in (the percentage depends on how long the leg has been
   accumulating sediment). The gate bars the direct reading (2.6-2.8% of the frame) and reports
   the marginal one, because only the direct one is a stopwatch.
6. **The pass moves material, never `zh`.** `erode.js` does not write the profile (bitwise gated
   over 2000 frames), so a fixture leg measures *material* redistribution and not profile
   evolution: any reading that expects the terrain to change is measuring the wrong thing.
