# 0.4.1 — Worklog: M1 the reader, M2 the reconstruction

Session record for the two milestones that turn a cut into a page: `js/section-pack.js` (the
reader and the section page, shipped in `e5e9994`) and `js/section-seed.js` + the section view
(M2, this session). The plan is `0.4.1-plan.md`; `archive/0.4.1-worklog.md` is the review of the
previous commit and the M0 format work; `archive/0.4.1-section-report.md` is the short version of
this file.

## 1. Review of `e5e9994`

The commit planned 0.4.1 and shipped M0 (the format) and M1 (the reader) in one step. Re-ran all
nine harnesses it claims — slice-cut 63, section-pack 39, smoke 65, view-check 119, pt-ui 37,
pt-check 18, pt-load-check 9, port-check 7, section-check 18 — all pass, `cmp index.html
particles.html` clean, node v22.22.3. Read `js/section-pack.js` against `port/slice-format.js`
and `0.4.1-plan.md` §4.

Four things were worth changing before M2 was built on top of them:

- **the reader's one refusal path was fine, its `describe()` was about to be wrong**: it printed
  the source's cell span alone (`202 km cells`), which is the number §4.3.1 says must be paired
  with the section's own (`→ 512 columns · 78 km`). M2 moved both into one line.
- **the raw strip's water fill could go negative** (`ys[i] − seaY` when a `wet` sample sits above
  the solved sea level, which the format allows: `sea.levelM` is a display datum, not a clamp).
- **the strip painted a gap sample as a hole in the plate band but still drew its neighbours'
  crust across it**; M2's `alive` handling (below) is what made the difference worth stating.
- **the plan named the mapping `S.layoutSection` and put it in `js/section-pack.js`.** M2 put it
  in `js/section-seed.js` as `SEED.layout(pack, {seed, t, Tm})` instead, for a reason the plan now
  records: `columns.html` loads the section modules *before* `sim.js`, so a mapper that read
  `SIM.t` would be reading a module that has not loaded yet, and a mapper that `require`d it would
  close a cycle through `section-pack.js`. Clock values come in as arguments; the page owns the
  clock. The plan was corrected in the same commit as the code.

`port/` was left alone, including three `verify` lines duplicated inside `SectionPack.loadPack`
that a shared helper could remove: byte identity with the upstream is worth more than that cleanup,
and `experiments/port-check.js` is what keeps it honest.

## 2. M2 — the mapper (`js/section-seed.js`)

`SEED.layout(pack, {seed, t, Tm})` → `''` or a message, and nothing in between: `measure` computes
`window`/`scale`/`nCut` and refuses *before* the first state write, so a refusal needs no restore
path. Then, in order: `S.reset()`, `MNT.init(seed)`, the box filter, the boundary crossings, the
three beds per column, the plates from the runs of `plate`, the seeded edges, `zDyn`, the model
(plumes, fan time, flow, extension, trenches), the ledger.

Decisions the measurements changed, all of them now in the plan:

- **a gap sample is not a trench sliver.** §4.3's `alive` row asked for the engine's existing sliver
  path. Tried first: a hole at L5 spans ~2.6 full-width columns, and `COL.intents` retires a sliver
  by handing its territory to one neighbour — measured **50.7 km of crust moved across a column,
  7.4 km of relief, and one record retired, in a single frame at 10 kyr/f**. The box filter's zero
  is the same information at a section's resolution: the gap thins the columns it covers, mass is
  conserved, and `|z − zM|` stays an identity for *every* column instead of needing an exception.
  A sliver record is still what an open window's outside columns are (they have no data at all),
  and that is safe only because that mode's clock never runs — which is now said where it is done.
- **the quiet start is a comparison, not an absolute.** §4.3.4 asked for "no column may move more
  than a few hundred metres on the first frame". A freshly reset planet of the model's *own* default
  world moves 3 377 m on its first frame at 10 kyr/f, because `zDyn` relaxes, the fan cools and a
  plateau above `hCollapse` starts to flow: the bound condemns every world the model makes, seeded
  or not. What the seeding is responsible for is measured instead: frame 1 re-signs 0 of 12
  boundaries, the topology cannot fire for `P.evAge` (nCol unchanged for 199 frames, first births at
  frame 300), and the seeded world's first frame (2 756 m) costs no more than the control's. The
  HUD prints the residual it is claiming (`spin-up since the cut: crust … · mean z … · N frames`).
- **the bed-age convention had to be picked, not averaged.** `makePlanet` stores a relative rock
  age in `layAg`; `js/surface.js` stores the world time of deposition; `js/render.js` prints either
  as Myr. The cut's `ageMyr` is a rock age → mafic and felsic beds carry it; a sediment bed has no
  age in the pack, carries the crust's, and books `sedimentAge`. The page says "their beds aged from
  the cut; the section dates its own beds from t 120.5 Myr", which is the true sentence.
- **one seed, not two.** `S.reset()` seeds the drawing stream from `P.seed`, so `?seed=` and
  `SEED.layout`'s `opts.seed` had to be the same number or the section's noise and its plumes came
  from different worlds. `layout` now sets `P.seed` (idempotent from the page, checked from the
  harness: seed 7 vs 11 changes the hash and the noise, and not the crust mass).
- **the ledger is one closed identity.** `seeded + tail = cut`, measured to 4e-16 relative on every
  fixture pack (closed at L5, dense at L7, coarse at 64 samples, and both windows), with
  `mapEquiv` as the ratio form for the HUD. Both masses come off `SEED.cutMass`/`SEED.seedMass` so
  the check closes on the numbers the page prints rather than on a re-derivation that could share a
  bug with the mapper.
- **assumptions live outside the hash.** `S.recon` is a plain object with 18 named counters, so
  `S.hash()` (numeric fields + typed arrays) does not see them — a check writes 25 to three of them
  and asserts the hash is unchanged. `S.reset()` zeroes the record, so a re-layout counts one world.

## 3. M2 — the page

`?start=section` now lays the world on load and switches to the engine's view: `UI.init()` +
`RNDR.init()` once, `GEO.setPreset('def')`, `GEO.lookAt` at the window's centre, then the section's
own overlay on top of the engine's `present`. Three switches in the Slice panel, `aria-pressed`
read back from the state (`UI.setPressed`) so a button cannot disagree with the canvas: `raw cut`
(`r`), `overlay` (`o`), `detach and run`. The overlay is the raw half of §4.3.4 — the pack's `zM` as
a step line, one segment per sample, a fresh subpath where the wrap put a seam on screen so a span
cannot be drawn as a diagonal across the whole view, and a hatch below `z − hDraw` marking where the
cut stops and the model starts. `frame()` forces `SIM.dG = 0` for a window, because the slider, the
URL and the space bar are three other writers of the rate.

Two page-side fixes M2 needed from the engine: `UI.updateHud` split so `UI.hudText()` is a function
of its own (the section's six lines go above the engine's without owning a second HUD), and
`UI.key` ignoring a keystroke whose target is a `textarea`/`input`/`select` — the paste box is the
section page's main control, and `m` inside a pasted blob must not move the mesh. `js/surface.js`'s
`morph` got a wrap fix while M2 was being written: a ghost run that starts at column 0 read
`colX[-1]` and handed the run NaN, which is exactly what a cut with a gap at the start of the
ring produces (no engine kernel can make that state; a cut can).

## 5. Found on the way: two crust laws read a metre-valued excess as kilometres

`CRU.belt` (`js/crust.js:274`) and `CRU.delaminate` (`js/crust.js:170`) both scale their flow by
`P.k… * excess * 1000 * dt`, while `excess`/`over` are differences of `hTot`, which is metres. The
constants are documented as rates *per km* — `0.1.5-plan.md` §M2(b): "the flux is `P.kBelt`
(0.12 /Myr per km of excess)"; `kDelam` "0.45 /Myr, foundering rate per km of excess" — so the
factor should be `1e-3`, not `1e3`: the two flows are a million times what the plan says, and
`delaminate`'s own "never more than half of it in one frame" cap (`over * 500`) is what the model
runs at on any sane frame size, which is why the ceilings look like they work.

Measured on a seeded cut: one C–C seam with a 5 406 m excess moved **13.0 km of felsic crust in one
frame at 10 kyr/f** (the law as documented gives 6.5 m). This is not M2's defect — it is in K5,
in code `e5e9994` did not touch, it is why §4.3.4's absolute first-frame bound cannot be met by the
engine at all, and it plausibly shapes 0.1.6's finding that a belt "never widens: it collapses to
one pair by 50 Myr". Fixing it changes every number the 0.1.x reports quote, so it goes to its own
milestone rather than into this one (recorded in `roadmap.md` §3). M2 reports what it does through
`over-collapse` and the spin-up line, and the quiet start is judged against the engine's own norm.
