# 0.3.0 P4.3 — weathering, erosion and sedimentation

P4.3 is the first P4 leg that moves material *sideways*: everything before it moved heat and rock
through the interior, and the surface stayed P2.3's kinematic profile. One new file,
`js/pt/erode.js` (`PTERODE`), sits at G7's tail behind `F.surface` and does three things per frame
on the profile the planet already has: it reads the drainage off `zh` (steepest-descent receivers,
a topological peel for the areas, and a deposition site at the end of every flow path), charges
every column a stream-power demand `kStream * sqrt(A) * |slope|` capped at `eroMax`, and pays that
demand by transferring mass from the column's shallowest in-band solid parcel into a deposit-phase
parcel seated at the site -- P3.4's own deposit stock. The law was chosen by a comparison harness
before anything shipped, and it is a measurement, not a preference: the conservative hillslope rule
loses on basin capture, on transport reach and on effect at equal work.

Primary gate: `experiments/pt-p4-erosion.js`, **ALL PASS (29 checks)** in 196 s for 820 Myr of
simulation — the pass's demand table, donor rule and transfer arithmetic read off one state by
hand, a 2,000-frame leg on the frozen relief fixture, the merge into P3.4's landing pile, two live
300 Myr legs (pass on and pass off), the pass's own attribution on a frozen frame, and a bitwise
replay. Law choice:
`experiments/pt-p4-transport-sweep.js`, **ALL PASS (37 checks)**. Logs
`experiments/logs/0.3.0-p4.3-*.txt`; the development record is `archive/0.3.0-p4.3-worklog.md`.

- the frozen-frame attribution: 1,416,066 transfers / 12,085.217 km² over 6,000 pass-only frames,
  the losing columns above the gaining ones by 0.227 km of mean height, 0.398 ms/frame of the
  pass's own work (2.47% of the frame); two 200-frame live legs replay to the same complete-state
