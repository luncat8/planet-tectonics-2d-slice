# 0.1.6 worklog — plate forces, the collision that has to absorb them, and the ledger

Working branch `arena/01a0e6a7-planet-tectonics-2d-slice`, off `5694051`.
Plan: `0.1.6-plan.md`. Every number below came from a script in this repository.

## 1. Why the plates were slow

`scratch/probe.js`, `scratch/sweep.js`. At `Lm = 2000 km` the four mantle modes are
`round(k·wrap/(2π·Lm))` = 6, 10, 13, 16 — all shorter than a plate, and a plate's drive is
the mean of the flow under it. Retention by plate averaging: 0.19 at 2000 km, 0.35 at
4000 km, 0.62 at 6000 km. Mean |plU| 2.73 mm/yr at 50 kyr/frame against `U0` = 5 cm/yr.

Raising `U0` alone is not an option: it is also the fan-advection speed. `U0 8e4` with
`Lm 2e6`, or `U0 12e4`, drive `max|plU|` into the `vMax` clamp. Chosen: `U0 8e4` with
`Lm 4e6` (modes 3, 5, 6, 8).

Old force budget, for the record: ridge push was the largest term and the noisiest
(±13 mm/yr), slab pull 0–1.4, collision ±3–8 — one boundary column's force divided by the
whole plate width.

## 2. What was changed

1. `PLT.fP[p]` — boundary forces as plate line forces (m²/Myr), summed in `PLT.solve`
   before the division by plate width. Slab pull `F = vSlab·slabPullK·s(age)·L`, sign
   following the polarity (the old unsigned `+=` pulled half the trenches the wrong way);
   collision `m = vColl·fb·(−relN)/vRef·L_belt`, equal and opposite on the two plates.
2. `SLAB.pullLen` — `SLAB.findRibbon` allocates a ribbon when nothing matches, so it
   cannot be used for a read.
3. `COL.beltAt` — one belt measurement; flanks just outside the flow's reach.
4. `PLT.extension = d(uMantle)/dx` and `extRef 5e-1` (§M4 of the plan for the numbers).
5. `MAG.plumeColumn`, `COL.drain` — the ledger (§3 below).
6. `COL.crush` + the two sliver rules (§4 below).
7. `gFloor 0.05 → 0.12` (§5 below).

## 3. The mafic ledger, bisected to one frame

`scratch/leak{4,9,11}.js`, `scratch/k4probe.js`. After 700 Myr at mixed rates the mafic
residual was −3.174e4 m³ (rel 6.25e-8); sed, fel and lava were at float noise. The whole
error was one K4 event: frame 59 of the 200 kyr leg, t = 112.0 Myr, `ledProd[maf]` delta
2.301e+3, `ledCons[maf]` delta 5.200e+8, no mafic `insertVol` at all.

`scratch/k4probe.js` runs an instrumented text copy of `COL.k4` (built by a python script
that copies the body out of `js/columns.js` and inserts balance marks) and placed the loss
between "before gather" and "after gather". The two slots retired in that frame held
chambers of 3.386e3 and 1.0157e4 m³ — a plume head had melted into a draining record's
chamber. `MAG.arc` already skipped slivers; the plume path used the bare `MAG.nearest`.

Fixed at both ends: `MAG.plumeColumn` walks off a sliver to the nearest real column, and
`COL.drain` books whatever a sliver still holds before the slot is dropped. The second is
the invariant — after it, no path can drop a slot with mass in it.

## 4. Three topology rules the faster plates exposed

Found by `scratch/nan.js` → `scratch/width.js` → `scratch/squeeze.js`: the 200 kyr leg
reached `colW = 42 m` and then `z = Infinity` at t = 496.6 Myr.

- A column squeezed on both sides has less territory than one floor, so no position clears
  both boundaries. `COL.crush[]` absorbs it across whichever gap has a real column.
- A sliver pile-up drains from the left (the rightmost marker waits on a far gap that is
  still a full column wide, holding the whole chain).
- A sliver whose trench gap has opened is orphaned: it marks no trench, and being on its
  own plate it moves with the column beside it, so the floor can never separate them.

## 5. The resolution condition

`gFloor 0.05` puts the floor at 3.91 km. At 200 kyr/frame a plate closing at 74 mm/yr
travels 14.9 km per frame — nearly four floors — so the pair is inside the floor every
frame and the consuming edge fires every frame. Measured: 2291 deaths in 3000 audit
frames at `gFloor 0.05`, 65 at `gFloor 0.12`. That single change also brought the crust
maximum from 120.1 km to 93.8 km and the sharpest collision peak from 3.36× to 1.75×.

## 6. A false green, and how it happened

`scratch/params.bak` was taken *before* `beltFeed` was added to `js/params.js`. The
parameter sweeps ended with `cp scratch/params.bak js/params.js`, which silently removed
it. With `P.beltFeed === undefined`:

- `COL.beltAt` computed `flankH = NaN`, so the run walk broke at once and `beltW` was just
  the pair;
- `CRU.belt`'s feed loop `for (k = 1; k <= P.beltFeed; k++)` never executed — the orogenic
  flow was a complete no-op;
- `contact-audit`'s `beltScan` got `flank = NaN`, so `beltN` stayed 0 and **both R2 checks
  passed vacuously**.

`contact-audit.js 3000 1 50 --strict` printed ALL PASS (9 checks) in that state. The
lesson is in `findings-pitfalls-skills.md`: a gate that skips on a NaN input passes, so a
parameter sweep must restore from a snapshot taken *after* the last edit, and an audit
that reports "0 collisions measured" is not a pass.
