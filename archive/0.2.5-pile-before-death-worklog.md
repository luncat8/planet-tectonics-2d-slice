# 0.2.5 — pile before death: the write-back rule, landed and re-accepted

Owner: `archive/0.2.4-vent-cadence-worklog.md` §7 — "If the section's *number* … must
reach 0.2.1's 57–69% before 0.3.0-P3, the knob is the write-back rule — pile before
death — which is 0.3.0-P3's own toy physics (`0.3.0-p3-plan.md`; 0.2.1 §3 parked it
there). This table's batching region (`Vbirth` ≥ 8) is the measurement that constraint
would free." Taken here as its own patch release: the rule is 0.3.0-P3's prerequisite,
so it lands now, and the batching region it frees is measured before anyone spends it.

**Landed: lava piles before death. `P.Vbirth` stays 4 km3, `P.tauVent` stays 2 Myr.**
K7 drops the per-frame lava write-back; `ERUPT.writeBack` runs only from `finish()` at
the vent's death, so both lithologies reach the stack in one batch. At the committed row
this is a strict cone gain and nothing else moves (§1); the freed batching region
(`Vbirth` ≥ 16 under the rule) measures worse on every gate and stays unspent (§2).

## 0. Review of the first landing attempt

The rule's code was committed first and documented second, and the documentation did not
match the record:

- The first draft of the report claimed the `(16, 2)` cone "recovers the design band
  scale (watchable 8+)"; its own log reads **7.61 px against the 8 px gate — FAIL**
  (`experiments/logs/0.2.5-pile-cone-vb16-tv2.txt`), and called a 73.6% mean duty "close
  to" 0.2.1's 57–69% band. The report here is rewritten from the logs.
- The landed configuration itself — the committed row `(4, 2)` under the new rule — had
  no measurement at all: the sweep ran only `Vbirth` 16 rows, no acceptance log exists
  for the rule change, and 0.2.4's gates were earned under the old rule. §1 supplies it.
- No worklog (0.2.1–0.2.4 each have one), no roadmap entry, and the kernel still carried
  the old rule: `js/magma.js` k7 commented "lava write-back" over a disabled call left
  in place, `js/erupt.js` still documented "lava can write every K7" with a `lith`
  parameter only that dead call ever used, and `tephra-pile.js` still gated the
  tephra-only rule.

Corrections landed with the re-acceptance: stale comments rewritten, the dead `lith`
parameter removed from `writeBack` (every caller passes two args), the leftover
commented call deleted, and `tephra-pile.js`'s live-write gate extended from tephra to
both lithologies — the check is now "a live vent writes nothing into the stack" (still
6 checks, so acceptance's quoted count is unchanged). `js/params.js` and
`melt-contact-sweep.js`'s "tephra-only rule" wordings stand: they describe what 0.2.2/0.2.3
measured, which is history this release does not rewrite.

## 2. The batching region the rule frees does not buy its number

The point of pulling the rule forward was 0.2.4 §7's `(16, ·)` rows. Re-measured under
the rule (`0.2.5-pile-*` logs): **no row is landable.**

- **Contact: 9 of 12 legs pass.** The reds: `(16, 0.25)` and `(16, 2)` on `5000/5/50`
  both trip R3's ceiling by +0.50 km (a big-batch death write lands on the belt), and
  `(16, 2)` on `3000/1/100` reads R1 1.27 against 1.25. `(16, 0.5)` is 8/8 — and then
  fails the cone.
- **The cone: all three `tauVent` rows FAIL the 8 px watchable gate** — 17.00 × 4.96
  (tv 0.25), 17.00 × 4.43 (tv 0.5, which also writes no surviving tephra beds: 0.0 m2
  against the scan), 23.00 × 7.61 (tv 2). Holding lava in the box cannot help a vent
  whose box never fills: above `Vch` the column batches the episode in its chamber, and
  a short life drains one chamber's worth, not the pile 0.2.4 measured at the old rule's
  write-out cadence.
- **Duty means 73.7–78.0%** (tv 2 / 0.5 / 0.25) — outside 0.2.1's 57–69%, and below the
  champion `(16, 2)` managed under the old rule (69.1%). The rule moves mass between the
  box and the stack; it does not create quiet time.

So the cone ceiling 0.2.4 named survives its proposed fix, now measured under the rule
itself: `Vbirth` 4 stays the row where the contact legs, the cone and the cadence all
hold. The batching region remains 0.3.0-P3's measurement to spend — with conserved-melt
toy physics, not with this engine's write-back timing.
