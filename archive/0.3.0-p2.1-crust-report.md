# 0.3.0 P2.1 — the crust forms: report

Two questions were asked: make the map bigger (4-6 convection cells, vertical resolution
~2x less), and find out when the crust should form and how to draw it — at 2 Gyr the
picture still looked like fluid. Both are answered and measured; the worklog with every
number is `archive/0.3.0-p2.1-crust-worklog.md`.

## 1. The map

16000 x 2900 km on a 256x24 mesh (was 8000 km on 128x48): the same 6144 cells and the
same 62.5 km x-spacing, so the frame budget survives (11.2 ms/frame shipped config), and
**4-8 convection cells** now play across the map instead of 1-2. Vertical rows are twice
as tall (8.3 km at the surface) — still five rows through the lid, which is what the crust
needed. The default window shows the whole periodic map at 12.5 km/px; the lid preset is
the 2x detail view. `archive/p21-300Myr.png` is the shape of the thing.

## 2. When the crust forms

Measured with `experiments/pt-lid.js` on the real pipeline (`experiments/logs/0.3.0-p2.1-lid.txt`):

- **The law's clock**: strength is (1 - exp(-age/tauWeld)) times the cold-rock factor. A
  parcel becomes plate-strength (mu >= 0.25) after ~5.8 Myr of being below TLock (0.35)
  and is fully welded after ~3 tauWeld (~60 Myr at tauWeld 20).
- **From the cooling start ('hot')**: 60% of the surface carries plate material at
  **13 Myr** (the plan's gate allows 90 Myr = 30 s of wall clock), 100% by 15 Myr. The
  lid then thickens with the thermal boundary layer — the strong band IS the T=0.35..0.45
  isotherm pair, 8-27 km at first, 25-50 km later — and breaks into 3-4 plates around
  300 Myr as the load builds.
- **From the demo's 'cool' start**: the skin welds by ~10 Myr, rides as one plate to
  ~130 Myr (lid 21%), then rifts into 4 plates (Nu 20->40) and the cycle keeps going.

So the crust "should" form as soon as the rock has been cold for a few Myr — the welding
clock — and the answer to "at 2 Gyr it still looks like fluid" is that without a failure
law the cold material merely flows slowly and the palette never says "rock". Both are
fixed: cold+aged markers now cluster into rigid plates that ride the flow's mean, rift
where the flow works them hardest (rate-form damage, calibrated: `pt-load-check.js`), and
weld again when the seam cools — `experiments/pt-crust.js` gates all three in-engine
(27 checks, ALL PASS).

## 3. How it is visualized

Grey rock over the thermal palette where the surface is plate (mug 0.25->0.55, the onset
at the cluster threshold), melt glowing over the crust, and the seams showing as rifts
between the grey bodies. The crust is thin — that is the physical truth (25-50 km at
2900 km depth) and the lid preset zooms to it. Keys: `c` toggles the crust layer, `l` the
lid guide; `archive/p21hot-30Myr.png` shows the cooling start, `archive/p21-300Myr.png`
the 4-plate state.

## 4. What is deliberately not claimed

- The cluster kinematics is the P2.1 core (rigid fit + projection). Shape matching, rest
  lengths and slab-pull gating are P2.2 (plan §4.2); the pair walk's cost and the
  fit's limits are documented in the worklog.
- The engine's *settled* one-phase convection runs ~2x weaker than pt-conv's spectral
  reference at matched time (Nu ~15 vs 27.45, u_nd ~1500 vs 3562) — the P1 gate state
  (the spin-up snapshot) replays exactly; the settled delta is a printed caveat in
  `pt-eng-conv.js` and an open item, not a silent pass.
- The damage parameters are calibrated to this engine's loads at this mesh (the worklog
  has the percentile tables); a different mesh needs `pt-load-check.js` re-run, and the
  fixture says so.

## 5. Suggested next step

P2.2: the cluster kinematics upgrade (accumulated inertia instead of the second-moment
fit, pair load gated by mu not cluster size), then slab pull gated with the fluid present
(plan §8 P2's last line). Alongside, decide whether the settled-state convection gap is
worth a transport fix (the marker disorder budget) or a documented method limit.
