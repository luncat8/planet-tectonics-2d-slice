## K5 felsic loss — accounted for

The 1.448376 km reduction of felsic thickness at column 238 is explained by two sequential
K5 operations—a conservative lateral transfer followed by ledgered foundering—not an
unbooked loss:

1. `CRU.collapse`/`CRU.belt` use `COL.collapseMove` to redistribute upper felsic beds. The
   two observed transfers from column 238 were each 3,972,217.4 m². At the 42,516 m source
   width, that is 93.4287656 m per transfer, or 186.8575313 m total moved out.
2. `CRU.k5` calls `CRU.delaminate` last. After those transfers the column is about
   81,822.193469 m thick, 1,822.193469 m above `crustMax=80,000 m`. At `dt=0.05 Myr`,
   backward-Euler foundering removes
   `1,822.193469 * (45 * 0.05) / (1 + 45 * 0.05) = 1,261.518555 m`
   from the basal layer. Here that basal layer is felsic, beneath the unchanged lava/mafic
   bed.

The predicted total felsic reduction is 186.8575313 + 1,261.5185553 = 1,448.3760865 m,
which matches the measured 1,448.376 m to 0.000087 m (rounding in the captured diagnostics).
`delaminate` records the foundered volume in `ledCons` by the basal lithology before removing
it; this is expected ceiling behavior, not an unexplained disappearance. A focused M2 check
now verifies that a lava cap does not shield a basal felsic bed and that the felsic consumed-
mass ledger closes.

## R2 — evidence, not a gate change

The controlled no-plume C–C run and one strict default-planet audit show why the R2 shape
measure needs a declared physical interpretation before more tuning:

- Default `kBelt=0.12`, seed 1, 4,000 frames at 50 kyr/frame: the collision stays live for
  all frames (67 retirements); shortening matches reported convergence at 99.2%; R1/R3/R5
  pass. R2 fails at peak/flank 2.30 and the width count is 3,406/3,984 (85.5%). At the
  worst-ratio sample, pair peak/flank are 80.4/35.0 km, immediate shoulders average 45.2 km,
  and all 6/6 profile columns exceed outer flank + 2 km. A separate narrowest sample is
  1/6, with a 62.3 km pair column against ~35–37 km neighbours: the width gate is catching
  a genuinely needle-like episode as well as the peak/flank gate's high outer contrast.
- Diagnostic `kBelt=3`, same seed/frame size: the conveyor stays live (69 retirements),
  shortening is 99.1%, and R1/R3/R5 pass. The width gate passes at 3,971/3,983, but peak/flank
  still fails at 2.14. At that worst-ratio edge the pair peak is 76.5 km, the immediate
  shoulders are 79.9 km (pair/shoulder 0.96), and all 6/6 local columns exceed outer
  flank + 2 km. Its profile has nearby high shoulders on both sides of a lower contact
  column; it is not an isolated one-column needle. The outer-flank ratio alone therefore
  is not a needle detector in this profile.
- Strict default-planet audit, seed 1, 3,000 frames at 50 kyr/frame: the worst outer ratio
  is 4.80, but the profile's immediate-shoulder ratio is 2.22 and its width count is 4/6;
  the narrowest built sample is 3/6. The 4.80x sharpness is strongly affected by the
  outer-flank selection and the local profile must be read with it. The full output is in
  `experiments/logs/0.1.8-r2-profile-seed1-50.txt`.

The R2 composite still catches genuine narrow episodes, but its peak/flank component can
also reject an elevated broad or twin-crest belt. The six-column width clause is a count, not
an actual contiguous-run test. This is evidence to settle whether R2 is meant to cap absolute
height contrast or to identify a local needle; it is not authority to relax a threshold or
change the definition. No R2 arithmetic or threshold was changed.
