# Review and continuation — `e7534f0`

## Review scope

Reviewed `e7534f0` against its parent `4f060aea`, focusing on the P2.3b surface changes in
`js/pt/fluid.js`, their fixtures, and the forward P3/P4 design.

The P2.3b surface implementation has no reproduced functional regression: the independent
collision and profile-diffusion fixtures, whole-pipeline wrap checks and live relief gates all
remain green. The surface routine is intentionally a one-dimensional elevation model, however;
it changes `zh` only. It does not move particle mass, create sediment or close a material-mass
ledger. Source comments, fixture labels and the future plan now state that boundary clearly;
the misleading `kErode`/`slopeErode` settings are now named `kProfileDiff` and
`slopeProfileDiff`. P4 is assigned actual particle erosion and deposition.

The review did find a determinism blind spot. `PTS.hash()` sampled only every seventh marker's
`x`, `y` and `T`, and omitted strength, damage, mass, velocity, cluster identity and the new
surface profile. A changed or nondeterministic orogen could therefore pass a replay check. It
now fingerprints every active particle's physical/mechanical fields, `zh`, and both ledgers using
reusable typed-array views and two independent 32-bit mixing lanes. `state.reset()` also resets
cluster labels to `-1`, so the initial fingerprint cannot inherit a previous run's cluster ids.
The surface fixture asserts identical same-seed replays and that edits to `zh` and damage change
the fingerprint.

## Forward-plan review

The master plan still called itself “pre-implementation,” described two-way solid-velocity
penalty coupling that is not in the engine, treated an early 256x96 timing profile as the page
default, and left P3 as a single broad milestone with a melt-mass equation that omitted melt
still in flight or in reservoirs. Its open questions also included already-resolved P2 work and
an `js/pt/scale.js` file that does not exist.

`0.3.0-plan.md` now distinguishes the shipped one-way coupling from optional future work, records
the measured 256x24 default baseline and clarifies that higher quality rungs need their own
benchmarks. Milestones specify gates rather than progress snapshots, and the fixture catalogue
states contracts rather than pass counts. The P3/P4/P5 gates now include phase inventory,
latent enthalpy, capacity pressure, contact and material sediment transport. The surface section
now distinguishes the flat
computational domain from its separate relief profile, and deep recycling is specified as a
booked transfer rather than an implicit global mass sink. `0.3.0-p3-plan.md` breaks the next
phase into pool/ledger groundwork, enthalpy equilibrium, melt segregation, conduits/eruption,
flight/landing and integrated acceptance. It separates gross throughput counters from current
inventory, sets explicit mass/enthalpy and capacity gates, and leaves physical constants and
pool sizes to the named experiments instead of guessing them in the plan.
