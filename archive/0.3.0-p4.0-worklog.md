## What shipped

`js/pt/contact.js` (one global, `PTCONTACT`; exports `detect`, `brake`, `hinge`, `guard`) and
the hooks that let it sit inside G4. `solid.js`'s `kinematics()` was split: it is now the fit
and the drives and touches no position, and `project()` applies the rigid motion. `crust()`
runs `detect → brake → project → hinge → guard` when `P.contact`, and the same
`strength → clusters → kinematics → project` when it does not, so the kernel-off path is the
shipped P3 path unchanged.

New state is all workspace plus four cumulative counters (`contactShort`, `contactPush`,
`contactHold`, `hgOver`), all cleared by `reset()` and all mixed into `hash()`: a run that
reached the same parcels through a different contact history is not the same run. `fluid.js`'s orogen term
now reads `S.ctConv` at a column boundary the pass resolved and falls back to its own velocity
reading where it did not, so a fixture driving G7 directly still sees the old behaviour.

## The three defects found and fixed on the way

1. **The subduction side was inverted.** `sub = dT >= 0 ? 0 : 1` numbered the *left* plate as
   subducting exactly when it was the *warmer* one, so the kernel put the buoyant plate under
   the dense one. Now `sub = dT >= 0 ? 1 : 0`.
2. **The hold clamped the wrong thing.** `S.csBX[cb] += closing*dx` set the override's
   velocity to the *subducting* plate's velocity, which is a speed and not a hold. Now only the
   approaching component along the trench direction is cancelled, so the rule is idempotent:
   the override may roll back, it may not advance.
3. **The bending band's blend ran backwards.** `t = u/band` made `t = 0` the trench and `t = 1`
   the band's inner edge, the opposite of what the code and the plan both say ("from the
   plate's rigid velocity at its inner edge to the slab velocity at the trench"). The band
   therefore sheared *behind* the trench and nothing ever descended at it. Now
   `t = 1 - u/band`.

## The fixture, and why it looks the way it does

The plan's fixture is "a controlled two-plate convergence leg, both regimes, driven through the
real `crust()` entry". Every geometric choice below is forced by the engine rather than picked:

- **A depth offset at the trench is not optional.** Two clusters bond when a marker pair is
  within 1.3 cells in x *and* 1.3 dEta in eta (`solid.js`'s pair test). Two plates that share a
  depth band and hold adjacent columns weld on the first frame — which is the weld the pass
  exists to prevent, and it cannot be measured if it happens before the pass runs. The
  required separation grows with depth: ≈34 km at 100 km, ≈54 km at 200 km.
- **The weak band between the lids must be weak for a reason that is not heat.** A hot band
  between two cold lids is buoyant, and the upwelling it drives sinks the override's lid into
  the slab's depth range until the two bond: the weld arrived at 3.6 Myr with a 90 km gap and
  at 3.2–4.6 Myr with a 50 km one. The band is therefore cold and isothermal with the lids and
  weak only because its markers' age is zero. Nothing in the fixture is buoyant, nothing cools,
  and the only motion is the slab pull plus the contact response.
- **The price of an age-zero band is a leg length.** Age-zero material welds after
  `tauWeld·ln(4) ≈ 5.8 Myr`, so the controlled legs run 5 Myr: long enough for the slab to
  deliver ~225 km to the trench and for the band to bend below the crust base, short enough
  that the fixture's own ageing is not what ends the leg.
- **The subducting plate must be the thin-bulk/deep-trench one.** `kinematics`' pull is
  `kSlab · csS/csM` with `csS = Σ m·ry` over the markers below the centroid, so a plate whose
  lid is deeper on one side is pulled toward that side. The fixture's subducting lid is
  150–200 km in the bulk and 150–250 km at the trench.
- **The override needs a root of its own, or the hold holds nothing.** A rootless 65–100 km
  surface lid carries no pull, so it drifts *away* from the trench and the subduction leg can
  only report that both plates are nearly still. The review gave it a 65 km lid whose base
  deepens to 140 km from 6 columns in from the boundary (`OVRROOT`/`OVRBUF` in
  `experiments/pt-p4-lib.js`), which is the geometry `experiments/scratch/p4-hold-probe.js`
  scanned: root 100 with a 4-column buffer never fires the hold at all, root 140 with a 6-column
  buffer takes the override from −4.481 cm/yr to −0.090 cm/yr over 168 hold-frames. The buffer
  is what keeps the 50 km depth gap at the adjacent columns — an override root that starts *at*
  the boundary columns welds on the first frame.
- **`P.kDamage = 0` on the controlled legs.** With the shipped 90 the same layout shatters
  (`csN 8 → 18`): the failure law breaks both lids' leading edges for a reason that has nothing
  to do with contact, and the pass then never sees a boundary. The live leg runs the shipped 90.
- **No antimeridian channel is needed.** The depth separation that keeps the two plates apart
  at the trench keeps them apart around the back of the planet too, so the fixture is one clean
  two-cluster system rather than three.
- **"The override's base" is read as the base of the override's crust column, `P.yCrust`.** The
  alternative reading (the bottom of the override's own lid) cannot be satisfied non-trivially:
  a band that starts above the override's lid base and ends below it would have to overlap the
  override's lid in depth, which is exactly the bond the fixture exists to avoid. `yCrust` is
  also the boundary P4.1 names ("a slab segment that has bent below `P.yCrust` is no longer held
  by the contact pass"), so the gate measures the hand-over point.

## Dead ends, recorded so they are not retried

- **Marker-level interpenetration metrics do not work on the live planet.** The minimum
  intra-cluster lid distance is 0.0–0.3 km (markers legitimately stack vertically), the
  shared-column census reads 7–13% in *both* legs, and the lid fragments into 40+ clusters whose
  members pile up. Interpenetration is decided on the controlled legs, and the live leg reports
  the guard's count.
- **One vertical cut cannot split a periodic domain into two clusters.** A single hot gap band
  leaves one connected arc, so `csN` stays 1. Two cuts, or a depth offset, are needed.
- **Prescribing velocity or temperature does not stick.** `grid.js`'s `gatherVel` overwrites
  `S.vx` from the Stokes field every frame, and `phase.js`'s `equil` derives `S.T` from `S.H`.
  Convergence must be driven by a real mechanism (the slab pull), and a fixture that wants a
  temperature must write `S.H = PH.byT(...).h`.
- **A strong Stokes driver converges the plates and shatters them.** A T = 0.05 trench curtain
  plus a T = 0.95 antimeridian column drove ±3–6 cm/yr with live contacts and `csN 2 → 27` by
  30 Myr. The fixture drives convergence kinematically instead.
- **`csR2/csM` for a half-planet-wide plate exceeds `ringMax·wrap²`** (2.8e7 > 1.024e7), so
  `kSlab` cannot pull a plate that wide; the fixture's plates are 123 columns each.
- **`SC.clusters`, `SC.strength`, `SC.kinematics` and `SC.project` are not exported.** Only
  `SC.crust(M, S, dt)` is callable from a harness, so the fixture seeds its clusters by
  stepping the real pipeline and reading the "before" state after the first frame — `reset()`
  clears `S.cl` but not `S.csN`, so a t = 0 reading would be the previous leg's numbering.
- **Contact detection keyed on the shallowest marker per cluster per column loses the site**
  once the leading markers fail out of their clusters (`sites 0` while `contactShort`
  accumulated). Bounded by `ctTop` = 4 clusters per column and recorded, not gated.

## Measurements

Controlled legs, 5 Myr / 100 frames each, `csN 2` throughout, no weld:

| leg | sites | worst closing | subducting plate | override | shortening | band below `yCrust` |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| arrest, kernel on | 2 | 1.440 /Myr | 0.27 cm/yr | 0.27 cm/yr | 864 km | 0 (nothing bends) |
| subduction, kernel on | 4 | 1.448 /Myr | 4.50 cm/yr | −0.000 cm/yr | 1,089 km | **41 of 749** (0 with the kernel off) |
| control, kernel off | 0 | 7.73 cm/yr closing | 3.25 cm/yr | −4.48 cm/yr | 0 km | 0 |

- The arrest leg's *post*-brake closing is **−0.000 cm/yr** against the control's 7.73: the
  frame's own closing is what the next frame inherits, so this is the closing that was actually
  resolved, not the one `detect` measured before correcting.
- The subduction leg's override reads **−0.000 cm/yr against the control's −4.48** — 0.0% of its
  approach left — with the subducting plate keeping its own 4.50 cm/yr.
- The control's shared ground grows 1 → 7 columns over the leg and it bends nothing: the
  defect, measured on the same fixture with the response removed.
- The two impulse gates are the brake's balance and the hold's lack of it: the arrest leg books
  `S.contactHold` **7.45e−9** against a plate momentum of 1.64e+6, the subduction leg **1.40e+9**
  against 1.33e+7 over 242 hold-frames, and the control leg books neither.

Ledgers, measured across the kernel's own four calls on a live state (six contact frames):
mass, composition, melt and enthalpy all **bitwise unchanged** (0 of 6 frames differ), and the
two legs' mass residual is 0.00e+0. The arrest brake's impulse pair leaves the two clusters'
combined momentum unchanged to **0.00e+0** over six measured frames (px 2.029e+7). Enthalpy is
*not* invariant over a whole leg — the wall flux moves it — which is why it is measured across
the kernel and not across the leg.

Live shipped planet, 300 Myr, seed 1, 50 kyr/frame, `P.slab` off: **26 sites in the worst
frame**, worst closing 2.053/Myr, 20,061 hold-frames, 346,095 bending markers, 30,775 km of
absorbed shortening, **1,318 guard pushes on 922 of 6,000 frames (15.4%)**, **0 unbent sites**
against `ctHinge` 32, **worst residual 0 hard pairs on 0 frames**, booked impulse −5.21e+9
against a plate momentum of 3.00e+6, 0 parcels outside the domain, mass residual 0.00e+0,
fingerprint **2394862740042747** (kernel off 2242168670959781). The kernel-off control books
0 sites, 0 km and 0 pushes — it answers nothing. The pass costs **0.157 ms/frame, 1.36% of the
frame**, against the plan's 2% line.

The 1,318 pushes are the plan's own "how often the velocity-level response left work for it": the
velocity response is sufficient on the controlled legs (0 pushes in 200 frames) and insufficient
on a minority of live frames, where the lid has fragmented into many clusters whose fronts
jitter — the residual frame the review dumped was a 2-marker cluster at 312 cm/yr against a
1-marker one. The guard separates every pair it finds; its scope is the table the pass builds,
which is the lid band above `yCrust` (§1.4), so the count is a lower bound on the hard overlaps
and is reported as such.
