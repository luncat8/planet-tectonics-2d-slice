# 0.3.0 P4.0 review worklog — what the review of the contact kernel found

The P4.0 milestone landed with its fixture failing to run (`LEGS is not defined` in the live
leg) and its report describing a fixture that was not the one in the tree. This is the record of
the review that followed: every defect found, the measurement that decided the fix, and the
numbers the corrected kernel and fixture produce. `archive/0.3.0-p4.0-worklog.md` is the build
record and `archive/0.3.0-p4.0-report.md` the paste-back summary; both were corrected to the
state this review ended at.

## Defects found in the shipped kernel

1. **The fixture did not run.** The live leg counted `LEGS` legs but `LEGS` was only defined in
   the controlled-leg section's scope. Reproduced before anything was changed, so the fix is
   known against a known failure.
2. **`S.contactHold` was dead.** The brake wrote it and nothing read it: not the HUD, not the
   fingerprint, not the fixture. It is now signed (Σ `csM·Δvx` over the brake's two sides, so an
   arrest pair cancels and books nothing while a subduction hold books the impulse the override
   did not return), mixed into `hash()`, and gated twice — the arrest leg's is 7.45e−9 against a
   plate momentum of 1.64e+6, the subduction leg's 1.40e+9 against 1.33e+7.
3. **`S.hgOver` was write-only.** The bending-band cap counted the subducting sites it turned
   away and nothing reported them. It is now a cumulative counter in the fingerprint, a HUD
   field (`unbent N/ctHinge`, printed only when non-zero) and a fixture gate.
4. **`ctHinge` 16 was below the live worst case.** Measured on the shipped planet at 300 Myr:
   26 sites in the worst frame before the review's other changes, 29 in an intermediate tree —
   a cap of 16 silently unbends a third of them. Raised to **32**; the fixture now gates
   "0 unbent sites over 6,000 frames, cap 32 against a worst frame of 26".
5. **`hgC`, the bending band's closing rate, was the two plates' closing projected on the
   trench normal** (`closing·dx`). That stretched the band's velocity blend by the override's
   own approach, so the band descended faster than the contact it belonged to. It is now the
   subducting plate's own trench-normal approach, which is what "the slab's speed is the
   convergence the contact measured" means for a band that belongs to one plate.
6. **The guard could push a marker outside the domain.** The separation is a position move, and
   a move in this engine ends the way every other move does: eta rebuilt from y, then
   `G.keepInside`. Both were missing; a pair straddling the reflection depth could be pushed
   through it. Added, and the fixture now gates "0 mantle or melt parcels outside
   `[eMin, eMax]`, kernel on and off".
7. **Indentation and a comment that had lost its subject** in `hash()`, the band blend and the
   guard. Cosmetic, but the block that was mis-indented was the block that mixed the contact
   counters into the fingerprint.

## The override had to be given a root

The fixture's override was a rootless 65–100 km surface lid, which is why the P4.0 report could
only claim "the override is held: 0.119 cm/yr against the control's 0.16" — both numbers are
noise around a plate that was never approaching. `kinematics`' pull is `kSlab · csS/csM` with
`csS = Σ m·ry` over the markers below the centroid, so a lid with no root has no pull and drifts
*away* from the trench: there was nothing for the hold to hold.

`experiments/scratch/p4-hold-probe.js` scanned the override's geometry against the hold's own
response (`ROOT1` = the root depth the override's lid deepens to, `BUF` = how many columns in
from the boundary it starts):

| override | hold, kernel off | hold, kernel on | hold-frames | impulse | deep records reached |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| root 100 km, buffer 4 | never fires | never fires | 0 | 0 | — |
| **root 140 km, buffer 6** | **−4.481 cm/yr** | **−0.090 cm/yr** | **168** | **1.38e+9** | 1 → 62 |

Root 140 with a 6-column buffer is what shipped (`OVRROOT`/`OVRBUF` in
`experiments/pt-p4-lib.js`). The buffer is what keeps the 50 km depth gap at the adjacent
columns — the weld condition is a marker pair within 1.3 cells in x and 1.3 dEta in eta, and an
override root that starts *at* the boundary columns welds on the first frame. The gate is now
the one the plan actually asks for: the override's approach is **−0.000 cm/yr against the
control's −4.48**, i.e. 0.0% of it left, while the subducting plate keeps its own 4.50 cm/yr.

## The residue hunt: what "zero hard overlaps" needed

The plan's gate is that colliding plates do not interpenetrate. Three things had to be true
before that could be measured rather than asserted.

**A frame-end re-read of the contact table is meaningless.** `POOL.remove` compacts the tail
into the hole it made, G5 re-phases wholly molten parcels and G6/G7 move material, so the slots
`ctP[]` names are stale by the end of the frame. An end-of-frame count therefore reports pairs
that no longer exist and misses pairs that do. The fix is two readings: `sweep(M, S, hard,
move)` gained a read-only mode (`move = false` counts and pushes nothing) and `guard()` now
publishes `S.d.resid`, read **inside** the guard where its own table is live. The fixture gates
that number per frame and re-reads a freshly built table once at the end of the leg.

**The first honest reading was non-zero: 3 hard pairs on 568 of 6,000 live frames.** A scan over
the sweep budget (`experiments/scratch/p4-sweep-scan.js`, 300 Myr each, `P.slab` off) showed
more sweeping did not clear it:

| `ctSweep` | worst residue | frames with residue | pushes | worst d/hard |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 3 | 301 / 6,000 | 1,073 | 0.523 |
| 3 | 3 | 568 / 6,000 | 2,870 | 0.976 |
| 6 | 3 | 401 / 6,000 | 3,720 | 0.993 |
| 10 | 3 | 248 / 6,000 | 3,560 | 1.000 |

Ten sweeps left three pairs *and* grew the push count, which is the signature of a solver
chasing something it can never satisfy. `experiments/scratch/p4-resid-dump.js` wrapped the guard
to dump a residue in situ, mid-G4, where the table is live:

```
IN SITU at t 176.85 Myr: resid 1, pushes 3, sites 2, csN 6
  cols 42/43  cl 1 (n 2, csY 8, csVX 31.27) vs 4 (n 1, csY 22, csVX 0.00)
  slots 151,1171  d 34.37 = 1.000 hard  y 8.1/22.4  ddx 31.25 ddy 14.31
after 21 more guard calls: resid 1, pushes 3
```

`d/hard = 1.000`: the pair was sitting **on** the radius. The push computes
`0.5·(hard − d)` and moves both markers along the separation, which lands them on the radius to
within an ulp — and a strict `d2 < h2` then finds them still inside, pushes them by ~1e−15, and
finds them again. Every residue the live planet produced was such a pair, which is why the count
never fell and why more sweeps only added pushes. `sweep()` now squares its radius with a
rounding slop (`hard² · (1 − 1e−12)`) and the fixture's independent `hardPairs()` uses the same
slop, so the two readings cannot disagree about what "inside" means:

| `ctSweep` | worst residue | frames with residue | pushes |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 3 | 301 / 6,000 | 1,073 |
| 2 | **0** | **0 / 6,000** | 1,318 |
| 3 (shipped) | **0** | **0 / 6,000** | 1,318 |

Two sweeps are what the live planet needs; the third is a no-op pass that costs nothing
measurable (11.35 vs 11.49 ms/frame) and is kept as margin, because interference — separating
p from q pushes p into r — is real and the budget is the only thing bounding it. The push count
falls from 2,870 to **1,318** with the slop in: 1,552 of the pushes the P4.0 report quoted were
the guard re-separating pairs it had already separated.

**Worst residue on the live leg is now 0 pairs on 0 of 6,000 frames**, and the leg-end re-read
finds 0 and leaves 0. The controlled legs never pushed at all (0 over 200 frames), so their
"zero hard overlaps" was never in doubt — it was the live leg that needed the slop.

## The fixture, rebuilt

`experiments/pt-p4-contact.js` went from 22 checks to **31**, and the geometry moved into
`experiments/pt-p4-lib.js` so that P4.1's harness drives the same two plates instead of a copy
(`k5-table-lib.js` and `lib.js` are the precedent: a library prints nothing and gates nothing).
The lib owns `configure`, `livePlanet`, `setT`, `twoPlates`, `plates`, `books`, `mean` and the
override constants; the harness owns its legs and its own isolation.

Two of the review's changes to the fixture are worth naming because they are the kind that
silently pass:

- **The "before" reading is not a t = 0 reading.** `deep0` and its kin are taken after the first
  frame, because `reset()` clears `S.cl` but not `S.csN`, so a t = 0 census is the previous leg's
  numbering. The claim "0 before the response" comes from the kernel-off control leg on the same
  geometry, not from a t = 0 snapshot.
- **Every owner harness isolates its own kernel.** Once `js/pt/slab.js` is in the tree, P4.1's
  pass changes the live planet, so `pt-p4-contact.js` sets `P.slab = false` in `configure()`,
  `live()` and its cost block. Its live numbers are P4.0's numbers and stay comparable with the
  P4.0 report; the integrated numbers belong to P4.4's acceptance harness.
- **A short `liveMyr` argument goes red, not empty.** The live leg needs ~100 Myr before the
  shipped lid fragments into converging plates; below that the two checks asking for a contact
  fail rather than report a smaller census. The fixture header said otherwise and now says this.

## Not changed, and why

**The contact table has no plate-size threshold.** The dump above shows the sites it answers
include 1- and 2-marker clusters (`n 2` at `csVX 31.27` km/Myr — 312 cm/yr of lid shrapnel) as
well as plates. A threshold at `P.plateMin` would make the census a plate census and would cut
the guard's push count, but it would also let fragments interpenetrate, and the plan scopes the
pass to "a pair of adjacent surface columns held by different clusters" with no size test. The
fragment population is a consequence of the shipped failure law, so this is a P4.4 freeze
question next to `contactYield`, `contactdT` and `contactHard`, not a P4.0 correction. It is
recorded here with its measurement so P4.4 does not have to find it again.
