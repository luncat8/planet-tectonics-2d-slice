# 0.1.0 — Review of M4 (slabs, plumes, melts) and the contact findings behind 0.1.5

Scope: the M4 commit (`305d96f`, `js/slab.js`, `js/mantle.js`, `js/magma.js`, the
`crust.js` arc/LIP terms and the `plates.js` slab-pull/drag terms), reviewed by running
the suite and by auditing what plate-boundary events do to the drawn section. Two
verdicts: M4 is accepted with two ledger bugs fixed in the same commit, and the contact
behaviour it exposes is the reason `0.1.5-plan.md` exists.

## 2. Bugs found and fixed

**Erosion converted lithology with no ledger entry.** `SURF.k6` removes beds with
`COL.removeTop` and re-deposits them as `sed`, so felsic and mafic rock became sediment
without either account knowing. Measured on the default planet after 150 Myr: sediment
mass +110.4e9 m3 against a consumption of 0.26e9 (relative error 208%), felsic 8.7%,
mafic 9.7%. Fix: `COL.removeTop` now reports the removal per lithology
(`COL.removedLi`) and the erosion loop books every non-sediment bed as an explicit
rock -> sediment transformation in `ledMixOut`/`ledMixIn`, the same account
`COL.compact` already uses for cross-lithology merges.

**A LIP stored lava and credited mafic melt.** `CRU.lipGrowth` pushed a `lava` layer and
incremented `ledProd[maf]`. Since `S.mass()` measures the stored lithology, the lava
account ended with 23.7e9 m3 of mass and no production entry (relative error 18x).
Fix: credit `ledProd[lava]`. `MAG.spill` had the same shape (chamber counted as mafic
melt, spill stored as `sill`); it now moves the production entry with the mass.

**The gate could not have caught either.** `ledger-check.js` M4.3 set `SIM.k[5]` and
`SIM.k[6]` to null for its balance fixture, so the surface and column kernels — the two
that break the balance — were disabled for the duration. The M4.3 result (1.94e-15) is
therefore a statement about K1/K4 only. Fix: a new M4.4 section runs the *complete*
pipeline (800 frames, every kernel) and demands the same closure, plus three targeted
fixtures (one erosion step on a felsic bed, a chamber spill, a LIP arrival). After the
fixes: worst relative error 1.09e-14 over 800 frames with everything enabled.

Also strengthened: `m2-check.js`'s 100 Myr and 700 Myr runs used a *total*-volume
ledger with a 1e-6 tolerance and a comment admitting that erosion converts lithologies.
They now use the strict per-lithology check (1e-12) and both pass.

## 3. Contact findings (the input to `0.1.5-plan.md`)

`experiments/contact-audit.js`, seed 1, 50 kyr/frame, 3000 frames (150 Myr), log saved
as `experiments/logs/0.1.0-M4-contact-audit.txt`:

```
crust     max 161.1 km   thickest column at a contact 76.6 km   belt 5 columns
surface   worst single-frame move 4657 m (frame 2985)   mean 1.4 m   1523 samples > 100 m
frames    on a birth/death frame 4657 m   on a quiet frame 276 m   (thickness 17.9 vs 1.3 km)
columns   worst single-frame thickness change 17.9 km   worst local slope 25.2%
topology  93 births/deaths, 91 of them at a site that had already flipped
stacks    135883 column-frames with an inverted bed, 66923 column-frames at layerCap
```

Tracing the events:

- **A consumption re-partitions territory.** Every event frame with a large move is a
  frame where the column count drops. The two columns flanking the dead one jump from
  62.6 / 78.2 km of width to exactly 101.6 km and lose 22–38% of their crust thickness
  in that one frame (e.g. 24.7 -> 15.4 km). The drawn surface (which is isostatic in the
  thickness) therefore drops by kilometres in a single 50 kyr step. `COL.consume` moves
  the *crust* carefully but the territory follows the midpoint rule, so the survivor
  inherits the dead column's ground and is diluted by it.
- **The first collision lands on one column.** At t = 4.5 Myr (frame 90): the winner
  receives the loser's whole stack (+11.8 km in one frame, 50.8 -> 62.6 km) while its
  neighbour is diluted by 9.3 km in the same frame. The collision happened at 4.5 Myr at
  the default slider, i.e. within the first two seconds of watching the page.
- **Boundary flicker is the most frequent source of pops.** 91 of 93 topology events
  happen at a site that had already flipped. `COL.intents` keys off the classified edge,
  which the hysteresis holds at 0.1–0.3 mm/yr, so a boundary with no meaningful motion
  spawns and consumes columns in alternation (one site in the log cycles 8 times with
  `nL` jumping 5 <-> 20).
- **Stacks churn and invert.** 135 883 column-frames have a denser lithology above a
  lighter one and 66 923 are at `layerCap`. `COL.consume` pushes the loser's stack on
  top of the winner's bed by bed, so two stratigraphies are stacked instead of merged,
  `COL.collapseMove` pushes moved felsic on top of the survivor's cover, and
  `CRU.addLayer` (arc, LIP) does the same. Bedding is the most visible thing in the
  section, so this is a look problem as much as a bookkeeping one.
- **The collision feed is unbounded.** Nothing caps `hTot`: 161 km at 150 Myr, and 946
  km in a fixture with K5 disabled. The design's `hCollapse` gate is a slow diffusion,
  not a ceiling, and `vColl`'s collision resistance is a fixed drag that a driven plate
  wins against.

Everything above is a property of the *model as specified*, not a typo: the design's
"remove the thinner column and transfer its complete stack" comes from a fixed Eulerian
grid, where emptying a cell is free. On a Lagrangian column with a derived width, the
same rule moves territory and dilutes neighbours in one frame. `0.1.5-plan.md` therefore
changes the rule (R1–R6 and M0–M5 there), and the audit script becomes the gate.

## 4. Follow-ups not taken here

- `m3-check.js` disables K1's M4 sources for its sediment fixtures (`SIM.k[1] = null`);
  that is now unnecessary for the ledger and can be retired when its fixtures are
  revisited in 0.1.5 M5.
- `SLAB.advance` moves the trench anchor with the *mantle* surface flow (`uSurf`) rather
  than with the overriding plate; M4's own design intent is the edge position, and the
  0.1.5 M4 item fixes it together with the anchor jitter.
- `MNT.stepPlumes` warms the fan without a per-column heat budget; the fan field is
  bounded but not conserved. Not a correctness bug (the fan is a temperature grid, not a
  mass account), noted for whoever revisits the plume heat budget.
