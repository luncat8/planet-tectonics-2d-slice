## Why the bound water in `reset()` is passive until a slab sinks

In the `'cool'` initial condition, rows `j = 1..2` (`M.yN[1] = 8.4 km`, `M.yN[2] = 17.1 km <=
P.yWet = 20 km`) have `T <= 0.22`. With `P.slabW = 0.005` and `P.meltWW = 20`, the solidus drops
by `0.10`, which puts `Ts(y, 0, 0.005) >= 0.68` at every depth — more than `0.45` above `T`. So:

- at `t = 0`, `PHASE.byT(T, y, 0, w0)` returns `melt = 0` and `h = T`, bitwise identical to the
  dry start;
- while those parcels sit in the cold lid or sink as a cold slab (`T <= P.slabT = 0.45`), `xi <= 0`
  in `PHASE.byH`, which returns `OUT.t = H, OUT.melt = 0` independent of `cW`;
- measured over 6,000 frames (300 Myr) of the live planet with `P.arc = false` on the same initial
  water (`1,399.35 km²`), **0.00 km² of water ever reaches a chamber (`invChW = 0.00`)** — the wet
  surface skin never melts on its own. Instead, its water rides the subducting slabs straight to
  `yDeep = 2,400 km` and parks `42.28 km²` in the deep records (`invDpW`).

When `P.arc = true` on the identical starting state:

- for the first `137.7 Myr` (`2,753 frames`), no detached slab parcel exists below `yCrust`, so
  the planet's trajectory is bitwise identical to P4.1;
- once detached slabs enter `(yArcMin, yArcMax]` (`250..600 km`), `ARC.run` releases `min(kArc *
  dt, arcFrac)` of their water into the warm mantle wedge above them (`P.yCham < y <= P.yArcMin`),
  producing `320.1 km²` of wedge melt, delivering `7.26 km²` of slab water to the shallow chambers
  (`invChW`), and leaving only the unreleased `7.25 km²` (`invDpW`, down from `42.28 km²`) to reach
  the deep records at `2,400 km`.

Because no water is created or destroyed after `t = 0` — every release debits `dW` from the slab
parcel and credits `dW` to the wedge parcel — `S.invWTot` closes to `0.00e+0` over 6,000 frames of
the live planet and in every P3 composition-closure gate (`pt-p3-melt.js` §7b, `pt-p3-erupt.js`
§9).

## Three measurements that shaped `js/pt/arc.js`

1. **Where the wedge is (`P.yCham < y <= P.yArcMin`).** `experiments/scratch/p4-arc-probe.js`
   measured the mantle above the slab across depth bands at 150–300 Myr. Allowing `y_wedge` up to
   `600 km` (`p4-arc-sweep3.js`) produced `5,994 km²` of deep melt at `350..500 km`, and **0.00 km²**
   of it reached the surface (`depW = 0.00`): a `PH.melt` parcel born at `450 km` has to rise
   `410 km` (`7 Myr` at `vMelt = 60 km/Myr`) through the cooler upper mantle, so G3 conduction cools
   it below `meltHold` and `MT.transit` freezes it back on the way up. Restricting the wedge to
   `(P.yCham, P.yArcMin]` (`40..250 km`, above the `250..600 km` slab window) puts every wedge
   candidate within `210 km` (`<= 3.5 Myr`) of `yCham = 40 km` and makes `y_wedge < y_slab` hold by
   construction for every `(wedge, slab)` pair.
2. **Retiring a wedge candidate once it reaches `P.meltHold` (`P.arcTop = 4`).** Without a
   saturation check (`p4-arc-sweep.js`), once the hottest wedge parcel in a column received water,
   its `Ts - T` gap became negative, so every subsequent slab parcel in that column dumped its water
   into the same already-molten parcel (`665.9 km²` of wedge melt at `kArc = 0.25`). Keeping the top
   `P.arcTop = 4` unsegregated (`melt < P.meltHold`) candidates per column and advancing `arcC[col]`
   as soon as `melt[best] >= P.meltHold` moves the remaining water to the next warm parcel in the
   column (`2,689.8 km²` in the same sweep — 4x more wedge melt from the same water). Conversely,
   capping `cW` at `0.015` (`p4-arc-sweep5.js`) stranded parcels whose dry gap was `> 0.26` just
   below `meltHold = 0.10` and cut wedge melt to `597.2 km²`; `meltHold` itself is the only
   physically honest threshold because `MT.detach` separates at `meltHold` on the very next line of
   G5.
3. **Shift equivariance (`pt-wrap.js`) and active-column filtering.** `ARC.wedge` bins columns by
   `Math.round(x / dx)` folded into `[0, nx)` and searches neighbour columns `di = -r .. +r`
   (`r = Math.round(P.arcSpan / dx)`), never computing a floating-point `x[q] - x[p]` distance. A
   whole-cell shift of the periodic domain therefore permutes column indices without changing a
   single candidate, gap, or transfer (`pt-wrap.js` passes at `200..205 Myr` with `max 1.32e-9 km`
   in `x`, `3.51e-13` in `eta`, `A 41 B 41` chambers, `transactions true`). Collecting the wet slab
   parcels into `S.order` first and marking only the columns within `+-r` of them (`arcN[i] = -1`
   for unreached columns) avoids scanning or sorting the ~8,000 upper-mantle parcels in columns with
   no slab beneath them, cutting `ARC.run` from `0.60 ms/frame` (`4.9%`) to **`0.21 ms/frame`
   (`1.70%` of the frame at `t = 300 Myr`)**.
