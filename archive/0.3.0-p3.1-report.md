Request (verbatim): "0.3.0‑P3 (conserved melt / eruption pool‑ledger for the particle engine) —
the pile rule it was waiting on is now in place." P3.0 (the pool, the phases, the routing) has

## What landed

- **The phase law** (`js/pt/phase.js`, global `PTPHASE`, loaded right after `params.js`): a
  depth- and composition-dependent solidus/liquidus and one normalized latent heat, with `byT` and
  `byH` as exact inverses. `H` is the conserved quantity; `T` and `melt` are the equilibrium
  state it maps to.
- **`params.js`**: P1's proxy block (`meltProxy`, `meltDepth`, `meltTop`, `meltExcess`,
  `meltRange`, `meltUpRef`, `meltRise`, `meltBuild`, `meltDecay`) deleted — none of those constants
  is a physical melt parameter. The new anchors are `meltSurf .77`, `meltSup .02`, `meltOnset 700`,
  `meltGap .15`, `meltLat .25`, `meltWF .2`, `meltWW 20`, with `meltDrop`/`meltInvLam`/`meltU0`/
  `meltDeep` derived so `Ts(700 km) = 1` bitwise.
- **The wiring**: G3 hands the conduction increment to `H` (`gatherDT` no longer writes `T`);
  `SIM.k[4]` is `PHASE.equil` then the crust pass; the crust law gains melt weakening `(1 - xi)`;
  the `flip < 1` blend relaxes toward the *enthalpy* the grid temperature implies.
- **The overlay** is now material: `S.Mg` is `scatterT`'s mass-weighted node mean of the parcels'
  own melt, the same field the HUD and the raster read. `S.MgS`/`S.rowT`/`F.melt`/`meltSample` are
  gone; the legend says `gold = the mantle's equilibrium melt (conserved in H, one host per
  parcel)`.
- **The books**: `gatherDT` books `S.hLedger` (enthalpy), `equil` books `S.ledger` (sensible); the
  gap is exactly the latent heat stored, `meltLat * (invLTot - invL0)`, with `invL0` captured at
  reset.
- **HUD**: `ledger <hLedger>  walls <wall>  gap <x>  latent <…>` and `melt <x>% of mass  max
  <xi>  depth <…> km`.
- `experiments/pt-p3-enthalpy.js` (new, 65 checks). `pt-check` (19), `pt-p3-routing` (39),
  `pt-load-check` (9) re-based; `pt-ui` (42) gained three HUD checks; `pt-wrap`'s twin list fixed;
  `pt-melt-check` **retired** — its contract was the deleted proxy.
- `index.html` and `particles.html` stay byte-identical.

## The numbers that matter

- **The curve** (gated): `Ts(0) .7700`, `Ts(100) .8853`, `Ts(300) .9626`, `Ts(700) 1.0000`,
  `Ts(2900) 1.0181`; `Tl - Ts = .15` everywhere; monotone in depth; water 100× the per-mass solidus
  depression of felsic material. Crossing the mush costs `1 + L/gap = 2.6667` heat per unit
  temperature (7e-13 relative).
- **The mapping**: 50 000 samples round-trip with worst `|dH| 4.4e-16`; the shipped initial
  condition is already the equilibrium state of its own `H` (0 of 23552 parcels disagree, worst
  `|dxi| 2.78e-16`).
- **The melt the shipped planet carries**: 1.033% of its mass at `t = 0` (4823 parcels, `xi` up to
  0.195, mean depth 398 km), 0.920% at 100 Myr, 1.367% at 300 Myr (`xi` 0.265, mean depth 256 km,
  deepest mushy parcel 1099 km at 200 Myr — carried down by its host). The worst parcel is a
  quarter-molten mush, and the zone never covers more than 1.4% of the mass: a mush, not a magma
  ocean. Node means reach 0.239 at 300 Myr over 1837 of 6400 nodes.
- **The books close**: every print of the 300 Myr report has `hLedger/wall = 1.000` and
  `hLedger - ledger == meltLat * (invLTot - invL0)`; the 150 Myr fixture run ends `dH_total
  -5.8039e5 == hLedger`, intake/wall 1.000, sensible −5.741e5 + latent −6.327e3 = the whole intake.
  The x operator (the direction with no wall) conserves every row and loses each row's second
  moment to exactly its Dirichlet energy.
- **Cost**: `PHASE.equil` 0.180-0.285 ms per pass over 23552 parcels; the shipped frame 12.6 ms
  (bar 16). P3.0's own unchanged code measures 13.6-14.7 ms on this host, so the frame budget is
  now gated on the host plus a relative check of the new pass.

## Decisions recorded

- **`t = 0` is mushy and stays that way.** Round 4's law leaves the shipped `'cool'` start with
  1.033% of its mass inside the mush, because the seeded initial-condition wave straddles the
  solidus below ~700 km. The alternatives — raise the solidus (melt then unreachable: round 3) or
  edit the initial condition (changes the planet every P1/P2 fixture is measured against) — were
  rejected. Gated instead: the start must be the equilibrium state of its own `H`, which it is, and
  the census is reported rather than hidden.
- **Melt stays attached to its host.** No segregation, reservoir, eruption or flight. The deepest
  mushy parcel of the run being a carried one is this milestone's signature, not a defect.
- **Four calibration rounds, three abandoned.** Recorded in full (constants, what each measured,
  why it was dropped) in the worklog §3, because the round-1 dead end ("the deep solidus sat above
  any attainable `H`") is the trap a reimplementation will walk into.

## Defects caught in review

- No engine defect: the new fixture's first-run failures were all harness assumptions — bitwise
  equality across two affine branches of the same law, `SIM.run(1)` on a live pipeline moving the
  total, a fingerprint test that hashed the welding clock, and a water-potency comparison at one
  reference amount.
- `pt-wrap`'s save/load list predated P3.1 and dropped `H`: the twin ran on the wrong enthalpy and
  the gate read 12/19 red. Fixed — 19/19.
- `params.js`'s law comment was rewritten: it named a tunable that no longer exists, wrote the
  solidus against the wrong asymptote, and claimed the deep mantle can never melt — false, because
  a descending drip carries its melt.

## What is not decided

- **The pool reserve is still unfrozen** (P3.0's open item; candidates `resMelt .05 / resAir .03 /
  resDep .12`). P3.2 is the first consumer.
- **The melt split criterion** (plan §5): what fraction of a mushy parcel's melt leaves it.
- **Density contrast / buoyancy of melt.** The Stokes solve reads `T`, so latent heat currently
  makes an isenthalpic parcel *colder* and less buoyant; real melt is less dense than its host.
  P3.2's segregation needs this settled.
- `meltWF`/`meltWW` are linear coefficients with no saturation or partitioning; the mush's heat
  capacity is one constant rather than a temperature-dependent curve.
