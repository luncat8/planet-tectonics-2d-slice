# 0.2.4 — vent cadence: the Vbirth / tauVent table, and Vbirth 4

Owner: `archive/0.2.3-melt-contact-worklog.md` §7 — "Duty cycle 93–98%. If repose matters
before 0.3.0-P3, `Vbirth` / `tauVent` is the knob, and it is its own contact-leg table."
Taken here.

**Landed: `P.Vbirth` 1 → 4 km3. `P.tauVent` stays the design's 2 Myr.** The row is the
best duty/repose the 0.2.3 arc cone survives: every row that buys more section duty
either flattens the cone below the watchable height or nicks a contact leg.

## 0. What the table had to carry

One table, three measurements per candidate, all delegated so no gate is implemented
twice:

- **contact** — the four strict contact legs (`contact-audit.js --strict`). Death timing
  moves when tephra reaches the stack, which is exactly R1/R3's business, so the gate
  set re-runs at every candidate. 60 legs, 15 candidates.
- **cadence** — one leg's eruption cadence (`vent-cadence.js`, new) on the *same four
  legs*: the decision columns. It separates what one duty number conflates: section duty
  (any vent feeding, the standing 0.2.x metric), **visDuty** (the box receives ≥ 0.1
  cells² that frame — a hover vent feeds a per-frame trickle the viewer cannot see),
  the style splits, and per-life banks (a life is one slot's birth → death: its length,
  its feeding share, the same-slot repose between death and the next birth). Two
  mechanism columns name the regime: **starved%** (alive vent-frames whose `venIdle`
  counter is running, i.e. the post-fill chamber below `Vdie`) and **maxIdle** (the
  furthest any counter got). `maxIdle` lands exactly on the completing step below
  `tauVent` at every candidate (1.95/1.90 at tauVent 2, 0.20 at 0.25): the death rule
  closes these lives, not tectonics — the lifecycle is alive, and the knob has somewhere
  to bite.
- **cone** — the live arc cone at the same candidate (`tephra-pile.js`), the design-scale
  gain 0.2.3 landed. Its band gates judge the chosen row only.

The hook is each owner's `VB` / `TV` environment override (contact-audit's `KG`
pattern), re-deriving `VbirthM2` and `kErupt` the way `params.js` does. Candidates are
absolute design units, so the base (1, 2) is the first row. `Vbirth` crosses `Vch` on
purpose: above the vent chamber's cap the column batches a whole episode before opening
a slot, which is where the repose response was expected to live.
`experiments/vent-contact-sweep.js` (new) prints the three tables and keeps every run's
log; it is report-only, like the other tuning sweeps.

## 2. What the two knobs actually move

- **`Vbirth` is the episode batch.** 1 → 4 (→ 32) cuts births 20–40% per leg (114 → 92
  → 42 on `3000/1/50`), grows the mean episode (9.8 → 11.7 → 23.9 cells²), and lengthens
  the per-edifice repose 2–4× (medGap 0.90 → 2.05 → 3.13 Myr on `3000/1/50`, 2.80 →
  5.00 → 6.70 on `5000/5/100`). The largest episode (126–305 cells² at the landed row)
  is the plume's batch life — design-scale, `erupt-bench`'s explosive reference is 150.
- **`tauVent` is the death clock, and shortening it costs the cone.** 0.25/0.5 fire the
  death rule sooner, churn lives (births up to 241 on one row) and buy 4–12 duty points
  — at the price of the cone on every candidate (`tauVent` ≤ 0.5 flattens all five
  `Vbirth` columns), and at (4, 0.25) of R2 as well. The design absolute (2 Myr) is
  where the cone holds. The table measured it; nothing needed to move it.
- **The section duty is a union over phase-tiled vents, and it has a floor.** The
  longest continuous section-wide feed stays in the 10³ frames at every candidate
  (935–4528; 1945 at the landed row on `3000/1/50`): with melt windows on several
  columns, someone is nearly always feeding. The duty − visDuty gap is the trickle
  hover (a chamber at hover feeds a fraction of a cell² per frame, counted as feeding,
  invisible). That is why the busiest leg (`3000/1/50`) only moves 97.9 → 97.5 while
  `5000/5/50` moves 96.9 → 78.9.

## 3. Why not the duty champions

`(16, 2)` reads the design's cadence number directly — duty 69.1% mean (53.6–88.2),
visDuty 53.0% (inside 0.2.1's 57–69% on three of four legs), repose up to 6.9 Myr, and
16/16 contact green — but its arc cone is 21.00 × 5.43 px against the watchable 8 px
gate, its piles run 33 px wide on `5000/5/50` (the design band is 10–30), and the
cone's `pileSumMax` says why: 71 cells² banked against 115.9 at the landed row. The
0.2.0 §2.1 claim (one episode builds a 10–30 px edifice) is an acceptance gate; the
duty number is the softer claim. `(32, 0.5)`'s 69.4% mean fails R1 outright. So the
landing takes the largest repose that keeps every standing claim green.

turns, and the table shows the ceiling (the cone) binding before the number reaches the
reference. The two ways past that ceiling are named in §7.

## 7. Open, and next

- **The duty number vs the cone ceiling.** `(16, 2)` measures 69.1% mean duty at the
  cost of the cone; the cone is written at death and slumps while it waits. If the
  section's *number* (not one cone's cadence) must reach 0.2.1's 57–69% before 0.3.0-P3,
  the knob is the write-back rule — pile before death — which is 0.3.0-P3's own toy
  physics (`0.3.0-p3-plan.md`; 0.2.1 §3 parked it there: "tephra should pile before it
  is written back"). This table's batching region (`Vbirth` ≥ 8) is the measurement that
  constraint would free.
- **The trickle hover.** duty counts any `venFlux > 0`; a chamber hovering near `Vdie`
  feeds fractions of a cell² per frame and keeps the union fed. `Vdie` (the hover vs
  "empty" threshold — the probe rows in `vent-cadence.js` carry a `VD` override for it)
  and `tDrain` (the fill/drain separation) are the remaining lifecycle constants, and
  either is its own table if the hover must stop counting as an eruption.
- **0.2.x stays a patch series.** The particle engine's conserved-melt eruptions are
  still 0.3.0-P3 (`0.3.0-p3-plan.md` P3.0 pool/ledger).
