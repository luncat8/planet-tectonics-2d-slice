# 0.5.0 M0 — soup kernel bake-off

**Result: ALL PASS, 78 / 78 checks.** The default carried into M4 is a 6,656-particle,
screen-space, two-sweep PBF kernel with row-mean buoyancy, clock-based carried circulation,
analytic pair heat and a 0.35-diameter displacement cap. Its 300 Myr leg passes the
preregistered S1 **fluid-distribution** gates. Contact is retained for granular deposits,
not accepted as a mantle-convection alternative. The planck reference is genuine and
passes its own checks, but the custom SoA kernel is 5–9x faster at matched populations.

This is an M0 kernel result, not release acceptance. There is no crust, mechanical lid,
phase transition, eruption, load-pressure melt or new page yet; S2–S4 have not run. The
existing application pages and engines are unchanged.

## Reproduce and evidence

Run on this checkout with Node 22:

```sh
node experiments/sp-bench.js \
  --out experiments/logs/0.5.0-M0-sp-bench.json \
  --svg experiments/logs/0.5.0-M0-soup.svg \
  > experiments/logs/0.5.0-M0-sp-bench.txt 2>&1
node experiments/planck-look.js \
  --out experiments/logs/0.5.0-M0-planck-look.json \
  --svg experiments/logs/0.5.0-M0-planck-look.svg
node experiments/planck-smoke.js \
  --out experiments/logs/0.5.0-M0-planck-smoke.json
```

The final soup bank ran in 120.8 s. Environment recorded in its JSON: Node v22.22.3,
Linux x64, Intel Xeon Processor @ 2.60 GHz. The quick development path is
`node experiments/sp-bench.js --quick`; it is not a substitute for the full bank.

Evidence files:

- `experiments/sp-prototype.js`: headless SoA kernel and display-geometry diagnostic;
- `experiments/sp-bench.js`: `sp-bench-v2`, correctness, cost, stability and behavior bank;
- `experiments/logs/0.5.0-M0-sp-bench.{txt,json}` and `0.5.0-M0-soup.svg`;
- `experiments/planck-look.js`: walls, persistent contacts, contact conduction,
  boundary temperatures and thermal lift;
- `experiments/planck-smoke.js`: separate settled-contact cost control;
- `experiments/logs/0.5.0-M0-planck-{look,smoke}.json` and `0.5.0-M0-planck-look.svg`.

Warm SP timings report best and mean of three batches plus per-frame p50/p95. The long

## Like-for-like behavior controls

### Contact is not the mantle default

The contact control changes only `mode` and runs to 100 Myr. It remains finite and its
books close, but at 100 Myr it reads **498.076 km/Myr** and **9 wells**, outside both S1
fluid bands. Its long-leg mean is 6.75 ms/frame. Warm matched rows below do make contact
cheaper than PBF; that cost advantage does not repair its behavior. The release plan
therefore no longer requires S1 from contact-mode mantle and no longer lists contact as
the first performance fallback.

### Carried circulation and mobility

These are deliberately labelled **50 Myr screening controls**, with six samples from 25
through 50 Myr. They are not 300 Myr acceptance legs.

| control | mean ms/frame | median peak vertical km/Myr | median wells | cell-band occupancy | final k | final max density residual |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| selected candidate | 5.620 | 61.427 | 6.5 | 50.0% | 1 | 0.09199 |
| `inertia=0` | 13.597 | 91.861 | 8 | 33.3% | 4 | 0.17511 |
| `mobContrast=4` | 13.181 | 195.730 | 5 | 100.0% | 3 | 0.05300 |

The no-inertia control is more expensive, has more cells and ends above the 10% density
reading used at the long checkpoints; it does not justify an overdamped SP default. The
original mobility setting also costs more in this window and ends at 262.748 km/Myr, but
its 25–50 Myr distribution is not a long-run failure. `mobContrast=0` is selected because
it belongs to the proven 300 Myr candidate and has the lower screening cost; mobility 4
may be revisited only with another full distribution run. Likewise, the candidate's own
50 Myr row is not claimed to pass the 100–300 Myr acceptance window.

Absolute-temperature and fixed-geotherm buoyancy gauges, clipping and anomaly smoothing
were explored during prototyping but are not comparative rows in this final bank. No
numerical claims about those exploratory runs are needed for this report. The selected
row-mean, unsmoothed, unclipped candidate is fully reproduced by the command above.

## Equal-population cost, not equal-`nx` cost

The first harness layout used the same `nx` in both spaces; that was not a valid cost
comparison because native row counts differed. The final bank uses exact populations and
native, near-square lattices in each space:

| population | screen lattice | km lattice |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 6,426 | 126 x 51 | 189 x 34 |
| 28,728 | 266 x 108 | 399 x 72 |

Uniform-size mean costs:

| population | PBF screen | PBF km | contact screen | contact km |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 6,426 | 5.314 | 4.440 | 3.785 | 3.213 |
| 28,728 | 21.622 | 19.984 | 16.177 | 14.749 |

KM is about 8–16% cheaper in these warm rows. Screen remains the default for display
geometry, not for a supposed speed advantage. Contact's lower price does not override
the behavior control above.

### Size policies and the mass book

Mean PBF costs for all three policies:

| population | space | uniform | material | heat-sized |
|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 6,426 | screen | 5.314 | 5.370 | 5.137 |
| 6,426 | km | 4.440 | 4.517 | 4.191 |
| 28,728 | screen | 21.622 | 25.094 | 21.307 |
| 28,728 | km | 19.984 | 19.755 | 20.228 |

The prototype's material policy makes every eighth particle half-diameter; heat sizing
shrinks by as much as one half from temperature. Neither edits material mass. A 40-frame
heat-size leg preserves every mass entry bitwise. Heat sizing is not inherently slower in
this CPU kernel, while material sizing is slower on the large screen row. These numbers
do not measure melt through cracks, so `sizeByHeat` stays off until M5's behavior leg.
No GPU claim is inferred from this table.

## Heat, stability, determinism and tunnelling

At 500 kyr/frame, with drive disabled for the one-step heat control:

| scheme | whole-step ms | heat passes | absolute heat gap | stock-relative | wall-relative |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| explicit | 28.003 | 8 | 4.972e-8 | 1.107e-15 | 3.757e-12 |
| analytic | 16.277 | 1 | 3.721e-8 | 8.282e-16 | 3.199e-12 |

Analytic exchange is the default: each pair is the exact exponential solution for two
heat capacities, each wall operation is an exponential relaxation, and pair direction
alternates. It is conservative and cannot overshoot for a large dt. It still has to pass
M4's `sp-conduct.js` profile fixture; conservation is not proof of the conduction profile.
Explicit heat remains available as a control, but its global CFL count can become
impractical for compressed pairs.

The 500-kyr stability bank covers 24 combinations: two native size rungs, both spaces,
both modes and all three size policies, 30 frames per combination. Every row stays finite,
contained, inside temperature bounds and closed in mass and heat. Final motion k is 3–6
on screen rows and 1 on km rows; these are final readings, not claimed maxima. Across the
matrix the worst stock-relative heat residual is 3.50e-14, the largest absolute gap is
1.57e-6, and the worst wall-relative residual is 9.98e-9. Mass residual is zero. Reporting
all three heat normalisations is intentional: a small relative residual can correspond to
a much larger absolute number in the km-space heat stock.

Two further correctness fixtures are explicit:

- same seed and replay are bitwise identical over 40 frames;
- an unclamped two-disc position pass **can tunnel**: it crosses in one substep and ends
  four diameters apart on the wrong sides. With cap 0.35 it takes 12 substeps, does not
  cross, and finishes one diameter apart. Position-based dynamics alone is therefore not
  a tunnelling guarantee.

## Planck: real thermal reference and cost control

`planck-look.js` is not the old falling-disc timing fixture. It creates four walls,
persistent contacts, equal-capacity contact conduction, hot-bottom/cold-top relaxation
and temperature-dependent lift. It writes a temperature-coloured SVG and checks finite
state, containment and sign-changing depth-average circulation. SP rows below use the
same body counts and nearly the same box aspect.

| bodies | SP best / mean ms | planck best / mean ms | planck wells | planck non-finite / escaped |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 403 | 0.27 / 0.27 | 1.33 / 1.37 | 4 | 0 / 0 |
| 1,000 | 0.67 / 0.68 | 3.68 / 3.86 | 13 | 0 / 0 |
| 1,960 | 1.36 / 1.37 | 10.98 / 12.74 | 16 | 0 / 0 |

This is a per-step cost comparison, not a physical-time or S1 equivalence claim: the two
fixtures advance different clocks and planck velocities use fixture units. The planck
image is more lattice-like than the selected SP image, but the quantitative look gate is
only its own circulation diagnostic. The custom solver wins the matched cost rows by
about 5–9x, so the Box2D default-flip rule is not triggered.

The separate no-sleep settled-contact control gives:

| bodies | velocity / position iterations | best ms/step | mean ms/step | non-finite / escaped |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 400 | 8 / 3 | 1.14 | 1.24 | 0 / 0 |
| 1,000 | 8 / 3 | 4.60 | 8.55 | 0 / 0 |

| 4,000 | 4 / 2 | 42.35 | 70.83 | 0 / 0 |

The large rows are noisy, so both best and mean are retained and no cause is invented
for the spread. Reducing iterations was not a cheap win in this run. The superseded
wall-free, sleeping-body timings are not used anywhere in the amended plan.

## What remains open

1. M1 shared substrate and both page skeletons; registry/page parity includes the amended
   `inertia`, `mobContrast` and `heatMode` entries.
2. M4 must implement the production SP engine, pass metric and conduction-profile gates,
   and run full S1 with default PBF in **both** coordinate spaces, including the lid
   requirement once crust exists.
3. M5 owns bonds, melt, eruption, unpressurize, granular contact behavior and the
   `sizeByHeat` crack-flow decision. S2–S4 are not attempted by this prototype.
4. Integrated page-frame and real rAF costs, option-matrix checks and browser smoke are
   later milestones. No rendering or GUI budget is claimed here.
5. The 26k/100k rows do not have 300 Myr behavior acceptance. Mobility 4 and any one-sweep
   fallback also require a full rerun before their defaults can change.

M0 is complete as a kernel bake-off. It does not justify skipping those gates.
