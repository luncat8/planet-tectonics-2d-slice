# 0.3.2 M6 — the bound against the world, and the repair join: the seventh was one world's variance

M5 §7 leaves two questions that one world per row cannot answer. The deposit-scoped bound removes
the tail and keeps the class at what looked like a seventh of the control's deposit→melt flux —
but the control's 9.7302 km² is dominated by one 7.4359 km² event, so the ratio is n = 1. And the
held-back concentration (68,140 km²·Myr on the quoted cell) had never been crossed with the mover
M5 walked into: the mesh repair relocates markers with their heat intact, so part of that
concentration might be coverage repair rather than melt routing. M6 runs the distribution and
builds the join. Both answers change what the row's rejection was about.

Banks: `experiments/logs/0.3.2-M6-{base,dep}-s{5,1,3,7}-600.json` (eight worlds, one bank each).
Specification: `0.3.2-plan.md` §M6. Assertions: the M6 section of
`experiments/pt-heat-tail-check.js`, which binds every number below to its own bank.

The ratio is not a constant of the bound. It spans **0.085 to 3.20** across four worlds, and the
upper end is an inversion: on seed 3 the candidate delivers over three times the control's flux,
eleven field-supported transfers against four. Seed 5 — the world the whole family was read on —
is a quiet control (9.73 km² against 35.9 to 142.7 on the others) whose flux is one event's; the
"seventh" M5 measured is that event's shadow. By the plan's own decision rule, **the row's
standing is open again**: the thinning was the world's, not the bound's.

The worlds also show the candidate is not a starved pipeline in general: on s1 it delivers a
152.73 km² host — larger than anything its control delivers — with 25 transfers and zero
refusals, and on s3 it runs the channel hotter than the control. Where the row removes flux (s5,
s7) it removes it the way M5 described; where it does not (s1, s3) the ordinary pipeline is
simply having a busy 600 Myr.

## 2. What every world agrees on: the defect is absent

The tail is the release's defect, and it is gone on all four worlds. The control's deposit phase
reaches T 1.6072 to 2.3128 and crosses the engine's own wall at **both** ends on every seed
(443–2491 samples below −0.5, 46–1112 above 1.5); the candidate's deposit phase never exceeds
**1.1136 to 1.1150** — the bound's own ceiling — with **zero** samples outside on every world. The
worst held T−field gap follows: 0.2503–0.2525, the bound's own width, against 1.1724–1.9945. The
held-back concentration falls on every world (694.7/68140.1, 19293.6/39009.4, 3983.2/18423.9,
1104.5/43694.6 km²·Myr) — 2× to 98×, world-variable like the flux, always down. Books, the
latent identity and the intake wall close on every frame of every cell; refusals are zero
throughout; the surface pipeline is alive in both rows on every seed.

The temperature gate read across *all* phases is red in both rows on three of four worlds, and the
split says why: the candidate's residual crossings are the **mantle cold side** (273–1815 samples
at T to −0.897) and the **air hot side** (229–488 samples at T to 1.595) — phases the
deposit-scoped bound does not touch, by design. Those are the M3 cold face and the flight face,
not this release's deposit defect; the gate as written is joint, the bound's scope is not. A
selection will have to state which reading it commits to.

## 3. The repair join: the held census is all relocated material, but mostly settled

The join records each material id's last relocation — a position diff across `G.reseed`, the only
mover that replaces a marker's position out of step with the flow, verified against the kernel's
own count (`movedSeen === movedSum` in all eight banks) — and ages every held routing sample
against it. The wrap is observation only: the seed-5 pair reproduces its M5 fingerprints exactly
(6191250240167390, 1808048552219233) with every banked diagnostic unchanged.

The first fact is structural: **never = 0 in all eight cells**. Every held routing sample of every
world sits on material the repair had moved at least once — 30,906 samples over 2,786 relocated
ids on the seed-5 control, 13,234 relocations in all. Holding is a long residence at the routing
threshold waiting for a field that does not arrive, and a long residence in a convecting mesh is
never left alone by the repair. The recent share is a different question, and it does not support
the reclassification M5 §7 named:

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| base-s5 | 68140.1 | 33.3% | 35.0% | 65.0% | yes |
| dep-s5 | 694.7 | 1.1% | 6.3% | 93.7% | yes |
| base-s1 | 39009.4 | 2.0% | 4.4% | 95.6% | yes |
| dep-s1 | 19293.6 | 15.2% | 16.4% | 83.6% | yes |

| dep-s3 | 3983.2 | 0.1% | 7.2% | 92.8% | yes |
| base-s7 | 43694.6 | 9.6% | 10.7% | 89.3% | no |

On the quoted world the within-one-frame share is 22,713.6 of 68,140.1 km²·Myr — a third, and it
is the population version of the M5 trace's one parcel (its widest gap three frames after a drop).
But the majority of the concentration — 65% on that cell, and never below 64% on any of the eight —
sits on material the repair had not touched in over a hundred frames, and on every world but s7's
control the **worst** held gap is settled material, not a fresh drop. So the concentration is
predominantly melt routing as the family has always read it, with a real but minority
repair-adjacent share that is world-variable (0.1–33%) and that a bound on deliveries does not
address. A bound on deliveries is **not** the wrong instrument for the majority; the repair's own
heat side-effect — non-local, unbooked, large per parcel — stays on the open list at its measured
minority weight.

The delivered side carries the same age and reads the same way. The seed-5 control's three
protected transfers were relocated 22, 36 and 14 frames before delivery — close enough to matter
to any future per-parcel reading — and its T 2.267 outlier id sat 205 frames from its last
relocation. The candidate's two: one 211 frames settled, one delivered a single frame after a
relocation. On s1 the candidate's 152.73 km² host went 3,076 frames after its last move. The
relocation age of a transfer is not a property of the row.

## 4. Timing, and what it is not

Both rows read ~30–35% above their M5 serial readings on this host: the controls 14.90–15.57 ms/frame
against 11.696 banked in M3, the candidates 21.35–21.80 against 15.768 banked in M5 — a
multiplicative shift on both rows that is host drift, not the instrument (the M5 trace, priced
against its own untraced twin, cost 0.004 ms/frame). The probe's own overhead is stable across
worlds: +6.1 to +6.6 ms over the matched control. The frame-time gate (< 20 ms) is red for the
candidate on this host and was green for the same cell on the M5 host; timing stays host-specific,
and the gate crossing here is the host's as much as the row's. The join's own cost is inside the
noise: it is a position diff per `G.reseed` call plus one age lookup per held sample, and the four
controls agree with each other to 5% while sitting 30% above the M3 host.

## 5. What M6 says

- **The flux objection is dissolved.** The candidate's deposit→melt cost is world variance, not a
  property of the bound: 0.085× to 3.201× across four worlds, including an inversion. The
  seed-5 "seventh" was one large event in one quiet world. By M5 §7's own rule the row's standing
  is open again.
- **The defect's removal is world-robust.** Deposit phase inside 1.1150 on every seed where every
  control leaves the wall at both ends; held gap the bound's own width; the concentration down
  everywhere; zero refusals, closed books, a live pipeline everywhere. This is the family's
  success measured at n = 4, not n = 1.
- **The held concentration is not coverage repair.** All of it sits on relocated material, but
  most of it sits on settled material, and the worst gaps mostly follow the settled population.
  The repair-adjacent minority (0.1–33%) is real and stays measured; it does not reclassify the
  majority.

What a selection still owes, in order: condition 5's material branch **beyond seed 5** — the
correspondence argument M5 gave on one world has to hold on the worlds the distribution adds,
above all s3 where the channel runs 3.2× hot and s1 where the candidate delivers a host bigger
than any its control does; the temperature gate's reading, joint across phases or scoped to the
bound's own phase, stated once and applied to every row; and a frame time quoted from a quiet
host, as M5 §6 already required. No constant moves on this milestone's numbers either.
