# Milestone M5 Worklog (ages and the observation log): the return path, closed

**Milestone:** 0.4.1 §4.3.2's dating convention made normative, §8.1's staleness rule given an
owner, and §8.5's observation log shipped — `js/core-log.js`, the D4 manual rung of the return
path, and the refusals around them. The step series: `0.4.1-m5-worklog.md` (step 1, the
envelope), `0.4.1-m5-step2-report.md` (step 2, the reconcile), `0.4.1-m5-correction-link-worklog.md`
(C1–C3, the ladder). Witnesses: `experiments/core-log.js` (33 checks, new),
`experiments/coupling.js` (107), `experiments/section-pack.js` (81), plus the twelve gates that
had to stay green.

## 1. What the state of the branch asked for

Three half-doors. D4 (the manual rung) existed only as an inbound paste: `outMsg()` built a log
that was nothing, and the link called `onOutbound` on a rung that has nowhere to send. Bed ages
had two conventions in the engine and the plan (§4.3.2) named the seam loosely: a viewer could
read `layAg` as a rock age or a clock reading, and `render.js` printed either as Myr. And the
section, once pinned by a clock, could be pinned forever: a globe that stops emitting coupling
snapshots but keeps its clock ticking kept the section's clock at 0 Myr and its K2 prescribed
forever. The plan also left §8.5 as four bullets — no field table, no acceptance rule, no owner
for any of it.

## 2. The dating law, and the column that argued with it

The convention is now one sentence: **`layAg` is the bed's formation time on the section's
clock**; rock age is derived (`t − layAg`) and the globe's `ageMyr` never enters a bed. The
strata law that follows — formation time never decreases upward across a depositional contact —
was tested against the live column array immediately, and column 437 refused it.

The first diagnosis (blaming the writer that "bypasses" the rule) was wrong. What was wrong was
the validator's exemption: the flag means *this bed was placed inside the stack*, and an
inversion is legal at a contact when **either side** carries it — the intruder below an older
ceiling is the same event as the ceiling above it, and only one of the two is the bed the engine
touched. `strataOK`, `validate` and the record rule all read the either-side form; the
`FLAG.intr` comment in `js/params.js` was rewritten to say so. Growth at the base of a lithology,
sills, belt transfers and collapse donors — every engine path that thickens crust without
surfacing it — carry the flag, which is why the same check also proves the engine's own honesty:
after 20 imports of evolution every column passes the §8.5 rule as written.

## 3. An import never stamps a bed into the past

The coarse-reconcile path grew beds at `msg.tMyr` while `freshStack` grew them at `tBed` — two
dates, and a lead-pinned section (clock ahead of an old message, which §8.3's cadence rule
creates by design) could import beds younger than the beds above them. Now one rule reaches all
five writers, seeded and imported alike: `tBed = max(msg.tMyr, opts.tNow)`, `tNow` passed from
the page's `SIM.t` (the harness rung passes none, so a test clock stays exact). Measured: a
message at t 120.5 applied while the section reads 127.75 dates its new beds at 127.75. The
rebuilt-column check then failed for a while — its oracle indexed the table by column
(`rows2[jj]`); the join maps columns to intervals, so the row that rebuilt a column is whatever
`joinTo`/wrap says, and that oracle is now written from `joinTo` itself, which also ties the
rebuilt-column count exactly to the ledger (`seen === COUP.last.freshColumns`).

A trap the floor check set: a zero-delta message writes no beds — the dating path never runs —
so any age test must carry a real thickness delta. The harness's first version asserted "every
new bed at 127.75" against a delta of 0 and passed nothing; it now grows 200 m of felsic.

## 4. Zero-thickness holes: the log is a record of rock, not of slots

The section-pack D4 run then refused its own log: `record 5 bed 1 is not thicker than zero`. It
was not quantization (`port/slice-format.js` `round6` went `toFixed(9)` → `toPrecision(6)`
mid-debug and the diagnosis held: tiny positives like 2.5e-13 survive; only non-finite values
need the guard, and `quantize` now routes them through a `q()` that turns them into NaN, which
the field check refuses with the offending value printed). It was `js/crust.js` `delaminate`: a
consumed bed is kept as a `layTh = 0` slot so same-lithology refuel can re-inflate it. That is
engine representation, not rock. The asymmetry the reader takes: **`build` skips zero beds and
skips columns of only holes** (and the `strataOK` walk over live arrays skips them too, stepping
to the previous real bed), while **`validate` still refuses a zero bed inside a foreign record**
— a log that carries one did not come from this engine. `quantize` also re-sums `hTotM` as the
sum of the *rounded* beds, so a record never fails its own rule by rounding order.

## 5. D4 and the staleness demotion

Manual-rung outbound is now placement: `pasteText` places in the textarea and reports
`clipboard: placed`; nothing was sent, and the HUD message says which of the two it was
(`core log: 510 records · 48.8 KB · placed · out field ready to copy`). The auto-log fires at
the moment a message is accepted — stale-snapshot re-arm and the routine cadence alike — with
`tNow` as the dating floor; a zero-delta accept logs nothing new and the page stays honest
about that. The out field always holds the last built log, sent or placed.

Staleness got its second half: the pin counts the section's own waiting frames (240 by default)
and then demotes — `COUP.activate(null)`, K2 released, `unsynced` on the banner, ledger kept.
Only an accepted coupling snapshot re-arms; a clock message proves the link lives, not that the
kinematics are fresh.

## 6. The format, as shipped

`pgt-core-log` v1: `{format, version, pathChecksum, packChecksum, arcKm, tMyr, epochMa,
records[{sKm, div, hTotM, beds[[lith, thM, formedMyr, flags]]}], checksum}` — six significant
digits, canonical order, the same two-lane FNV as the other two envelopes; the checksum is
mandatory on the wire. One record per logged column ascending by arc; `div` is the column's own
fraction (`S.colDiv`, not the section average — the average made every column move whenever one
did: 512/512 against 43/512 at threshold 1e-3 after the fix, which is the whole idea of the
cadence rule). Cadence build, commit at emission, not at build — a dropped send leaves the same
view owed next time, asserted with a double build that returns the identical view; `CLOG.reset()`
on a new cut; 510 records and 48.8 KB for a full pinned L5 log; determinism checksum
`cb2fcbc73ca50f06`, repeated build noted-by-nobody equal; the state-hash test (build, commit,
reset never move `S.hash()`) and fourteen named refusals (foreign format, stale checksum,
off-cut record, age inversion without a flag — and legal once flagged, empty set, bad lith,
thick-but-zero-sum, off-arc mid, unknown flag bit, and so on). `lastEmitted` moved onto the
module object because `experiments/lib.js` freezes its modules.

## 7. Gates, and one that was fake

`node experiments/core-log.js` 33 ✓ · coupling 107 ✓ · section-pack 81 ✓ · coupling-link 11 ✓ ·
core-log's own engine untouched by observation ✓ · smoke 66 ✓ · checkpoint 50 ✓ · section-seed
36 ✓ · m2-check 241 ✓ · view-check 119 ✓ · ledger-check 19 ✓ · m3-check 22 ✓ · deposits 76 ✓ ·
pt-ui 37 ✓ · slice-cut 63 ✓ — all re-run at the final tree, logs in `experiments/logs/`.

One gate lesson worth more than the rest: the coupling suite briefly printed **ALL PASS (134
checks)** while being completely fake — a check in it required `./core-log.js`, which from
`experiments/` is the core-log harness: it shared `check.js` (its 33 checks inflated the count
mid-run), printed its own `ALL PASS`, and its `process.exit(0)` ended the coupling process
before the remaining sections ran. Green output was indistinguishable. The fixed check requires
`../js/core-log.js` (like every other harness loads the codec, via `lib.mods`), and 134 became
107 — six checks that had silently never run, now run and pass. Check counts must match the
suite, not just the mood.

## 8. Deliberately left

- **The sea datum does not follow a message** (`wet` is hardcoded `z < 0` in `js/surface.js`;
  a `sea.levelM` change would silently misclassify every coastline — named in the link notice).
- **New-boundary spawning** stays the engine's (§8.1 table row unchanged; the import moves
  edges/polarities, ribbons live or die on their own).
- **`SectionPack.clear()` does not reset the log side table** — the reset belongs to a new cut,
  and a cleared page that reloads the same pack wants its `sentEver` back; §8.5 records the
  rule, M6's page state can bind them.
- The import-cost number (§9.5's budget) is 2.3 ms mean / 4.6 ms worst per hard-reset import at
  L5; the plan marks the cadence decision settled at N = 5 Myr.
