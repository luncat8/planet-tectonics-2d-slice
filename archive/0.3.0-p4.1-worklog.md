## The three rules

**Background.** A slab is dense against *something*, and the engine's own buoyancy law
(`grid.js` `stokes`) reads a gradient of the node temperature field, so the proxy has to be the
same field. `Trow[j]` is the mean of row `j`, and the contrast is `Trow[j] − T[p]`. A drive that
read the marker's own node would read the marker's own scattered temperature back and find no
contrast at all. Rows 1..`ny` are filled, including `ny`: `Math.round(ny − 0.5) === ny` in
JavaScript, so a marker at `eMax` maps to the bottom row, and the background there is the CMB
wall — a slab against it is as dense as it can be.

**Sink.** A parcel below `P.yCrust` descends at `kSink · contrast`, capped at `vSinkMax`. It is
a position move, not a velocity: a marker's `S.vy` is the flow's and the next frame's rigid fit
reads it, so writing a sink speed into it would hand the whole plate the slab's descent. The move
ends the way every move in this engine ends — eta rebuilt from y, then `G.keepInside`.

**Recycle.** Below `P.yDeep` the parcel leaves the pool for a deep record: mass, enthalpy, melt
and composition debit the parcel and credit the record, the record's position is the
mass-weighted mean of what it has eaten, arrivals within `deepJoin` share a record, and a full
record set defers (`tx.refuse++`, the parcel waits at the boundary) instead of dropping mass.

## The population is the whole design, and it was measured twice

The first implementation transferred **any** solid parcel below `yDeep`, on the reasoning that
the boundary is a place. `experiments/scratch/p4-deep-flux.js` ran it for 300 Myr:

```
t 50 Myr  driven/frame 6   recycled 3543785 km2 in 18 records
t 300 Myr driven/frame 2513  recycled 11348596 km2 in 21 records (24% of the world's stock)
```

What is below `yDeep` on a live planet is the hot deep mantle, not a slab. The transfer moved
inside the population the pass owns — cold, below the crust base, detached — and the drain became
a flux. But the contrast test alone was still too wide, and the second measurement
(`experiments/scratch/p4-slabT.js`, 300 Myr per row, everything else shipped) is what fixed it:

| population | driven-parcel-frames | worst frame | mean descent | transfers | records (% of world) | pool |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| contrast only | 10,866,203 | 3,887 | 15.57 km/Myr | 8,159 | 4,035,256 km² (**9.68%**) | 23,552 → **17,367** |
| **contrast and `T ≤ 0.45`** | **1,060,438** | **509** | **29.98 km/Myr** | **439** | **86,273 km² (0.21%)** | 23,552 → **25,017** |
| contrast and `T ≤ 0.35` | 405,929 | 262 | 30.00 km/Myr | 48 | 7,978 km² (0.02%) | 23,552 → 25,435 |

The contrast test selects the flow's whole cold half: at 150 Myr, 24% of the detached material
below `yCrust` is colder than its row by `slabdT`, and 83% of it is inside 0.05 of the row mean
(`experiments/scratch/p4-contrast.js`). The ambient cold boundary circulation crosses `yDeep`
too, and a transfer is one-way, so the world was being drained by a quarter and the pool's
resolution with it.

`P.slabT` is the discriminator the engine already had a constant for: **`TSoft` (0.45), the line
above which `strength()` stops letting rock age** — where rock stops being cold lithosphere. With
it the pass drives 0.71% of the pool per frame at the cap (3 cm/yr, a slab's own speed), books
439 transfers over 300 Myr, parks 0.21% of the world's mass in the records, and the pool *grows*.
`TLock` (0.35) starves it: 48 transfers, 0.02%.

Also measured and rejected: the engine's lithosphere memory `S.age` as a provenance tag. It looks
like the right discriminator — age grows only while a parcel is at or below `TLock` — but
`strength()` resets it above `yWeldMax` (300 km), so at depth every parcel reads 0 and nothing is
ever a slab (`experiments/scratch/p4-age-crit.js`: 11–41 parcels at a mean depth of 270–278 km on
every mark from 150 to 300 Myr, never one below 300 km).

## The exclusions, and why they are tested by hand

Three things must not move: the lid above `yCrust` (P4.0's scope, and P2.1's relief reads it),
warm material at depth (the drive answers a density anomaly, not a depth), and lithosphere still
riding a plate (a plate's rigid fit is the motion this pass must not fight — pulling a root down
from under its own plate would fight the projection the plate rides on). A leg cannot say which
rule kept a parcel where it was, so the fixture calls `SLAB.sink` by hand on one state and reads
all three populations:

- 9,508 parcels above the scope line, **0 moved**;
- 749 parcels in plate-sized clusters below it, **0 moved**, and the pass reported all 749 as
  `d.slabBond`;
- **100 of 13,295** detached parcels below the line moved — exactly the seeded 100-parcel blob,
  at 0.60 cm/yr, which is `kSink · 0.1` for a blob 0.1 colder than an isothermal planet. The
  other 13,195 detached parcels are at the background and the pass leaves them alone.

The state is the shared two-plate fixture with `yCrust` lowered to 150 km, because on the shipped
planet a plate's root and the scope line are the same 250 km: attached lithosphere below the line
only exists if the line moves.

On the live planet the exclusion never fires — **395 attached-parcel-frames against 1,060,438
driven over 6,000 frames** — which *is* the plan's §4 answer: below `yCrust` the shipped planet
has no plate-sized cluster, because a plate's root is `yCrust`, so every cold parcel the pass
finds there is detached and keeps sinking. The exclusion is not decoration; it is what a leg with
a deep-rooted plate would hit, and the hand call above is where it is proven.

## Two things the fixture had to accept about the planet it runs on

**A seeded cold blob does not survive a frame.** At 2,200 km a mesh cell is ~450 km tall, so the
heat pass equilibrates a 200 km body with the cell it sits in. The first version of the arrival
leg seeded at t = 0 and reported 0 transfers of 30 parcels; worse, the tagged material *rose*
620 km in 14 Myr, because the start-up transient of the shipped initial condition advects the
deep mantle upward at ~44 km/Myr — more than the drive's 30 km/Myr cap. So the controlled legs
settle the fields the pass reads with one real frame (pass off), seed afterwards, and rebuild
temperature, node field and background without stepping; and the arrival leg starts after 150 Myr
and holds its slab's temperature each frame. Holding a temperature prescribes enthalpy, so that
leg gates mass — the field a transfer has to conserve — and reports the enthalpy it wrote.

**Water is not a spectator at depth.** `meltWW` is 20 per unit of `cW`, so a slab seeded with
`cW = 0.05` at 2,450 km is fully molten by its own phase law (`PHASE.byT` gives melt 1.0000) and
is not a slab at all. The fixture seeds `cW = 0.005`, which carries composition and stays rock.
The same law is why the record's melt is 0.00e+0: a slab that reaches this depth is cold, so what
P4.2 needs from the record is its water, not its melt.

**A prescription writes enthalpy, not temperature.** `L.setT` writes `S.H`, and every kernel
that reads temperature reads `S.T` or the mesh field, which only G4's equilibrium mapping and
G1's scatter refresh. `twoPlates()` now ends with `PHASE.equil` + `G.scatterT`, because without
them the first frame of every leg scatters the initial condition's hot interior into the mesh and
the contact pass sees no contrast at the trench — one frame, but the frame the leg's numbers
start from. Correcting it moved the controlled legs' worst closing from 1.446/1.447 to
1.440/1.448 /Myr and the subduction leg's shortening from 1,084 to 1,089 km, and nothing else.

The ledger gate is the one that matters most and it did not move at all: `intake/wall` 1.024, the
same number to the last digit, on a planet that now transfers 86,272.6 km² out of the pool and
into the deep records — because both inventories count the records, so a transfer is internal to
the stock. The wrap and two-clock identities still hold bitwise, which is what a pass that moves
positions in y and eta only should do.

The four timing-bearing harnesses (`pt-check`, `pt-p3-enthalpy`, `pt-p3-budget`, `pt-p3-check`)
were re-run serially after the set: the first pass ran the suite as two parallel chains, and
contention inflated their ms/frame lines by 4–12% (10.8 → 10.4, 11.14 → 10.73, 12.36 → 10.98,
12.73 → 11.68). The logs and the table above hold the serial numbers. The frame moved ~0.5–1.0 ms
against P4.0 while the pass itself costs 0.114 ms/frame measured alone, so the rest is a planet
whose deep material now moves — a different cluster and pool history, not a slower kernel — and
every budget gate keeps at least 40% headroom.
