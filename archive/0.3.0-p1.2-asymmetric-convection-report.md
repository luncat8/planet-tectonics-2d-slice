# 0.3.0 P1.2 — upwelling contrast and shallow melt extraction

## Findings

The P1 fluid is a one-phase, nearly constant-viscosity convection model. Its cold downwellings
are not a sign error: they are a normal Rayleigh–Bénard return flow. The request is about the
look and the missing phase contrast, so this pass adds a deliberately modest reduced-order
asymmetry rather than claiming to simulate all mantle rheology or magma dynamics.

The intermittent yellow streaks at the surface were consistent with the `melt*` diagnostic,
not a grid-gap color: its pressure factor was clamped to full strength above the 80 km horizon,
so the non-conserved overlay could remain painted up to the surface wall.

## Changes

- `upwellBuoyancyBoost` slightly increases buoyancy for hot anomalies relative to their row
  mean, a low-viscosity plume proxy. The flow remains divergence-free and scales linearly with
  Rayleigh number.
- `downwellMix` adds conservative, cold-anomaly-weighted lateral flux. It broadens narrow
  cold fingers without changing a row's heat integral; it does not weaken or reverse the
  physical downward flow.
- Melt segregation was raised to 400 km/Myr. The diagnostic is extracted at the shallow
  80 km cap, removing the surface-wall yellow streaks instead of presenting an indicator as
  surface magma. Conserved magma and surface accumulation remain future work.
- Added `experiments/pt-asymmetry-check.js` for the buoyancy, mixing, and row-integral
  invariants; updated the melt fixture for the extraction cap.
