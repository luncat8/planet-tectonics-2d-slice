# 0.1.5 worklog — contact mechanics

Record of what was implemented against `0.1.5-plan.md`, what it measures, and what is
still open. The plan is the artifact; this is the log.

## 1. Phase log

### M0 — the audit as a gate

`experiments/contact-audit.js [frames] [seed] [kyrPerFrame] --strict` measures on a
2048-point raster of the drawn surface and gates §1 of the plan. Nine checks, exit 1 on
a violation.

The record matching across frames is the part that took the work. A frame's worst `dh`
is only meaningful if the record it happened to is the same record, and with births,
deaths, draining records and re-partitioned territories a naive nearest-position match
pairs a column at 4700 km with one at 3100 km and reports a 38 km jump that never
happened. The audit matches by position *and* width, matches a record that turned
draining to the record it replaced, and reports `unmatched` and `rematched` counts so
a reader can see how much of the quiet-frame worst is real. With that in place the
event sites move 0 m: the plan's R1 is met by construction, and what is left to measure
is the quiet background.

### M1 — quiet topology events

**(a) Consumption converts.** `COL.consume` transfers the loser's stack to the ribbon
and then leaves the record: empty, `colGhost`, on the overriding plate, in place.
Deleting it re-partitioned a full column of territory between the two neighbours in one
frame — 20–35% of their crust, a multi-kilometre pop for both.

The record is *drawn* as the line its two real neighbours already make across it
(`SURF.morph`), so neither the consumption frame nor the sliver's life moves the
section. Runs are walked whole, not one column at a time, so a chain of slivers is
pinned to the two margins that bracket it. One exemption: a sliver that has just been
drained walks onto the line over `P.tauSliver` (4 Myr) instead of in one frame — it
was standing on its own ground, a trench several km below that line, and the snap is a
5 km step in one frame (measured: 5186 m at frame 4983 on seed 5, where a 15 km
oceanic record was consumed under a 49 km continental margin).

Retirement is geometry, not a classifier state: a sliver whose two gaps are both on
`P.gFloor * w0` is holding territory worth nothing to the picture, and handing it back
moves each neighbour by a few per cent of its own width.

**(b) The floor.** `COL.floor` runs at the end of K3 transport and is a *plate*
correction: each plate is displaced rigidly by the largest overlap it holds, half each
way. Three passes, as first written, left a C–C pair 7.5% inside the floor after
500 Myr on seed 5, and the first attempt at fixing it (repeat until no plate moved)
exits early on a plate held between two equal overlaps — every plate's left and right
corrections cancel, nothing moves, and two pairs are still inside the floor. Repeating
until no *pair* is inside the floor converges:

| passes | floor kernel | worst deficit | of the floor |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | 23 us/call | 293.1 m | 7.50% |
| 4 | 28 us | 163.2 m | 4.18% |
| 6 | 43 us | 69.6 m | 1.78% |
| 8 | 54 us | 32.5 m | 0.83% |
| 12 | 77 us | 7.4 m | 0.19% |
| 16 | 107 us | 1.9 m | 0.05% |

A ring of contacts can over-constrain a rigid correction — a plate held between two
equal overlaps has nowhere to move — so the floor is a bound and not an equality.
`P.floorPass` is 16 and `P.floorTol` (1% of `gFloor`) is what the check allows: 20x
headroom over the 0.05% the iteration actually reaches, and 7.5x tighter than the
three passes it replaced. The whole kernel is 0.10 ms of a 1.9 ms frame, and it stops
early when nothing is inside the floor.

This also removed a raster artefact: with pairs 7% inside the floor, two records 6 km
apart could carry a 5.2 km elevation step, and a 19.6 km raster sample landing on that
step read 10.8 km of movement in one frame. The quiet-frame worst is 0.2–2.3 km across
the matrix now.

**(c) Sustained state and a real speed.** Both topology intents in `COL.intents` are
gated on the classifier's own entry terms (`|edgeRelN| > P.epsHi`, `edgeAge >
P.evAge`), and a consuming pair must additionally be at the floor. A C–C edge consumes
nothing: the pair squeezes and sutures instead.

### M2 — collision builds a belt

No consumption at a C–C contact, `CRU.belt` flows the excess sideways once the pair is
a wedge (a root of `P.beltYield` before it flows, so the flow cannot flatten the
boundary until no belt is left), and every stack operation goes through
`COL.insertVol` at the bed's `P.LITH_RANK`. `CRU.delaminate` is the ceiling: a
recorded `ledCons` sink peeled from the base of the stack, never a clamp.

`P.LITH_RANK = [4, 2, 1, 3, 3, 0]` for `sed fel maf tephra lava sill`, so a stack runs
sill, mafic, felsic, volcanic, sediment from the bottom up. Sill below mafic basement
is the intrusive reading; the plan's first draft put sill with the surficial rocks and
was wrong.

### M3 — convergence is absorbed

Implemented as the geometric route, not the plate-speed limit the plan first wrote: the
belt widens, the ceiling caps the root, and a C–C edge slower than `P.vSuture` for
`P.evAge` sutures its two plates. A sutured pair is one plate and cannot close against
itself, so the arrest needs no second actuator in the plate solve.

### M4 — subduction side

`SLAB.appendStack` anchors the ribbon at the midpoint of the consumed pair's gap, the
consume intent requires the pair to be at the floor, and `SLAB.remove` keeps its
explicit `ledCons`.

### M5 — validation, tuning, docs

See §2 and §3.

## 2. The one defect the audit found in this plan

R6 held for every kernel except one path in K6, and only on a mixed frame rate.

**Symptom.** `experiments/m2-check.js` reported a per-lithology residual of 2.25e-3 on
sediment after 1000 frames at 100 kyr followed by 3000 at 200 kyr, with
`now = 4.511e10`, `ledCons = 2.088e10`, `ledMixIn = 3.292e10` and no sediment
`ledProd` or `ledMixOut`. A constant 100 kyr run of the same length closed to 1e-4 m³.
Raising `P.sedCap` from 4000 m to 1e9 made it disappear, which located it in the
basin spill.

**Cause.** `shed = hSed + th - P.sedCap` is bounded by the basin, not by the load. A
margin whose accretionary prism already stands over the capacity — subduction hands it
half of every sediment bed it consumes, and nothing takes it back — has
`hSed > P.sedCap`, so the spill exceeds the arriving thickness and K6 deposits a
**negative bed**. A negative bed is a hole in the stack: every later sum of that
column is short by it, and it is booked nowhere.

**Why it took 500 frames to show.** The hole is invisible while it sits in a stack. It
surfaces when the column is finally consumed, at which point `COL.consume` clears the
stack and the unbooked volume appears as a ledger error — three frames in 700 Myr, at
frames 3084, 3110 and 3520 of the reproduction. The frame-to-frame residual walk
(bisecting the per-kernel residual through K0–K9, then around the steps inside
`COL.consume`) is what turned "a ledger error at the end of a long run" into "one
negative bed, here, at 604 Myr".

**Fix.** The capacity limits what a basin *takes*, never more:
`shed = min(hSed + th - P.sedCap, th)`, with the surviving fraction of the arriving
load `1 - shed/th` (the first version squared it, which mis-scaled the placer and
ore bookkeeping), and no bed written when the whole load spills. Measured after the
fix on the same reproduction: no leaks, 0 negative beds, 15 of 497 columns over the
capacity with a maximum of 7.9 km (a subduction prism, which is what it is).

## 4. Open

- **The drawn profile across a near-coincident pair.** The raster samples the drawn
  surface by linear interpolation between record centres, so a steep section between two
  records a few km apart is a discontinuity for it. The floor change removed the worst
  instance; the sensitivity itself is still there and a raster denser than the record
  spacing, or a profile that interpolates in `u` rather than in `z`, would close it.
- **A prism that outgrows its capacity.** `COL.consume` hands the margin half of every
  sediment bed it consumes and `P.sedCap` never gives any of it back, so a margin that
  consumes a deep ocean floor ends up with 8 km of sediment drape (15 of 497 columns,
  maximum 7.9 km, mean 1.3 km). The spill is bounded now and the ledger is exact, but
  the *model* is wrong in the direction of unlimited accretion. The honest fix is for
  the prism to respect the same capacity and hand the rest to the slab, which is a
  change to subduction and not to a bug fix.
- **The `dh` identity artefact.** A single column's drawn thickness can change by tens
  of km in a frame that the audit calls quiet, when the record on one side of it is
  re-partitioned (the audit reports `rematched` counts of 3.6k–17k per run, and a
  41.4 km worst `dh` with a 9.6% worst `dw` on the same frame). The move is a
  redistribution between two records, not a change to the section, but the number is
  currently the largest thing in the report and deserves a first-class answer rather
  than a footnote in `rematched`.
- **M3 is geometric, not a speed limit.** It is the honest 1D reading, but a reviewer
  who wants `|relN|` at a mature C–C contact to decay as a *number* will not find it in
  a plate-speed term. The decay is there — through the belt width and the suture — and
  measuring it directly (a prescribed two-continent convergence, closing speed against
  belt width over 200 Myr) is the missing acceptance for R4.
