## 1. The decision: do not open the `COL.inherit` / `COL.accrete` R1 plan now

The suggested next step was checked against the saved logs
(`experiments/logs/0.1.13-quiet-identity-*.txt`, re-read this session), and its
numbers are correct:

| 5000-frame matrix, 32 worlds | count |
| --- | --- |
| worlds with `R1 > 1.25` | 16 of 32 |
| — loudest event is a birth (`COL.inherit`) | 10 |
| — loudest event is an accretion (`COL.accrete`) | 6 |
| — loudest event is a sliver retirement | **0** |
| over-budget worlds at 50 kyr/frame | 13 of 16 at that rate (3 of 16 pass) |
| over-budget worlds at 100 kyr/frame | 3 of 16 at that rate (13 of 16 pass) |

Those 16 worlds are **deferred, not blocking**, for three plain reasons:

1. **0.1.13 already met the acceptance it was opened under**
   (`0.1.13-plan.md` §5): the four strict legs pass 13/13, and across the
   32 worlds exactly what §5 asserts holds — 0 retirements over budget,
   `maxH <= 82.8 km`. The 32-world `R1 <= 1.25` figure was reported, never
   gated. Reopening it now would be moving the goalpost after the shot.
2. **`R1` is a loudest-event statistic, so it cannot be "finished".** It is
   (biggest one-frame surface move at any event) / (biggest move on any quiet
   frame). Something is always the loudest. 0.1.5–0.1.13 each removed the
   then-loudest event and promoted the next one; that is how a retirement, a
   birth and an accretion took turns at the top. The sliver retirement had to
   go because it was *fake* — a deleted record dumping territory and dropping
   the drawn surface ~5 km in a frame the audit called quiet. A birth and an
   accretion are real geology, seen by the audit, with the mass books closed.
   They are the section working, drawn in one frame.
3. **The 50 kyr concentration is largely the clock, not a new defect.** A birth
   or a merge is a one-frame topology change at any step size, while a quiet
   frame's surface move shrinks with `dt`. Halving the step roughly halves
   the denominator and leaves the numerator standing, so the same physics
   reads 13/16 over budget at 50 kyr and 3/16 at 100 kyr. Chasing it means
   ramping real events over frames until a ratio built from two maxima
   behaves — weeks of matrices to make a statistic quiet, on an engine the
   integration does not gate on.

If this is ever reopened, it is its own release with its own exit written
*before* the first run (for example: "birth and accretion sites at 50 kyr
read as 100 kyr sites do, with no strict leg, book or ceiling moving") — not
a continuation of 0.1.13, and not a precondition for anything in §3.

## 2. What this project is, in plain words

Two sibling projects draw the same planet two ways:

- **`planet-geotectonics` (the globe)** — the planet seen from above: plates
  moving on a sphere, real Earth data, deposits and prospecting. Mature.
- **This repository (the slice)** — one vertical cross-section through a
  planet: what the crust and mantle look like *under* a line, in depth,
  bed by bed. It has two engines: the older **column engine**
  (`columns.html`, `js/*.js`) whose geology the globe's design owns, and the
  newer **particle engine** (`index.html`, `js/pt/*`), a self-contained
  experiment in emergent plates.

The point of the slice is the end goal in `draft.txt` and
`0.9.0-draft-sync.md`: **draw a line on the globe, and watch that exact
line's cross-section run, live, beside the map** — the globe drives the
plates, the slice shows what is underneath.

The last fifteen sessions were not wandering. They were making the section
*trustworthy enough to be driven by somebody else's planet*: no fake
surface jumps (0.1.5–0.1.13), mass and heat books that close to zero
(0.3.1–0.3.2), a file format both repositories read byte-identically
(0.4.2), a way to cut a line on the globe and rebuild it here (0.4.1), and a
coupling message that slaves the section's plates to the globe and
reconciles its crust (0.4.1 §8, `js/coupling.js`, 138 checks green today).
The megabytes of logs are the receipts for those claims, and they are why a
second repository can be trusted with this one's output.

### The one open block, in plain words

Today, when a coupling message arrives, `COUP.apply` repaints the *edges*:
it puts the globe's boundary types on the nearest section seams and spawns a
slab ribbon for a new trench (`js/coupling.js`, `syncEdges`). But it never
changes **which plate each column belongs to** (`S.colPlate` is untouched by
an import). So if the globe's boundary has walked across ten columns since
the last message, the section draws the new boundary in the right place and
then drags those ten columns at their *old* plate's speed, because
`COUP.slave` averages the message's velocities per existing plate. The
picture is right for one frame and wrong from the next. That — not surface
smoothness — is what stands between "paste a cut and run it" (works today)
and "stay in sync with a running globe" (0.9).

## 4. The next job: 0.4.3 — rebuild plate membership at import (gate 4)

One job, on this side only, no globe changes, no live channel, no `port/`
touch. It reuses what exists: `COUP.apply` already walks every column's arc
midpoint against `msg.plates` (for `slave` and for the crust join), and
`SEED.plates` already turns runs of globe plate ids along a cut into the
section's local plates at seed time, including the `plateCap` merge and the
closed-cut wrap merge.

- **M0 — prove the stale membership, once.** One new section in the existing
  owner harness `experiments/coupling.js`: import a message, then import a
  second whose boundary has crossed several columns, and show the crossed
  columns keep their old `colPlate` and take the wrong `plU` under
  `COUP.slave`. No matrix, no sweep; the fixture either shows it or the
  premise of this plan is wrong and the plan stops.
- **M1 — rebuild at import.** In `COUP.apply`, after the geographic walk and
  before `syncEdges`: assign each column the local plate of the
  `msg.plates` interval owning its midpoint, using the seed's own rule —
  contiguous runs of one globe id are one local plate, a closed cut's
  first/last runs of one id are one plate, more runs than `plateCap` merges
  the narrowest into its wider neighbour and books it
  (`S.recon.shortPlateMerge`, as the seed does). Stacks, deposits, vents,
  ghosts and ribbons are untouched: membership is not geology. Which plate
  disappears, appears or is crossed is booked in `S.recon`, and the crust
  identity `before + added + fresh − removed − retired = after` still
  closes, because no bed moves.
- **M2 — acceptance, finite and written down now.** (a) the M0 fixture now
  reads the crossed columns on the new plate, with the new plate's speed
  after `slave`; (b) an unchanged second import changes no membership
  (idempotent — no churn, no re-booked boundaries); (c) a boundary crossing
  the wrap, a plate appearing, a plate vanishing, and an open window all
  land or are refused with the running section untouched; (d) the existing
  suite stays green: `experiments/coupling.js`,
  `experiments/coupling-link.js`, `experiments/acceptance.js`.
  Then gate 4 in `0.4.2-plan.md` §5 and `roadmap.md` is re-read and either
  marked satisfied with this evidence or left open with the reason.
- **Explicitly not in 0.4.3:** the live channel beyond the existing ladder,
  `port/core-log-format.js` and the C-return side table (both need the
  globe's explicit agreement, 0.4.2 §5), C1 namespacing (its own later
  step, `0.4.1-plan.md` §8.6), and any `R1`/smoothness work (§1).

After 0.4.3, the remaining road is short and mostly negotiation, not
measurement: agree C-return/C-live with the globe (its 0.8.3 gate list),
then C1 + the globe-side adapter for 0.9. Each of those wants its own plan
written against the globe's then-current commit, per `0.4.2-plan.md` §10.
