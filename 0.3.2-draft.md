# 0.3.2 — Draft: the light-parcel heat concentration

Reference record of the request that opens this line of work, in the words it was asked in.
The plan itself is not written yet; this file is what a later session reads first, the way
`0.3.0-draft.md` and `0.4.1-draft.md` serve theirs.

## What was asked

Run the full-series re-acceptance on the current tree first and bank the baselines, then take
0.3.2 — the light-parcel heat concentration:

> a particle with T 2.267 against a field of 0.288 at 74 km depth is not just a small feature
> of the routing, it is a strong outlier against the engine's own threshold T < 1.5. Dealing
> with the distribution's tail and verifying a fix through the intake/wall gates looks more
> important than moving on to the geological section in 0.4.1.

The reason it is 0.3.1's business at all: that release's buried-deposit route closed at M2 with
its intake filtered by a field-support term, because every parcel its four-term gate took at
300 Myr was reaching `meltHold` by this concentration rather than by the mantle around it being
warm. The 300 Myr intake stays held back until the tail is answered — no constant is to be moved
to reopen it — and at 600 Myr, where the route does run, the three transfers it takes are the
ones a remedy must leave alone.

## The measured facts a plan starts from

All from `experiments/pt-buried-deposit-melt.js`'s routing-event columns and the 0.3.1 logs
(`experiments/logs/0.3.1-M2-remelt-*.txt`, `0.3.1-buried-deposit-*.txt`):

- The gather is the adjoint of the deposit: `grid.js` renormalizes by `cw = mu_k / W_k` so
  `sum_p m_p a_pk = mu_k`, and the domain's whole intake equals the operator's own wall flux.
  `pt-p4-check.js` gates that at |intake/wall − 1| ≈ 5e-5 over a 300 Myr leg. It is exact and
  it is not what is in question.
- The price is distributional: a parcel's *specific* increment is `sum_q W_pq cw_q inc_q`, so a
  parcel carrying a small share of its stencil's marker mass takes an amplified increment and
  drifts away from the field it sits in. `flip` is 1 (pure FLIP), so nothing relaxes it back.
- Seed 6, 300 Myr at 50 kyr/frame, one leg, all four readings side by side:
  | population | samples | T − field | adjoint gain | mass share of stencil |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | mantle, routed by G5 | 3221 | +0.004 mean (−0.175..0.664) | 1.013 | 0.177 |
  | mantle, all (1/8 stride) | 1.76e7 | −0.0007 mean (−0.576..0.509) | 1.052 (0.13..12.96) | 0.089 |
  | buried deposits, all (1/8 stride) | 39889 | −0.126 mean (−0.473..0.090) | 1.079 (0.44..9.62) | 0.092 |
  | deposits the four-term gate took | 6 | **+0.244 mean (0.222..0.305)** | **1.200** | **0.005** |
- The tail is not bounded by the 300 Myr legs. M0's clock matrix ran a 600 Myr cell (seed 5 at
  50 kyr/frame, `experiments/logs/0.3.1-buried-deposit-clocks.txt`) whose buried-deposit stock
  reaches 7.65e5 km² and whose eligible exposure is 7.461e4 km²·Myr; its closest approach is a
  deposit at **xi 1.0000, T 2.267, H ≈ 2.5, 74 km deep, in a field of 0.288** — 1.6127 of H past
  the threshold, and outside the −0.5..1.5 wall `pt-crust.js` and `pt-p4-check.js` gate on. Five
  of the six parcels 0.3.1's four-term gate took on seed 6 were slivers of 0.014–0.021 km² of host
  mass, against a base parcel of ~1.8e3 km².
- The M2 tree re-ran that 600 Myr cell with the route live
  (`experiments/logs/0.3.1-M2-remelt-600myr.txt`, 37 checks, fingerprint 8261233554728015), and
  the field term separates the two populations there cleanly:

  | | 3 transfers the term admits | 30906 samples it holds back | 5083 mantle routings |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | T − field | +0.0228 mean (+0.020..+0.063) | +0.2152 mean (+0.067..**+1.328**) | +0.0150 mean (−0.347..+0.816) |
  | field − parcel solidus | **+0.0005..+0.0419** | −0.8045..−0.0289 | −0.7714..+0.3625 (mean +0.0017) |
  | adjoint gain | **0.734** mean (0.683..1.878) | 1.476 mean (0.399..**11.880**) | 1.027 mean (0.169..10.191) |
  | stencil mass share | 0.0066 | 0.0238 | 0.4467 |

  The admitted transfers took 9.7302 km² of melt from 53.776 km² of host over 2.6888 km²·Myr of
  sampled mass-time, with 94.55% of that host mass within 0..0.05 of its field — the mantle's own
  routed distribution (84.64%). What the term holds back is 6.814e4 km²·Myr of host mass-time and
  1.579e4 km²·Myr of melt-equivalent. The route moved that cell's trajectory (buried peak
  755684.4 against M0's 764855.5 km², end stock 989750.1 in 1350 parcels against 975746.7 in
  1412, eligible exposure 7.238e4 against 7.461e4 km²·Myr) and left its extremes exactly where
  they were: closest approach xi 1.0000 at 73.9 km, t = 445.30 Myr, T 2.267 against a field of
  0.288; hottest parcel in the world T 2.268 at 8.4 km in phase dep against a field of 0.006,
  while the hottest non-deposit material below 2400 km reached T 1.170.
- The gain extremes in the mantle population itself reach 12.96, so this is not a deposit-only
  mechanism; deposits are where it shows up because erosion and landing make light parcels and
  the surface quench gives them a cold start to be amplified from.

## Constraints carried into the plan

- The intake gates are the acceptance, not the routing count: any remedy is measured against
  `pt-p4-check.js`'s |intake/wall − 1|, the global mass and enthalpy books, the P3.1 latent
  identity and the frame gate, and it must not be chosen by whether it reopens 0.3.1's route.
- `S.hash()` moves when state moves, so a remedy that touches parcel fields moves every quoted
  fingerprint; the plan says which baselines it re-banks.
- Standing rules apply: file:// friendly, classic script tags, no build, guarded
  `module.exports`, single-tab indentation, no allocation in the frame loop, one global per file.
- No value in `params.js` is tuned from a count; a new constant needs its own measured gate and
  a freeze line the way P3.5 and P4.4 froze theirs.

## What the plan has to answer, therefore

1. What is the right statement of the defect: a parcel's temperature is not bounded by the field
   it sits in, or a parcel's mass is allowed to fall far below the node measure it claims?
2. Which remedy, measured: a mass-share floor or a gain cap on the gather, a phase-scoped
   relaxation toward the field (`flip < 1` for deposits, or for every light parcel), a minimum
   parcel mass below which a deposit is merged rather than carried, or a redistribution that
   keeps the adjoint identity exact while bounding the specific increment?
3. What each candidate costs in the intake gate, the frame budget and the fingerprint set, and
   which of them leaves the frozen P3/P4 behavior recognizable.
4. Whether the T bound becomes a gate of its own — every frame, every cell, per phase — so the
   next tail is caught by an acceptance pass rather than by a harness column.
5. What 0.3.1's route does afterwards: re-measure the intake with the remedy in on the 600 Myr
   cell, where the route runs, and check that the three field-supported transfers survive it
   (9.730 km² at gains of 0.683..1.878) while the 6.814e4 km²·Myr of held-back concentration
   stops being generated at all. Only then decide whether condition 5 stays as the filter it is,
   loosens, or becomes redundant.
