# 0.3.0 — Plan: the particle engine

A second engine, on its own page, beside the column engine. The fluid is a grid *and*
particles; the solid is particles only; plates are not a data structure, they emerge from
bonds whose strength is a function of temperature and age. The demonstration clock is chosen
so that convection, plumes and eruptions are all watchable in the same run.

Status: pre-implementation. P0 feasibility is measured and green — three fixtures in
`experiments/` carry every number quoted below. The column engine and the 0.1.x / 0.2.x
series are untouched by this document.

## 0. Conventions

- Style, runtime and discipline are inherited from `AGENTS.md`: single tab indent, LF,
  file:// friendly, classic `<script>` tags, no modules, no build, guarded
  `module.exports`, preallocated typed arrays, no allocation in the frame loop.
- Length in km, time in Myr, velocity in cm/yr on screen (1 cm/yr = 10 km/Myr); temperature
  is normalised: 0 = surface, 1 = the mantle's own drop to the CMB, solidus and liquidus are
  fractions of it.
- New code lives in `js/pt/`; the new page is `particles.html`. Nothing in `index.html` or
  the existing `js/*.js` is retired: the column engine keeps its own page and plans.
- Every number in this plan is reproducible from a script in `experiments/`. If a number is
  not reproducible there, it does not belong here.
- Every kernel no-ops at zero time step, and every kernel that can produce a NaN must fail a
  fixture rather than silently pass one. P0 already caught a false pass built exactly that
  way (a gate that skipped on NaN input reported "0 collisions" as success).

## 1. Why a second engine

The column engine models a vertical slice with 512 columns. A plate is a data structure
(`plN`, `plU`), a contact is a rule table, and the eruptive system is a separate toy box that
writes its output back into the stack. That is a good model of the *statistics* of a surface,
and a poor model of three things this plan is about:

- a plate has no internal structure, so collision has to be scripted;
- erupted material is not the mantle material that produced it, so nothing is conserved
  across the melt, and the toy clock and the plate clock are only coupled through a buffer;
- rigidity is imposed. There is no way for a plate to *become* rigid as it cools, which is
  what the request is about.

The particle engine inverts all three. Bonds hold rock together or they do not; a plate is
whatever the bonds currently hold; the particle that erupts is the particle that melted,
and it welds into the plate it lands on.

## 2. The demonstration clock

### 2.1 The problem the clock has to solve

Real mantle flow is 1-30 cm/yr. Real lava is 1-100 m/s. The ratio is 10⁷-10⁹, so no single
honest time-lapse shows both. The request settles it: pick a demonstration scale and make
the picture move. The chosen scale is derived, not guessed.

### 2.2 What the physics gives (`experiments/pt-conv.js`)

**Units note (P1).** That fixture's box is 1000 km deep, so its cm/yr column is
`kappa/depth` for *that* depth; the engine's slice is 2900 km deep and the same
nondimensional flow is 3.45x slower in cm/yr (measured: the same Ra 1e6 run is 3562 nd, i.e.
11.2 cm/yr in the 1000 km box and 3.9 cm/yr in a 2900 km one). P1 gates on the nondimensional
velocity, `pt-eng-conv.js` prints both columns, and the §7 table quotes the engine's.

A uniform 4:1 Rayleigh-Bénard box, free slip, periodic in x, 128x48, solved with the same
FFT-in-x + tridiagonal-in-y machinery the engine uses. κ = 1e-6 m²/s, D = 1000 km, so
**1 nondimensional velocity unit = 3.16e-3 cm/yr** (= 31.6 m/Myr). Steady state, t = 0.5
diffusion times. The px/frame column is in the bench's own frame, 1280 px over 3000 km:

| Ra | Nu | max&#124;u&#124; | max&#124;v&#124; | upwellings | max&#124;u&#124; cm/yr | max&#124;v&#124; cm/yr | px/frame @100 kyr/f, 2.34 km/px |
|----|----|--------|--------|-----------|--------------|--------------|------------------------|
| 1e5 | 125 | 1759 | 2133 | 9 | 5.6 | 6.7 | 2.9 |
| 3e5 | 26 | 1350 | 2136 | 4 | 4.3 | 6.7 | 2.9 |
| 1e6 | 27 | 3562 | 6123 | 3 | 11.2 | 19.3 | 8.3 |
| 3e6 | 35 | 9950 | 16767 | 5 | 31.4 | 52.9 | 22.6 |

Ra ≈ 1e6 is the demonstration regime: it gives Earth-like peak velocities of 10-20 cm/yr at
Nu ≈ 25-30, and several cells across the box. (The Ra = 1e5 Nu of 125 does not match the
Nu ∝ Ra^(1/3) trend of its neighbours — the diagnostic or the run length is off at the low
end; see §10.)

### 2.3 The chosen scale

**dtGeo = 50 kyr per frame at 60 fps — 3 Myr of geologic time per second of wall clock.**
Slider range 5-500 kyr/frame (0.3-30 Myr/s); the engine's default view is 1280 px over
16000 km (the whole periodic map at once), so 12.5 km per pixel — the lid preset zooms to
2x and the old 6.25 km/px detail — and the same velocities at the default zoom are:

| process | rate | per frame | px/frame | time to watch |
|---------|------|-----------|----------|---------------|
| mantle flow, typical | 6.7 cm/yr | 3.4 km | 0.27 | — |
| plume / upwelling core | 19.3 cm/yr | 9.7 km | 0.78 | 2900 km in 5 s |
| a full cell circuit (8000 + 2900 km of path) | 6.7 cm/yr | 3.4 km | 0.27 | ~1-2 min |
| plate drift | 3 cm/yr | 1.5 km | 0.12 (7 px/s) | 8000 km in ~2.5 min |
| a rift opening | — | — | — | 10 Myr in 3 s |
| an ocean closing | — | — | — | 100 Myr in 33 s |
| a planet cooling into plates | — | — | — | 100-300 Myr in 30-100 s |

A 60-second session is ~180 Myr, one to two mantle overturns. Everything in the list is a
physically-scaled rate driven by one number.

The display clock is not the transport step: **50 kyr is the maximum fluid substep**. At the
default it is one solve per rendered frame; 100-500 kyr/f is split into 2-10 identical fluid
substeps while retaining the requested geologic advance per rendered frame. This keeps a large
clock setting on the same trajectory rather than letting a marker leap across the fine upper
rows. The HUD reports the substep count and measured Myr/s, so extra work at high clocks is
visible rather than implied.

### 2.4 Eruptions: one second clock, and it is off the physics

At 50 kyr/frame an eruption is not an event, it is a rate: the melt the mantle delivers in
50 kyr is what a vent emits in that frame, so at this clock a volcano erupts *continuously*.
That is not a cheat — it is what a 50 kyr resolution of a volcano should look like, and it is
exactly what the column engine could not do, because its toy box needed discrete events.

The one thing that needs a different clock is the trajectory of an individual clast. A
fountain particle has to fly for about a second of wall clock to be legible, which is
roughly what a real clast does, so:

- airborne particles integrate on **wall-clock kinematics**: an ejection velocity of
  2-6 px/frame and a demo gravity chosen so an arc is 10-40 px tall and lasts 0.5-1.5 s.
  While a particle is airborne it does not advance the geologic clock;
- everything else — how much melt is waiting, how long a vent stays open, how much rock is
  built — is the geologic clock's business, and the mass is exact;
- the dilation is stated, not hidden: 1 s of wall clock is 3 Myr of geology at the default,
  so a second of fountain is a real clast flown at ~1e-8 of its true rate. The HUD prints
  both clocks whenever anything is in the air.

This is the deliberate difference from the column engine. There, two clocks meant two
physics talking through a buffer. Here there is one physics and one presentation parameter,
and it touches only the airborne pass.

## 3. State

### 3.1 Particles

One structure of arrays, fixed capacity, preallocated; nothing is created in the frame loop.
Fields per particle: `x, y` (km), `vx, vy` (km/Myr, but see the solid pass, §4.2), `m` (mass
per unit thickness), `T`, `age` (Myr since the particle last froze), `comp` (two bytes:
felsic fraction, water), `melt` (0-1), `size` (km, see merging below), `phase`
(fluid / melt / solid / airborne), `cl` (cluster id), and the bond head index.

Budgets, from the measured costs in §7: 64k capacity, of which ~24.5k are fluid markers (one
per grid cell), ~8-16k are solid, and ~2k are transient eruptive. Particles of different
size coexist: a newly erupted particle is seeded fine (0.25-0.5 km) so an edifice has
detail, and a particle that has been welded and buried for a long time merges with a
neighbour (`m` and momentum and heat sum; the pair keeps one id). Merging is what keeps a
60-second run inside the budget: the count grows during eruptions and decays afterwards.
Mass, momentum and heat are conserved by the merge, and a fixture asserts it.

### 3.2 Bonds and clusters

Bond list, fixed capacity, 8 slots per particle (the measured working set: the four lattice
neighbours plus four diagonals) with a reverse index per slot. Per bond: partner, rest
length, bond age, damage, strength. Clusters are not stored: they are recomputed as the
connected components of the bonds that are currently strong enough to hold (§4.2), which is
cheap at the measured 0.63 µs per particle and removes every consistency problem a stored
partition would have.

### 3.3 The grid

A 2D slice in (x, η): x is periodic and linear; η = asinh(y / yLin) with **yLin = 40 km**,
the same vertical map the column engine draws with, so the ruler, the scale lines and the
log-depth feel carry over unchanged. y runs from the surface to the CMB, 2900 km, so
η spans 0..5.0. Default mesh **256 x 24** (Δη = 0.2074): an 8.3 km top row, a 601 km bottom
row, and a 20 km sky band above the surface for the airborne pass — the log metric is what
makes one grid resolve both the crust and the mantle. 256x24 is P2's shape (P1 ran
128x48): the same 6144 cells at the same 62.5 km x-node spacing, with the periodic map
doubled to 16000 km so four to six convection cells fit (P1's box carried one or two), and
the η rows twice as tall — still five rows through the 25-40 km lid. 256x24 carries 24k
markers at the frame budget of §7; the quality ladder is 256x24, 512x48, 1024x96 (nx must
stay a power of two for the radix-2 FFT), each rung halving the cell size.

**x wraps, everywhere, by construction.** The FFT that diagonalises the x operator *is* a
periodic boundary condition (walls in x would need a sine/cosine transform and a different
spectral derivative), so every stage follows it: the deposit, the gather, the x Laplacian,
the melt indicator's backtrace, the marker move, the cluster pair walk and the rigid fit
(a plate's centroid is folded into [-wrap/2, wrap/2) around its first member, so a plate on
the seam is one body). The view follows it too: the raster samples modulo wrap, a marker is
drawn at its image nearest the camera centre, the camera centre lives in [0, wrap), and the
zoom stops at one period across so no tile is drawn twice. `experiments/pt-wrap.js` is the
gate: translate the whole state by an integer number of cells, run it, translate back, and
the markers agree with the unshifted run to round-off (7e-10 km after 100 frames with
three plates), a plate parked across x = 0 keeps one cluster id, and a camera centred on the
seam draws markers on both sides.

The consequence for the initial condition: on a periodic map a single-mode perturbation of
wavenumber k is k exact copies of one box, and only the markers' sub-node jitter breaks the
symmetry (measured, `experiments/pt-band.js`: 0.07 from 4-fold symmetry at 150 Myr, 0.22 at
300). The shipped draw therefore puts 60% of the perturbation's energy (`icBand`) on
wavenumbers 1..12 with amplitudes and phases drawn from the seed, 40% on the dominant
`icMode` 4: the cells come out of different sizes (0.49-0.54 from symmetry by 150 Myr), and
a new seed is a new planet rather than the same one rotated around the axis. Validation
fixtures pin `icBand = 0`, the single mode the reference comparisons were made with.

The mesh is the *fluid's*: η 0 is the surface and η etaBot the CMB, so the wall nodes are
Dirichlet values (T = 0 and 1) and never take a marker's average.

Markers sample it at **4 per node** (`mpc`), each carrying 1/4 of the node's measure
dEta·JN·dx. One per node is the thinnest possible sample of a stretched mesh: any fold in
the Lagrangian cloud empties a node outright and the field then has to be patched. Four also
makes the deposit's own weight per node come out to its measure, which is what the intake
bookkeeping below rides on. Marker counts are the frame's real cost, so `mpc` is the first
lever the quality switch pulls.

**The deposit is a 3x3 quadratic B-spline**, not CIC: a node is fed by every marker within
1.5 cells, so the clumping a convecting flow inflicts on the cloud (measured: half the nodes
down to one or two markers, a quarter up to six or more) no longer leaves the field without a
sample, and the deposit is smooth enough for the spectral solver. Nodes no marker reaches keep
the value they were carried at until `fillHoles` smooths them from their neighbours — leaving
a stale patch is a buoyancy source that does not exist, and measured at Ra 1e6 it drove the
run to T = -0.8 and Nu = 300.

**The walls reflect**: a marker whose normal step would take it through the boundary node is
mirrored, because that is what an impermeable wall does to material. Clamping instead collects
it: measured, a single convection cell swept the cloud onto the two wall rows and left 60% of
the nodes empty.

Cell-centred fields: `T`, `comp`, `phi` (solid fraction), and the staggered velocity pair;
the Stokes solver works on the same mesh with the metric factors
`J = yLin cosh η`, `J_face = yLin cosh η_face`, so
`lap f = f_xx + (1/J) * d/dη ( (1/J) * df/dη )`.
`experiments/pt-bench.js` is that operator: FFT along x, Thomas along η with the face
metric. Its residual against a dense solve is 1.5e-12 at 128x96 and 4.3e-13 at 256x96, and
the FFT itself matches a naive DFT to 2.2e-14.

The projection needs the operator *pair* that composes into that Laplacian exactly, or the
divergence it removes is not the divergence it left:

```
G at the x face:  (p[i+1] - p[i]) / dx
G at the eta face: (p[j+1] - p[j]) / (dEta * Jf[j+1])
D at the cell:     Jc * (u[i+1/2] - u[i-1/2]) / dx + (v[j+1/2] - v[j-1/2]) / dEta
```

with the boundary face carrying a zero flux rather than a missing term — the first version
of `pt-metric.js` dropped that term and reproduced only the interior of the operator.
`experiments/pt-metric.js` verifies `D(G p) = L p` to 2.4e-16, the buoyancy-driven
projection's divergence to 7.5e-15 of its source, quiescence under a conduction profile to
1.6e-13, and the sign of a hot and a cold anomaly, then times the whole buoyancy + projection
step at **1.5 ms** for 128x96.

### 3.4 The invariant

Particles are conserved. There is no mass ledger to balance because nothing is created or
destroyed: melt converts a particle, freezing converts it back, merging sums two into one.
The only true sink is deep recycling (§4.3, absorb), and it is counted explicitly. The HUD
reports particles by phase, cumulative erupted mass, and the absorb count, and a fixture
asserts the totals across a whole run.

Heat is the one quantity that crosses the walls, so it carries a ledger from P1 on. The
scatter hands each node `cw = mu_k / W_k` (mu_k its measure, W_k the weight that arrived) and
the gather hands the markers back `d_pk * cw_k`: that pair is the adjoint of the deposit, so
`sum_p m_p a_pk = mu_k` for every node and the intake the markers receive *is* the integral
the conduction operator charged the field — measured, the two agree to 1.7e-7 with conduction
alone and to 1-8% in a convecting run, the residue being the nodes the deposit missed
(`S.empty`, reported) rather than a bookkeeping leak. The HUD prints both sides: the marker
ledger and the operator's wall flux, and their ratio. The plan's §10 acceptance depends on
this because an eruption or a weld has to conserve the same book.

## 4. Physics

### 4.1 Fluid

Infinite-Prandtl Boussinesq convection. In the continuum this is `lap(omega) = -Ra dT/dx`
with `u = dpsi/dy`, `v = -dpsi/dx` — the formulation `pt-conv.js` uses to measure the clock,
and the one the engine implements: per x-wavenumber, `lap(omega) = RHS` then `lap(psi) =
omega` as two tridiagonal solves in eta, with psi = 0 on both walls. The staggered pair the
grid carries (u at the eta cell centres, v at the nodes) telescopes exactly, so the flow is
divergence-free by construction — measured 8.8e-17 of its own source — and no projection step
is needed. `experiments/pt-metric.js` verifies the operator pair anyway, because the particle
passes need the same faces:

```
G at the x face:   (p[i+1] - p[i]) / dx
G at the eta face: (p[j+1] - p[j]) / (dEta * Jf[j+1])
D at the cell:     Jc * (u[i+1/2] - u[i-1/2]) / dx + (v[j+1/2] - v[j-1/2]) / dEta
```

Markers ride the **streamfunction** itself (`u = (1/J) dpsi/deta`, `v = -dpsi/dx`, bilinear in
the cell), not two separately interpolated velocity fields: one interpolant cannot disagree
with itself about a cell's circulation, and the map it generates preserves area to 1.3e-4 of
the mean Jacobian per frame (fixture-checked). One trap the bench measured: take the
horizontal derivative spectrally and the vertical one by difference, and the divergence does
not close — a few per cent of the source survives. Every operator in a chain has to come from
the same discretisation, which is what the `D(G p) = L p` check in `pt-metric.js` is for.

- buoyancy comes from the particles: T, composition (dense slab, light crust) and melt
  fraction are scattered to the grid each frame; the reference state is subtracted;
- markers are advected with a FLIP/PIC blend (default 0.9 FLIP) so the flow does not diffuse
  the composition it carries;
- conduction is applied on the grid for the fluid and along the bonds for the solid, which is
  the natural place for it: thermal contact in rock *is* the bond network;
- the solid enters the fluid as a velocity constraint, not as a viscosity: cells whose
  `phi >= 0.5` are penalised toward the solid's interpolated velocity. This deliberately
  avoids the viscosity-contrast problem (1e8 contrasts are where visco-plastic codes go to
  die) at the cost of a few extra projections. P1 measures the coupled cost; the fallback is
  one-way coupling (the solid rides the flow, the flow feels only its buoyancy) which is
  enough for P1-P3 and is a strictly cheaper mode.

### 4.2 Solid: bonds, strength, clusters, shape matching

Every solid particle's strength:

```
heat(T)   = smoothstep(Tsol_lo, Tsol_hi, T)          // hot rock is soft
mu(T,age) = (0.05 + 0.95 * (1 - heat)) * (1 - exp(-age / tauWeld))
bond(i,j) = min(mu_i, mu_j) * (1 - exp(-bondAge / tauWeld))
```

`tauWeld` is a real timescale (10-40 Myr, tuned in P2), so *age* means geologic age and
cooling means what it says. A bond is **elastic below its own strength**:
`strain_yield = eps0 * bond`, with `eps0 = 0.05` measured; above it, damage accumulates and
the rest length relaxes plastically. Damage reaches 1 and the bond is gone; a hot or young
bond yields long before it breaks, which is what makes deformation smooth instead of brittle.

**P2.1's failure law is the rate form of the same idea**, and it is what the engine ships
until the rest-length bookkeeping above lands: the bond's load is the *flow's strain rate
across the pair* (from the gathered flow velocities, never the rigid fit that follows),
`load = |(v_rel . r)| / |r|^2`, elastic below `yieldRate * mu` and accumulating
`dmg += kDamage * mean_over_pairs(load - yield) * dt` above it (clamped to 1). **The mean is
P2.2's**: the same excess charged once per pair makes the damage rate proportional to how
many partners a marker happens to have, and the Lagrangian sample is not uniform — a lid
marker's partner count runs from ~10 to ~50 across the box, so a crowded marker failed about
as much faster as it had more partners. Density is a property of the sample, not of the
rock; the mean makes the law independent of it (and, later, of any marker re-deal or cap).
The integral is accumulated during the pair walk and applied in one pass after it, so the
walk itself is read-only and no bond decision depends on the order the buckets were visited.
Cold quiet damage anneals over `tauHeal`; a soft marker resets to zero. `mu_eff = mu * (1 -
dmg)` feeds the clustering, so a failed seam stops being plate while it is still cold — that
is the rift.

The numbers are calibrated to the engine's own load distribution, not guessed:
`experiments/pt-load-check.js` runs an intact lid (damage off) and percentiles the pair
loads, and computes both damage laws' rates from the same run so the rescale between them is
measured rather than fitted. Measured at 256x24, wrap 16000 (1/Myr): at 50 Myr p50 0.0025 /
p80 0.0051 / p90 0.0070 / p99 0.0166, at 200 Myr p50 0.0122 / p80 0.0273 / p90 0.0424 /
p99 0.0950 — the load grows with lid age, so the yield must sit above the body of the
100-200 Myr distribution and below its hard-worked tail. `yieldRate = 0.06` does (p80 < yield
< p99); `tauHeal = 40` puts seam failure at tens of Myr; `kDamage = 90` is P2.1's per-pair 3
times the measured rescale (24.6x at 50 Myr, 24.9x at 100, 33.9x at 200, so the constant sits
inside that band and `pt-crust`'s emergence gate pins it there). Rank correlation between a
marker's damage rate and its partner count: 0.45-0.73 under the per-pair law, 0.12-0.19 under
the shipped mean. Higher yields (0.09-0.18) measured as one unbreakable plate at 300 Myr —
below 0.06 is where plate breakup lives at this mesh.

**Welding is confined to the upper lithosphere (`y <= yWeldMax = 300` km)**: below the upper
thermal boundary layer (`S.y[p] > P.yWeldMax`), high lithostatic pressure and mantle creep
prevent cold material from welding into a rigid plate (`S.age = 0, S.dmg = 0`). Without this
depth ceiling, an unheated `'rb'` run pools cold sinking plumes along the CMB into an
artificial bottom plate (measured at 150 Myr: 11,252 of 11,958 strong candidates in rows
14..23, 7,941 in row 23 alone, driving `solid.js` to 34 ms/frame; with `yWeldMax = 300`, `'rb'`
at Ra 1e6 runs at 9.38 ms/frame and `pt-crust` gates that cold markers at the CMB never weld).

**Slab pull and why rigid-body velocity projection needs a horizontal drive**: in a symmetric
or weakly asymmetric Boussinesq flow, compressive strain rate at narrow downwellings
(`du/dx ≈ -0.065 /Myr` over 150 km) exceeds `yieldRate = 0.06` while tensile strain rate over
broad upwellings (`du/dx ≈ +0.013 /Myr` over 1,500 km) is five times smaller. Plates therefore
break at trenches and span across upwellings from trench to trench. `experiments/pt-drift.js`
measured the consequence: the leftward branch of the cell under a plate (`~-1.5 cm/yr`) and
the rightward branch (`~+1.5 cm/yr`) cancel in the plate's mass-weighted mean (`0.31 cm/yr`
at the plate, `0.31 cm/yr` at the plate base, `0.37 cm/yr` at 300 km depth where the local
speed is `0.66 cm/yr`). Worse, a rigid cluster `(csVX, csVY, csW)` has no hinge at the trench
to turn the vertical sinking of its cold attached root into horizontal translation of the
surface plate.

P2.2 supplies that hinge through a **slab-root depth-asymmetry moment**: during the cluster
moment pass in `kinematics`, every marker deeper than its cluster's centroid (`ry = S.y[p] -
csY[c] > 0`) contributes to the horizontal-to-depth moment

```
csS[c] += m * (rx / |r|) * ry        (for ry > 0, |r|^2 = rx^2 + ry^2 > 1e-9)
pull    = clamp(kSlab * (csS[c] / csM[c]), -vSlabMax, vSlabMax)
```

Summing over `ry > 0` only is essential: at a trench end (`rx > 0`), the shallow lid markers
sit above the cluster centroid (`ry < 0`) while the attached slab root hangs below it
(`ry > 0`), so a full-cluster sum cancels half the root's torque against the trench roof.
For any broken plate (`csR2[c] < ringMax * wrap^2 * csM[c]`, with `ringMax = 0.04` excluding
an unbroken planet-wrapping lid whose `r^2 / (M * wrap^2) = 1/12 ≈ 0.083`), `pull` is added
to `csVX[c]` (`kSlab = 18 /Myr`, `vSlabMax = 45 km/Myr = 4.5 cm/yr`). Measured in
`pt-drift.js`: an unbroken lid at 100 Myr stays at `0.01 cm/yr`, mean plate drift across
100..400 Myr rises `5.2x` over the cancelled net flow (`1.60 vs 0.31 cm/yr`), and active
slab-bearing plates travel at `2.03-4.95 cm/yr` (`6.28 cm/yr` peak in `pt-crust`'s 300 Myr
run).

The assembly step per cluster is shape matching (Müller-style, and it is what makes an
emergent plate *look* like a plate):

- clusters = connected components over bonds with strength >= 0.25;
- a cluster's target pose is its centroid plus the best-fit rotation of its rest shape;
- each particle is pulled toward its target by `alpha * mu_i`, so a cold cluster moves as one
  body and a hot one follows the flow;
- **the rest offsets are measured from the rest centroid of the particle's own cluster**, and
  the goal uses `q - q_bar` per cluster. Both halves of that sentence were learned by
  breaking them: referencing particle 0, then referencing the parent body, each put the goal
  a whole body-length away and the projection exploded;
- **welding freezes the rest shape as well as the rest length**, and the rest shape relaxes
  for soft material. That is exactly "a smooth connection whose strength grows with age":
  the suture remembers the shape it hardened in, not the shape before the rift.

### 4.3 Phase change, melt and the surface

- **Melting** is temperature against a depth-, water- and composition-dependent solidus. Melt
  weakens the bonds (through `mu`), lowers density and raises buoyancy, so a diapir is just
  hot, molten rock doing what the fluid solver already does;
- **a conduit** forms when a melt parcel's overpressure exceeds the lithostatic load plus the
  rock's tensile strength: the same damage machinery, driven by pressure instead of by
  strain. An eruption is therefore not a special case in the code; it is bond failure with a
  different source term;
- **erupted particles** run the staged kinematics of §2.4, land, cool at the surface over a
  few frames, and bond to what they land on with `age = 0`: young, weak, and welded over
  `tauWeld` as they age. A volcano is therefore a seam that closes, and a collapsed edifice
  is a seam that never got the chance to;
- **subduction** is the same failure mode at plate scale: convergence accumulates strain in
  the contact band; the weaker (hotter, younger, denser) side yields first and rolls under.
  Slab pull is real but, as P0 measured, a rigid cluster hanging in a void cannot turn
  hanging weight into plate motion — the mantle's support and drag are part of the
  mechanism. P2 measures it in the engine rather than in a fixture;
- **deep recycling**: material that reaches the base is absorbed into the grid (temperature
  and composition added to the cell, particle freed, counter incremented). This is the one
  sink, and it is what makes the composition cycle rather than grow;
- **surface transport**: a particle above the surface on a slope steeper than repose (34°,
  the column engine's number) slides on the presentation clock, as airborne material does;
  burial compacts it and it welds; weathering is a composition change with depth.

### 4.4 Two rules that were measured, not guessed

1. A bond must be elastic below its own strength. If damage accumulates from any strain at
   all, every loaded bond creeps apart and every plate disintegrates — the first version of
   the fixture did exactly that.
2. Rest offsets belong to the cluster, not to the body the cluster was born in. Two
   different frame errors of this kind put the shape-matching goal 2.4e5 m away and threw
   particles 1.4e5 m in a single step. A fixture now pins the geometry.

### 4.5 Surface review: why the top is currently flat and how to build real relief

Through P2.2 (`archive/p22-band-300Myr.png`), the planet's top boundary is a ruler-flat
horizontal line at `y = 0` (`eta = 0`). Three separate mechanisms conspire to keep it flat:

1. **Separable Stokes and conduction coordinate top (`eta = 0`)**: `solveStokes` (`js/pt/grid.js`)
   factors `lap(omega) = -RaK dT/dx` and `lap(psi) = omega` into a 1D radix-2 FFT along `x`
   and a 1D Thomas tridiagonal solve along `eta`. Separability requires the coordinate top
   `eta = 0` to be an iso-surface with `psi = 0` (`v = 0`, impermeable free-slip wall), and
   `keepInside` reflects every marker against the half-cell ceiling `eMin = 0.5 * dEta`
   (`yMin = 4.14 km` on the shipped `256x24` mesh).
2. **Hardcoded sky horizon in `js/pt/render.js`**: the raster loop paints `SKY` (`0xff0e0b0a`)
   whenever a screen row maps to `eta < 0` (`j < 0`), drawing a flat horizontal cutoff across
   all `16,000 km` regardless of whether a column sits over a hot plume, a rifted spreading
   center, an old cold basin, or a convergent subduction zone.
3. **Coarse marker spacing versus vertical topographic scale**: at `256x24` (`dx = 62.5 km`,
   4 markers/node -> ~31 km horizontal marker spacing, top cell `8.3 km` tall), deriving the
   visible horizon purely from the raw upper envelope of discrete markers produces either the
   flat `yMin = 4.14 km` reflection line or a noisy `62.5 km` sawtooth. Real planetary relief
   (`±5-10 km`) spans `1.2-2.4` top cells (`18-36 px` at `lid` zoom) and is governed by column
   buoyancy, normal stress at the lid base, crustal thickness, and elastic flexure.

**How the surface is built (`0.3.0-P2.3`, implemented)**: the separable `(x, eta)` domain
`[0, wrap) x [0, eMax]` remains the computational reference frame for the FFT + Thomas
Stokes solver. State carries a periodic 1D horizon `S.zh[i]` in km above the reference sea
level, with exactly zero horizontal mean. G7 builds and filters a target, relaxes the horizon
toward it, then applies conservative hillslope transport:

```
z_raw = flex(z_collision) + kLid * flex(hLid) - kRift * flex(rft)
        + flex(z_therm + z_dyn)
zh    += (1 - exp(-dt / tauSurf)) * (z_raw - zh)
zh    += dt * div(D(slope) * grad(zh))
zh    -= <zh>
```

- **Thermal / Pratt isostasy (`z_therm`)**: integrate each upper-column temperature anomaly
  against that node row's horizontal mean to the compensation depth `yIso = 150 km`:
  `kIso * sum((Tg[i,j] - <Tg[j]>_x) * jN[j] * dEta)`, with `kIso = 0.09 km / (T * km)`.
  This is the broad plume/ridge term; it is relative, so uniform warming does not move the
  global sea-level datum.
- **Dynamic stress (`z_dyn`)**: `kDyn * yIso * du/dx` from the shallow horizontal flow, with
  `kDyn = 0.3`. Convergence depresses the trench-side profile; divergence raises it. Both
  fields are flexurally filtered with the same periodic `[1,2,1]/4` stencil.
- **Crust proxy (`hLid`, `rft`)**: marker contributions are linearly splatted between adjacent
  x columns rather than binned to the nearest column, removing the marker-histogram comb.
  `hLid = sum(m * mu) / dx` over `y <= 250 km`; `rft` is the mass-weighted damage over the
  same sample. Relief uses the *low-pass* load `kLid * flex(hLid)`, not
  `kLid * (hLid - flex(hLid))`: the latter is a high-pass that turns a narrow marker fluctuation
  into a needle with negative side moats. `kLid = 0.05` keeps this welded-lid proxy secondary;
  it is not physical crust thickness. Rift damage contributes `-kRift * flex(rft)` with
  `kRift = 2 km` at unit damage. Real continental roots and plateau loads require P3's `comp[i]`.
- **Convergent plate uplift (`z_collision`)**: the shallowest strong plate marker in each
  column (`y <= 80 km`) supplies a cluster id and horizontal velocity. Across adjacent columns
  belonging to different clusters, positive shortening is
  `max(0, (v_left - v_right) / dx - collisionYield)`. Half of the capped uplift
  `min(zOrogenMax, kOrogen * shortening)` is assigned to each side of the boundary before
  flexural filtering. The shipped proxy uses `collisionYield = 0.03 / Myr`,
  `kOrogen = 45 km * Myr`, and `zOrogenMax = 8 km`. It creates a broad belt only when distinct
  plates converge; a divergent contact or rigid translation does not uplift. This is a
  kinematic collision proxy, not an inter-plate contact solve, and it cannot yet distinguish
  subduction from buoyant continent-continent collision.
- **Filtering, relaxation and erosion**: every source is passed through `lFlex = 4` periodic
  `[1,2,1]/4` passes. The horizon relaxes over `tauSurf = 1.5 Myr`. A conservative nonlinear
  diffusion then transports relief downhill, with edge diffusivity
  `D = kErode * (1 + min(|slope| / slopeErode, 2)^2)`, `kErode = 20 km2/Myr` and
  `slopeErode = 0.025`. Steep one-cell crests therefore erode faster than broad swells, while
  the periodic flux preserves total profile height; subtracting the residual mean keeps the
  sea-level datum exact. No per-frame buffers are allocated.

**Two-way visual and mechanical coupling**:

- **Terrain-warped raster, ocean band, and marker offset in `js/pt/render.js`**: once per frame
  the interpolated drawn horizon `zVis[px] = kRelief * clip(zh(x_px), +-zVisMax)` is built into
  a preallocated `Float64Array(ow)` (`kRelief = 2`, `zVisMax = 8 km`). For each pixel `(px, py)`
  the row's mesh depth is `yRow` and the lookup depth is `yRow + zVis[px] * exp(-yRow / yTaper)`
  (`yTaper = 120 km`, so the CMB at `2900 km` stays flat), converted to a fractional node row by
  the row's `1 / J(eta)` factor:
  - `yRow < -zVis[px]`: sky if the row is also above the flat sea level, **ocean** if it is
    below it -- the sea surface stays at `y = 0`;
  - otherwise the thermal, crust and melt fields are sampled at the shifted row, and the row
    above the horizon takes one brighter pixel: the surface trace.
  A marker is displaced by the same warp at its own depth, so the stipple rides the horizon
  the raster draws. The `relief` switch turns the whole warp off, sea included.
- **Topographic gravity drive (ridge push) in `js/pt/solid.js`**: the cluster pass accumulates
  the periodic central difference of the profile, `csP[c] = sum_p m * dzh/dx(x_p)`, and adds
  `push = clamp(kPush * csP[c] / csM[c], +-vPushMax)` to `csVX[c]` for every cluster, alongside
  P2.2's slab-pull `csS[c]`. A flat profile gives exactly zero, so the slab-pull unit gates are
  unchanged.

Measured by `experiments/pt-surface.js` (250 Myr, shipped mesh): the profile range is
`-9.03..+3.53 km`, the hot 5% averages `+2.41 km` and the cold 5% `-7.67 km`, and its
correlation with the upper-column thermal integral is `0.95`. A hand-built pair of plates
converging at `12 km/Myr` makes a `+1.77 km` smoothed crest; a one-cell `4 km` peak erodes to
`3.897 km` in a `0.5 Myr` step while its adjacent columns rise by `0.052 km`. The profile's
mean remains below `1e-12 km`, the view gates remain periodic, and the surface/render passes
construct no typed arrays per frame. These are broad-relief gates, not a claim that the model
already has realistic continental collision or mountain belts.

## 5. Frame pipeline

Kernel slots, mirroring the column engine's `K0..K9` so the house habits carry over. Each
no-ops at `dtGeo = 0` and at zero population.

| kernel | does |
|--------|------|
| G0 | clocks, sliders, the staging queues |
| G1 | particles -> grid: T, composition, melt, solid fraction, momentum (3x3 quadratic deposit, leaving `cw` behind for G3); repair only nodes whose deposited weight fails that same coverage test |
| G2 | buoyancy, Stokes solve (FFT x, Thomas eta), solid penalty |
| G3 | conduction (implicit in eta, explicit in x), the increment back to the markers (FLIP weighted by `cw`), the psi-map advection; P1.1 also carries a no-feedback pressure-release melt indicator for the view |

| G6 | eruptions: reservoirs, vents, staging, airborne pass, landing |

| G8 | diagnostics: ledger, counts, timing; the HUD's numbers |

## 6. Rendering and UI

- the grid is a low-resolution background raster (T, composition, solid fraction) drawn
  through a prebuilt palette LUT, exactly the pattern the column engine's renderer uses;
- before P3 provides conserved melt particles, a gold **pressure-release melt indicator** may
  overlay only hot, upward material in the shallow decompression window. It is labelled as an
  indicator and has no feedback on P1 flow; it makes the separate magma expectation visible
  without falsely reversing the cold-slab mantle flow;
- particles are additive splats sized by `size` and coloured by composition and melt (the
  measured 3x3 splat at 40k costs 1.63 ms; the additive path is the intended one);
- bonds are drawn as a faint line web, on a quality toggle, only when zoomed in — it is the
  feature that makes the emergence of a plate visible as a *thing*, not just as a
  consequence;
- **the surface relief (`zh[i]`, §4.5, implemented)** warps each raster column's lookup row and
  each shallow marker by `kRelief * zh * exp(-y / yTaper) / J(y)`, so mid-ocean ridge swells,
  axial rift grabens, abyssal basins and deep subduction trenches are visible on the separable
  `(x, eta)` mesh. The sea surface stays at the reference `y = 0`: a column whose drawn horizon
  is below it is filled with the **ocean band**, which is what makes a ridge stand out of the
  water and a trench fill with it. The row just above the horizon takes a brighter pixel -- the
  surface trace -- and the `relief` switch in the bar turns the whole warp off;
- the camera can frame the whole mantle (η 0..5) and the near-surface detail (the same
  asinh map, so the ruler is continuous). All camera moves funnel through one function, as
  the column engine's does through `afterView()`;
- the HUD carries: Myr since start, kyr/frame, px/frame at the current zoom (so the
  demonstration scale is legible, not implied), particle counts by phase, the staging factor
  during an eruption, the surface profile's own extremes, and the ledger;
- the control bar is an instrument too: every switch carries its state in `aria-pressed`,
  set by `pt/ui.js` from the renderer's own flag and lit by one CSS rule, so an enabled
  overlay is visible at a glance and the button cannot disagree with what is drawn. The
  three camera presets are a radio group, and any manual pan or zoom puts the group out
  because the camera no longer is one. `experiments/pt-ui.js` drives every button and key
  against a DOM parsed from `particles.html` and reads the attribute back.

and the divergence boundary term only appeared against the solver's own operator.

## 10. Acceptance (whole series)

A viewer opens `particles.html` and watches, at one clock, for a minute: a hot planet cool
into a rigid lid; convection cells and plumes move the interior; a rift opens where the lid
is weak and closes again where it welds; a vent erupts and its deposit welds into the plate;
a slab sinks and is recycled. The HUD reports the geologic clock, the pixel speed, the
particle counts and the ledger. 60 fps at the §7 budget on the reference box, file://, no
build. Any fixture that produced a number in §2, §4 or §7 must still pass.

## 11. Open questions

- **The low-Ra end of the clock table is not physical, and is now explained.** Nu reads 125
  at Ra = 1e5 with 9 upwellings in an aspect-4 box, which no Ra 1e5 convection does; the
  engine, whose advection is Lagrangian and therefore adds no numerical diffusion, reads
  Nu = 1.00 there (conduction only, as it should be). The reference's semi-Lagrangian step
  (Courant 6) is the suspect. The reference's Ra >= 1e6 end agrees with the engine to 2.3%,
  so the clock is trustworthy there; do not quote its low-Ra rows until they are reworked.
- **The Lagrangian cloud clumps, and the field has to cope.** A convecting flow at Ra >= 1e6
  folds the marker sample along its strain lines: measured, half the nodes end up with one or
  two markers and a quarter with six or more. No marker repair is affordable -- re-dealing the
  cloud onto the lattice resamples the field and held a convecting box at Nu = 1.00 at every
  cadence tried (16, 64, 1000000 frames), and the local repair converges to the same place
  when it runs often. What works is making the *field* not care: the 3x3 quadratic deposit,
  `fillHoles` for the nodes nothing reaches, and the adjoint weights so the intake the
  markers miss is exactly the intake the ledger reports. Measured: holes 0-8% at Ra 1e6,
  ledger 1.00-1.08, T inside [-0.4, 1.0]. P3's eruptions will change the marker count and with
  it this balance; re-measure there.
- **Slab pull (resolved in P2.2).** P0 measured that a rigid cluster hanging in a void does not
  convert hanging weight into plate motion, and `pt-drift.js` proved that on the convecting mesh
  plates span from trench to trench across upwellings so basal flow traction cancels (`0.31 cm/yr`).
  P2.2's slab-root depth-asymmetry moment (`csS[c]` summed over `ry > 0`, `kSlab = 18`, `vSlabMax =
  45`) supplies the missing trench hinge and drives slab-bearing plates at `2.03-6.28 cm/yr`.
- **Flat surface vs separable Stokes domain (resolved in P2.3).** The FFT + Thomas Stokes solver
  requires a flat `eta = 0` coordinate boundary (`v = 0`) and `keepInside` reflects markers at
  `yMin = 4.14 km`, so the raw particle top is ruler-flat by construction and the solve stays
  `0.6 ms`. What fills the horizon is a separate periodic 1D profile `S.zh[i]` (thermal
  isostasy + dynamic stress + smoothed lid/rift loading + convergent-cluster uplift, followed
  by hillslope diffusion; `pt-surface.js` 33 checks), drawn by a per-column terrain warp with
  an ocean band and coupled back into the plates as ridge push. The markers keep their flat
  coordinate top: the profile *is* the surface, and the solver never sees it. The collision
  term remains a kinematic proxy until the model has compositional crust and inter-plate
  contact.
- **Coupled cost.** The x3 penalised solve is 9.7 ms at 256x96, which is too much next to
  the particle passes. P1 decides between the penalised two-way coupling and the one-way
  mode, on measurement.
- **How fine the crust must be.** One marker per 2 km cell at the surface makes a 30 km
  edifice 15 particles across. Fine particle seeding at eruption (0.25-0.5 km) plus merging
  is the plan's answer; if it is not enough, P3 splits the near-surface grid.
- **Non-uniform particle mass** is what makes the merging scheme work, and it puts a
  variable into the shape-matching rest shape. Watch for it in P2.

## 12. Relationship to the column engine

- the column engine stays the engine of `index.html`, with its own 0.1.x and 0.2.x plans;
- shared code that is genuinely shared: `js/rng.js` (xorshift + stable noise), `js/perf.js`,
  and the asinh display map, which the new engine re-exports as `js/pt/scale.js` rather than
  importing the column engine's `geom.js`;
- the two engines answer different questions: the column engine is about the statistics of a
  surface over a very long run; the particle engine is about mechanism at a watchable scale.
  A fork may keep one and drop the other, which is why this plan is written to stand alone.
