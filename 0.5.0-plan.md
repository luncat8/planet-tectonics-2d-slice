# 0.5.0 — Plan: two FFT-free convection engines

Two new engines beside the two that exist, each on its own page: `hybrid.html`, where the
magma is a liquid on the cell grid and the crust is particles (links on or off), and
`soup.html`, where everything is particles — magma without links, crust with links whose
strength carries the plates, the melt and the cracks. Both drop the spectral Stokes solve
of the 0.3 engine for local mechanics: an iterative cell solver in the hybrid, a pairwise
particle solver in the soup. The purpose is **frame cost and legible gameplay**, not
precision: the gates are behavior scenarios and conservation ledgers, not solver
residuals.

The 0.3 engine (`index.html` / `particles.html`) stays as the precision line with its own
harness series; the column engine (`columns.html`) is untouched. The request record is
`0.5.0-draft.md`.

## 0. Conventions

- Style, runtime and discipline inherited from `AGENTS.md`: single tab indent, LF,
  file:// friendly, classic `<script>` tags, no modules, no build, guarded
  `module.exports`, preallocated typed arrays, structure of arrays, no allocation in the
  frame loop, one global per new file.
- Units identical to the 0.3 engine's: length km, time Myr, velocity km/Myr (cm/yr on the
  HUD at 0.1×), temperature normalised 0 at the surface and 1 at the CMB, enthalpy H with
  one latent-heat unit. Nothing about the solver change redefines the physics units.
- New code: `js/pc/` (shared substrate, globals `PC*`), `js/hy/` (hybrid engine, `HY*`),
  `js/sp/` (soup engine, `SP*`). Pages `hybrid.html` and `soup.html`, cross-linked from
  `index.html` and `columns.html`. No mirror pages this time — `particles.html` exists as
  an alternate URL for the 0.3 engine only.
- Harnesses in `experiments/`: `pc-*` shared, `hy-*` hybrid, `sp-*` soup, plus
  `planck-smoke.js` and its vendored `experiments/vendor/planck-1.5.1.min.js` (the Box2D
  reference leg; pages never load it).
- Every number asserted here is reproducible from a script in `experiments/`, or is
  explicitly marked *estimate, to be replaced by M0/M2/M4 measurement*. Where a default is
  a decision this plan makes ahead of its measurement, §6 says which measurement can
  overturn it and how the default flips.
- Both engines stay deterministic given the seed: fixed visit orders, fixed iteration
  counts, no wall-clock input anywhere except the airborne pass, which advances on
  supplied intervals clamped by the 0.3 flight rule (`flightDtMax`).

## 1. Why two more engines

The FFT is not what the 0.3 engine pays for — the solve is 0.89-3.24 ms of a 10.8-11.1 ms
frame at 256x24. What the spectral solve *costs structurally* is the target here:

- **a flat coordinate top.** The separable Stokes domain needs η 0 to be the surface
  everywhere, so relief lives in a separate periodic horizon model that never moves
  material (`fluid.js` G7), the ocean is a display band, and a trench is a warp of raster
  rows — not a hole in the ground that material falls into;
- **a radix-2 global transform every frame**: `nx` must be a power of two, and the solve
  is one global synchronisation with no local decomposition — nothing about it ports to a
  GPU compute pass;
- **an imposed rigidity story.** Plates are a strength overlay projected onto markers
  (kinematic rigid fit, a slab-pull *moment* added by hand because a symmetric flow cancels
  at the plate, 0.3.0 §4.2). Nothing in the mechanics actually pulls;
- **pressure is a depth formula.** The solidus reads y, not the load that is actually on
  top of the parcel, so "unpressurize" cannot be a physical event in that engine — its
  pressure-release melt was a diagnostic overlay, and was retired.

Both new engines answer all four with local mechanics. The surface is material: a pile of
particles is the relief, a trench is a gap, an edifice is clasts that landed. The
lithostatic pressure is the weight that is actually there, so a rift that unloads a column
drops the solidus of what is under it and the melt that follows is physical, not painted.
`nx` is free. Every per-frame operation is local — a red-black sweep over cells, a pair
walk over a hash — which is the shape a GPU compute pass wants (§6, GPU row).

What is deliberately given up: converged solves, spectral accuracy, and the 0.3 engine's
operator-adjoint heat bookkeeping between markers and nodes. The melt/flight/ledger
machinery that produced 0.3's conservation guarantees is transplanted instead (§3), so
what is kept is exact: mass, enthalpy, identity and the books. What is relaxed is
*fidelity*: velocities within bands, not residuals; cells counted, not converged.

The behavior list the request names is the whole acceptance surface: **convection,
melting, eruption, unpressurize** (§3.6, §11).

## 2. The two engines at a glance

| | HY `hybrid.html` | SP `soup.html` |
|---|---|---|
| magma (mantle liquid) | cell grid: enthalpy/composition fields, staggered velocity, iterative pressure projection | particles, no links; contact or PBF density mode (§6, smooth row) |
| crust (mechanical plates) | particles: a thin skin on the lid plus every pile, edifice and deposit; links on/off (toggle, default on) | particles with links: bond law transplanted from 0.3, cracks are damage, melt is strength → 0 |
| who owns mass | cells own the liquid, particles own the solid, transfers are booked transactions (§4.4) | particles own everything; the pool is the only book (§5.1) |
| heat | finite-volume donor-cell conduction + advection on the mesh (conservative) | pairwise conservative exchange on the same pair walk as mechanics (§5.4) |
| pressure | grid dynamic pressure + particle overburden on the top boundary | column-bin hydrostatic integral + the density-constraint multiplier (§5.5) |
| surface | particle pile top per column; no free liquid surface exists above it (§4.5) | particle pile top per column |
| what it buys | the cheapest frame: a 6k-cell liquid plus a few thousand crust particles | the most mechanical: slab pull, rifts, cracks and piles are forces, not overlays |
| frame target (default rung) | ≤ 8 ms (§8; M2 liquid measured 1.27 ms) | ≤ 11 ms at ~6.7k particles; the ~26k coverage rung is optional (§8, M0-measured kernel) |

Both share: the (x, η) asinh geometry and wrap of 0.3 §3.3, the demonstration clock of
0.3 §2, the phase law, the bond law's form and starting constants, the pool/id/transaction
discipline, the flight constants, the ledger discipline, the option registry and the four
scenarios (§3).

## 3. The shared substrate (`js/pc/`)

### 3.1 The phase law, transplanted (`pc/phase.js`)

The 0.3 law verbatim (params.js of `js/pt/`): the solidus curve anchored at the surface,
the deep margin and the onset depth, the liquidus gap, the normalized latent heat, H the
conserved quantity with T and melt its equilibrium readings, both mappings exact inverses.
One amendment: **the solidus evaluates against pressure, and the pressure has two
sources** — `solidusOf: 'depth'` (y, bitwise-identical to 0.3) and `'load'` (the emergent
lithostatic load of §4.4/§5.5). Default `'load'`, because that is what makes the request's
"unpressurize" a physical event; `'depth'` stays as the comparability option against 0.3.

Gate: `experiments/pc-phase.js` evaluates `pc/phase.js` and `js/pt/phase.js` over the same
(y, cF, cW, H) lattice with 0.3's frozen constants and `solidusOf: 'depth'` — the two must
agree bitwise. The `'load'` branch is gated by the hydrostatic fixtures of each engine
(§10) and the S4 scenario (§3.6).

### 3.2 The pool (`pc/pool.js`)

The 0.3 pool discipline on the new layouts: `0..n` a dense prefix of a fixed capacity, a
stable never-reused id per parcel, transactions (admit, split, merge, carry, release) that
conserve mass, enthalpy, composition and momentum and book themselves, capacity staging
that defers or reservoirs instead of discarding. The helpers take the caller's SoA
descriptor; each engine owns its arrays. Starting capacities are M0/M2/M4 measurements,
not assumptions, exactly as 0.3 P3's budget was.

### 3.3 The bond law (`pc/bond.js`)

The 0.3 form: `mu(T, age) = (0.05 + 0.95 (1 - heat(T))) (1 - e^{-age/tauWeld})`, elastic
below `eps0 * bond`, the rate-form damage law with the per-marker mean excess, cold-quiet
annealing over `tauHeal`, `mu_eff = mu (1 - dmg)` feeding clustering, welding confined to
`y <= yWeldMax = 300` km. The grafted constants are 0.3's measured ones (`yieldRate` 0.06,
`kDamage` 90, `tauHeal` 40 Myr, `clusterMin` 0.25, `tauWeld` 20 Myr, `TLock`/`TSoft` 0.35/
0.45, `yWeldMax` 300 km); each engine recalibrates them against its own load distribution in
its own fixture (0.3's `pt-load-check` pattern), so the transplant is a starting point and
never a promise. One constant is **not** a transplant: `eps0` 0.05 is new here. 0.3 carried
the elastic bound implicitly inside its cluster projection and never named a strain, while
these engines' bonds are pairwise springs, so the bound has to exist as a number with an
owner in `pc/params.js`. It is a guess at the size of the elastic regime (half a bond's rest
length per unit strain would be 1.0; 0.05 is the order 0.3's measured lid strain rates
imply), and M3/M5 own the fixture that measures it.

### 3.4 The clock

Unchanged from 0.3 §2, because it was derived from the Ra reference, not from the solver:
geological 5..500 kyr/frame with 50 default; wall-clock 1/60 s frames for the airborne
pass only; the HUD prints both clocks whenever anything is in the air. The substep rule
changes shape (a displacement clamp, §5.3 / §4.3) but not the contract: a rendered frame
contains k identical substeps when the clock or the motion asks for them, k printed on the
HUD.

### 3.5 The option registry (`pc/opt.js`) — the request's central ask

options to choose and adjust in GUI and see how it behaves". So the registry is a
deliverable, not a convenience. Each option: id, kind (toggle / range / enum), range,
default, `live` (applies per frame) or `reset` (applies at next planet), and the section of
this plan that owns its default. The page's bar is static HTML (the house pattern, so
`aria-pressed` and the harness can read it); `experiments/pc-opt.js` asserts the registry
and the two pages agree entry for entry — id, kind, range, default — so the GUI cannot

### 3.6 The scenarios (`pc-scenario.js`)

Four, named exactly as the request names them, run headless against either engine through
one interface (the engine exposes step, read fields, ledger):

- **S1 convection.** From the 0.3 initial-conditions family (`ic: 'cool'`, `icBand` 0.6,
  seed 1): at 100-300 Myr the box carries 3-6 cells, a lid forms (surface-band mobility
  falls below a stated fraction of the interior's), and peak vertical velocity sits in
  the demonstration band **3-20 cm/yr**. Reference table: `pt-conv.js` Ra 1e6 row
  (Nu 27, 3 upwellings in the 4:1 box). The band's floor is anchored on measurements at
  this plan's own depth: the shipped 0.3 planet on the same IC, clock and seed reads a
  33 km/Myr (3.3 cm/yr) median peak over 100-300 Myr, `pt-conv`'s steady reference
  converts to 67 km/Myr at 2900 km (its printed cm/yr column is calibrated at 1000 km,
  where the same flow reads 3.45x faster — the source of an earlier 6 cm/yr floor,
  corrected at M2), and M0's soup leg read 71.66 km/Myr.
- **S2 melting.** Where an upwelling limb rises through its own solidus window, melt
  fraction exceeds 0 inside that window and is ~0 outside it; the melt mass equals the
  equilibrium reading of the local H and P through `pc/phase.js` (a consistency gate, not
  a rate gate).
- **S3 eruption.** At least one vent episode per 300 Myr leg: liquid/melt reaches the
  surface through a damaged path, fountains on the wall clock (arcs 0.5-1.5 s, the 0.3
  flight constants), lands, builds an edifice, and the ledger closes through the whole
  chain (source phase → airborne → deposit → weld).
- **S4 unpressurize.** A scripted instant unload of a crust patch (mass removed to a
  named ledger line, never deleted): the solidus under it drops by the load's own weight,
  melt appears within ≤ 2 Myr, its mass matches the equilibrium response of the unloaded
  column to that solidus drop within a stated tolerance, and the pulse decays as the load
  re-equilibrates. With `solidusOf: 'depth'` the same fixture must produce **no** pulse —
  the negative control that proves the mechanism is the load and not a coincidence.

## 4. Engine HY — the hybrid (`js/hy/`, `hybrid.html`)

### 4.1 State

- The mesh wraps 16000 km by 2900 deep, default 256x24, with `nx` **no longer required
  to be a power of two** (kept at 256 for rung comparability; the ladder may use any nx
  from M2 on). The rows are **tanh-clustered at both walls** (~8 km cells against each
  boundary layer, ~300 km mid-mantle), not 0.3's one-sided asinh rows: measured at M2
  on the asinh rows, the 570 km bottom cell passes 1.4% of the surface heat flux, so a
  cell planet free-cools through its own CMB resistor instead of convecting on CMB
  input (0.3 survives those rows because its markers resolve sub-cell against the wall;
  a cell average cannot). The asinh mapping stays the *view* — the raster resamples the
  mesh by depth and the shared ruler is unchanged — while every operator coefficient is
  built from actual y differences, so the stretching is mesh data. Cell fields: H, cF,
  cW (conserved, finite-volume), T and melt (equilibrium readings), the load column,
  solid-fraction scratch from the particle scatter (from M3), staggered u, v.
- The crust pool: the 0.3 SoA field set (x, y, e, vx, vy, m, H, id, ph, age, mu, dmg, ...)
  with the phases crust / airborne / deposit. The **mechanical lithosphere is a thin
  skin** — a few tens of km thick, ~1-4k particles at the default rung, plus every pile,
  edifice and deposit — while the thick thermal boundary layer under it stays grid liquid
  with low mobility (`mob(T)`). Basal accretion grows the skin downward as the lid cools;
  melting eats it back (§4.4). This division is what keeps HY cheap: the lid's mass is
  cells, only its structure is particles.
- Population targets (*estimate, replaced by M3 measurement*): 1-4k crust skin, 2-6k
  edifice/deposit/airborne reserve at the default rung.

### 4.2 Kernels

| kernel | does |
|--------|------|
| H0 | clocks, sliders, staging queues |
| H1 | particles → grid: mass, enthalpy, composition scatter (the load and solid-fraction fields) |
| H2 | buoyancy from T; viscous predictor `lap u = mob(T) F` and red-black projection to divergence-free, `projIters` sweeps split between them |
| H3 | donor-cell (or MacCormack) advection of H, cF, cW; finite-volume conduction; wall Dirichlet T 0/1 (velocity is a diagnostic balance, never advected) |
| H4 | phase equilibrium (pc/phase) on cells and particles; crust skin: bonds, damage, clusters |
| H5 | melt/freeze transactions particle ↔ cell, basal accretion, capacity management |
| H6 | vent scheduling from overpressured melt cells under damaged paths; wall-clock flight, landing, welding |
| H7 | pile top per column (relief), deposit bookkeeping |
| H8 | ledgers, phase counts, HUD diagnostics |

Each no-ops at dtGeo = 0 and at zero population.

### 4.3 The liquid

Infinite-Prandtl in spirit: no inertia term, and velocity is a diagnostic that is never
advected. Per fluid step a red-black Gauss-Seidel **viscous predictor** relaxes
`lap u = mob(T) F` (F the row-mean-removed buoyancy `-RaK (T - rowMean)`, on the
staggered D/G operator pair in the metric form 0.3's `pt-metric.js` verified; only the
*solver* of the Poisson problems changed), then the same sweep budget's second half
solves the projection Poisson problem and `u -= grad p`. The pressure never feeds back
into the predictor: for constant mobility the Laplacian and the projection commute, so
the pair's fixed point is the Stokes velocity, while a steady predictor carrying
`grad p` in its source is a structurally unstable iteration (measured at M2: it
amplifies gradient modes geometrically and is gone in two frames). Neither solve is
**converged** in a step — that is the deal — both are warm-started from the last step,
and the gate is not a residual: S1's cell count and velocity band are the gate, plus a
divergence *budget* the HUD prints so the sloppiness is visible rather than hidden
(it settles under 1% of peak strain). A rendered frame is `k` fluid steps of at most
50 kyr (0.3's `dtFluidMax` contract): the unconverged projection's residual divergence
compresses flux-form transport by ~(div · dt) a step, harmless at 50 kyr and a runaway
at 500, and re-solving per substep makes sweeps-per-Myr independent of the clock.
`mob(T) = exp(+b (T - 1/2))`, b = `mobContrast`: hot material is more mobile and the
cold lid is sluggish by construction; where it is not sluggish enough, the crust skin
carries the rigidity instead.

Advection of the conserved fields is donor-cell upwind by default (conservative, diffusive
— the ledger loves it); MacCormack is the sharpness option, clamped, with what the clamp
removes booked on its own ledger line so its heat book still closes exactly (measured at
M2: the unbooked clamp eats 43% of the gross wall throughput over 100 Myr, which is the
option's stated price, not a rounding tolerance). The heat ledger closes against the
wall flux in the 0.3 form (|intake/wall − 1| < 5e-5 over a leg; measured at M2:
residual 7e-13 of gross throughput over 300 Myr at the donor default).

### 4.4 The crust and the two books

Cells own the liquid's mass; particles own the solid's. Three transaction families, each
booked on both sides every frame:

- **melt**: a crust particle whose equilibrium melt reaches 1 is absorbed into its host
  cell (mass, enthalpy, composition credited; id retired);
- **freeze / basal accretion**: a cell just under the skin whose melt is 0 and whose
  temperature is below the solidus donates mass to the adjacent crust particle (or admits
  one), debited;
- **erupt / land / weld**: liquid from overpressured melt cells becomes airborne clasts
  (mass leaves the cell book for the air book), flies on the wall clock, lands as deposit
  particles, welds into the skin (phase change only, no mass change).

Coupling is one-way first (grid velocity → particle drag; particle buoyancy against the
local liquid density), with the load-back (particle mass and stress onto the top boundary
and the projection) gated in M3 behind a stability fixture. The **load pressure** for
`solidusOf: 'load'` is the particle overburden integrated down the column plus the
liquid's own hydrostatic part — an O(nx) column pass, not a solve.

Subduction needs no special kernel: the skin's bonds carry a plate into a converging
margin, the plate bends under the pair forces, its leading edge heats, melts and is
absorbed — the recycle book is the melt book with the id's history attached.

### 4.5 The surface

No free liquid surface exists above the pile: everything above the liquid's top boundary
is particles (skin, edifices, ejecta), so the pile top per column *is* the relief and no
volume-of-fluid machinery is needed. The renderer's horizon, ocean band and surface trace
read the same pile top the ledger does. Trenches are gaps; a rift valley is the skin torn
apart; an edifice is clasts that landed — each is the same particle set, drawn once.

### 4.6 Ledgers

Liquid book + particle book + air book + reservoirs, every transfer a named counter, the
HUD reporting inventories and cumulative transfers separately — the 0.3 §3.4 vocabulary,
with the cell book in place of the marker book. Mass closes exactly (transactions are
exact); enthalpy closes against the wall flux in the 0.3 form.

## 5. Engine SP — the soup (`js/sp/`, `soup.html`)

### 5.1 State: the warped particle plane

No field grid exists. The only lattice is the neighbour hash, and it owns no field. All
pairwise mechanics run in the **(x, w) warped plane**, w = η·s with one frozen aspect
constant `s ≈ 1.3e3 km per η unit` (computed once from the default view geometry: 12.5
km/px horizontal against the η-per-px of the full-mantle preset; the camera presets scale
x and η together, so roundness is zoom-invariant). The constant is chosen so that one
warped km is one screen scale in *both* axes — the plane is isotropic on screen by
construction. A particle is a disc of diameter d in the warped plane:

- its **mass** is `m = rho * d * (J(y) * d / s)` — the physical area it stands for is d
  wide and J·d/s tall — which is the 0.3 marker-measure policy in disguise: mass grows
  with J (72.5x from surface to CMB) so the deep is coarse in *content*, not in
  coverage. The check that the disguise is exact: with d = dx = 62.5 warped km, a
  surface particle carries ~120 km² against the 0.3 surface marker's ~130 km² measure;
- its **shape** is round on screen at every depth, a wide-flat pancake in km near the
  surface (anisotropy s/J = 32:1 at y = 0), near-isotropic at y ≈ 1300 km (1:1), tall-thin
  at the CMB (0.45:1). Piles and trenches therefore keep depth-consistent *looks* — the
  angle of repose the eye sees is the angle the solver used — which is the whole point of
  `space: 'screen'`. `space: 'km'` swaps the pair metric to physically isotropic circles
  and is kept as the option, because the request asks to see both (§6);
- the hash bins are **squares of side d_max in the warped plane** (x and w are the same
  units now), walked over the 3x3 neighbourhood. Every 0.3 mesh row is the *same warped
  height* (Δη·s = 267 warped km), so a warped-square lattice has **uniform occupancy at
  every depth by construction** — the pt mesh shape, as a bucket structure only. x wraps
  in bin index.

Population has two distinct rungs. The default behavior rung is `nx = 128`, about 6.7k
particles, because that is the population whose 300 Myr circulation and frame cost M0
measured. The optional coverage rung is `nx = 256`, about 26k particles: its warped-square
lattice of side dx = 62.5 km puts ~4.3 particles in each 256x24 mesh cell (62.5 wide x 267
tall in warped km), matching 0.3's `mpc` 4 coverage. It is not the default: M0 measured
19.39 ms for its PBF kernel alone (§8). The deep needs no denser sample because its
particles are heavier (§6, size row).

### 5.2 The unified pair walk

One hash pass per substep serves everything pairwise, in this order, read-only first:

1. kernel sums: density estimate ρ̂, the PBF multiplier λ (`magmaMode: 'pbf'`), pairwise
   conduction weights;
2. bonds: rest length, strain rate, damage increment, healing (crust only);
3. position pass: contact pushout (`magmaMode: 'contact'`) or PBF density projection
   (`pbfIters` iterations), bond elastic/plastic response, per-pair wrap folding.

Per-phase mode: the mantle is pbf, deposits may be contact (granular edifice slopes —
visually a feature: tephra sits at an angle of repose while lava pools flat), the crust is
always contacts + bonds. **Classic weakly-compressible SPH is rejected**: its stiff
equation of state at dt = 50 kyr is the instability the request worries about; position-
based density constraints avoid that explicit equation-of-state stiffness, but they are
not unconditionally stable or converged. The HUD counts unsatisfied constraints and the
density residual; the displacement clamp in §5.3 is mandatory because a large jump can
move a pair through one another before the neighbour search sees an overlap.

### 5.3 Integration: carried circulation, displacement-clamped

HY remains quasi-Stokes. SP uses a carried-circulation relaxation rather than a velocity
Verlet term: `gamma = inertia^(dt / 50 kyr)` and the predicted velocity relaxes from the
previous substep's velocity toward the current buoyancy/constraint drive with weight
`1 - gamma`. `inertia = 0` is the overdamped limit; the SP default is **0.98**, with the
retention evaluated from the geological clock so changing the substep count does not
change the physical relaxation. This is a demonstration dynamics choice, not a claim of
finite-Prandtl mantle physics.

Buoyancy uses `T - horizontalRowMean(T)` at the current warped row. The instantaneous
row-mean gauge removes the depth-mean thermal load that a few local PBF sweeps cannot
represent; absolute temperature and a fixed initial-geotherm gauge are experiment
controls, not shipping defaults. No anomaly smoothing is applied by default. SP's
`mobContrast` default is 0; enabling it uses the same hot-is-more-mobile law as HY and
must re-run the S1 distribution gate.

The **substep rule** is `k = ceil(max |predicted Δw| / (substepCap * d))`, applied
globally and printed on the HUD. It includes carried velocity, not just the new forcing.
At the selected rung M0 measured k = 1 at the 100/200/300 Myr checkpoints and finished
the 500-kyr stability leg at k = 3; the 26k coverage rung finished its fast-clock leg at
k = 6. A per-particle clip remains an experiment option, never a silent substitute for
the global rule, and any use
re-gates S1. Position projection alone does **not** prevent tunnelling: the neighbour
hash must see the pair. M0's two-disc fixture crosses without the clamp and remains
separated at 1 diameter with `substepCap = 0.35` and 12 substeps.

### 5.4 Heat

Pairwise and conservative on the same walk. The default **analytic** scheme solves each
wall relaxation and each two-capacity pair exchange by its exponential solution over the
substep. Every pair operation is convex, cannot overshoot, and conserves the pair's heat
for any dt; alternating the pair visit direction removes fixed-sweep bias. This replaces
an explicit global heat-CFL substep count that grew without bound for compressed pairs.
`heatMode: 'explicit'` retains the symmetric flux
`dH_i = -kappa w_ij (T_i - T_j) dt` as a control. At 500 kyr/frame M0 measured one
analytic pair walk at 16.28 ms against eight explicit heat passes at 28.00 ms, with
stock-relative closure 8.28e-16 and 1.11e-15 respectively.

Boundaries: particles inside the surface cooling band relax toward T = 0 (the
`depositCoolDepth` 30 km idea), particles inside the CMB band toward T = 1, and every wall
increment is booked. The ledger reports absolute, heat-stock-relative and wall-relative
residuals so a large stock cannot make an absolute error invisible. The warped-space
normalisation against physical km² is still calibrated by fixture, not derived here: a
conduction-only column must relax to the 0.3 conduction profile within tolerance
(`sp-conduct.js`). Conservation alone does not prove that profile.

### 5.5 Pressure

Two parts, both cheap: a **column-bin hydrostatic integral** (particles binned by x into
fine columns, one downward pass, O(n) — the load that is actually there) and the PBF
multiplier as the dynamic part. `solidusOf: 'load'` feeds their sum to the phase law, and
S4's unload is a physical event: remove the load, the solidus drops, the column melts to
exactly the equilibrium the law predicts. The hydrostatic column at rest must reproduce
∫ρg dy with the J factors (`sp-metric.js` gate).

### 5.6 The crust: links, cracks, melt

The transplanted bond law with the same starting constants. **Welding** forms a link
between solid neighbours that stay neighbours (age clock, `yWeldMax` ceiling — the 0.3
measured lesson that without it the CMB grows a bottom plate). **Cracks** are damage: a
link whose mean pair excess exceeds `yieldRate * mu` accumulates damage and goes — a
stress concentration tears a seam, and the seam is visible as a gap because the pair
pushout opens it. **Melt** is not an event: as the equilibrium melt fraction rises, `mu`
falls through the law's own `heat(T)` term, links yield at ever-lower excess and finally
vanish at melt = 1 — the crust rots into the soup continuously. The plate that rides a
converging margin hangs its cold root below the cluster centroid and **pulls**: the root's
weight is a real force on its bonds, not a fitted `kSlab` moment. Whether the emergent
pull lands in the 0.3-measured drift band (2-5 cm/yr for slab-bearing plates) is M5's
calibration gate — the 0.3 lesson (a symmetric flow cancels at the plate; something must
pull) is pre-registered here as the risk it is (§12).

### 5.7 Eruptions and the surface

Melt is less dense, so **segregation is automatic**: no G5-style routing exists in SP; a
molten particle rises by its own buoyancy through the bond web, and where the web is
damaged it reaches the surface. A vent is a self-maintaining channel — hot particles,
zero strength, the pile's own weight supplying the head. Emission meters through the 0.3
flight constants (wall-clock arcs, `emitEff`-style caps), landing and welding as in HY.
The pile top per column is the relief; the renderer treats it exactly as HY's.

### 5.8 Ledgers

One book — the pool — plus the air phase and the reservoirs, in the 0.3 vocabulary. Mass
closes exactly (no grid to reconcile against); enthalpy closes against the wall bands
(the surface cooling band and the CMB band are its only externals, both booked).

## 6. The decisions, answered

The request's named questions, each with the default this plan ships, the option that
exposes it, and the measurement that can overturn it.

**Smooth or not.** Default: **two-sweep PBF** density constraints for the mantle,
granular contacts for deposits, contacts + bonds for crust (`magmaMode`). M0's selected
6,656-particle leg held the S1 fluid bands over 100–300 Myr (median peak vertical velocity
71.66 km/Myr, median six upwellings, 90.2% of samples in the 3–6-well band). The
like-for-like contact control was not cheaper enough to redeem its behavior: at 100 Myr
it read 498.08 km/Myr and nine wells. Contact therefore remains a granular/deposit mode,
not a mantle fallback. A future contact law can flip this only by passing the same full
distribution gate and winning on cost. Classic SPH remains rejected on explicit-stiffness
grounds (§5.2), not preference.

**Particle size — same or different, constant or changed.** The bookkeeping answer that
organises the rest: **size is a spacing and presentation parameter; mass is the book.**
A particle's mass is set once (by the measure policy, §5.1) and survives every size
change; shrinking a hot particle therefore raises its rest density — it takes less room
and keeps its weight, which is precisely the "more room to move" the request's experiment
reported. Defaults: `partSize` 1.0 (uniform d in the warped plane — which *is* the
screen-uniform policy), `sizeByMaterial` on (eruption seeds fine at d/2..d/4 for edifice
detail, merges coarsen buried parcels — 0.3 P3's measured pattern), `sizeByHeat` **off**
until M5 measures it (the crack-penetration leg: does a shrinking melt phase actually
flow through a damaged seam better, and what does it cost). M0 confirmed that heat size
never edits mass; at an exactly matched 28,728 particles the PBF mean costs were 21.62 ms
uniform, 25.09 ms material-sized and 21.31 ms heat-sized. Those are warm-kernel costs,
not a crack-flow result. The heat default is still overturned only by M5's leg, on
flow-through-crack rate and frame cost.

**The log vertical scale.** Adopted deliberately, not suffered: with the asinh map,
screen-uniform size means emulated size grows with depth — a CMB pixel represents 72.5x
the physical area of a surface pixel — and that is the 0.3 marker-measure policy arriving
through the side door: it is what lets ~26k warped-uniform particles cover the whole map
where surface-fine km-uniform circles would need ~72x more to fill the deep, and it is
what the engine's own mesh has always assumed (a 601 km bottom row). The alternative
(`space: 'km'`, physically isotropic circles) remains an option with its consequence
measured: at a 1280x520 display, screen-space rx/ry is 0.9954 everywhere, while the
matched km lattice spans 0.0434–2.224 (51.3x anisotropy range). KM is slightly cheaper,
not more expensive, in the warm matched-population PBF rows (4.44 vs 5.31 ms at 6,426;
19.98 vs 21.62 ms at 28,728). Screen is therefore the default for display geometry and
pile legibility, not under a false cost claim; M4 must run the full S1 leg in both spaces.

**Do we need Box2D.** No for these pages; the decision is measured on the vendored
planck 1.5.1 with walls, sleeping disabled, persistent contacts, contact conduction,
boundary temperatures and thermal lift. At exactly matched populations and nearly matched
box aspects, mean step costs for SP / planck are **0.27 / 1.37 ms at 403 bodies**, **0.68 /
3.86 ms at 1,000**, and **1.37 / 12.74 ms at 1,960**. The custom kernel is about 5–9x
faster on these matched rows. Planck's thermal states are finite, contained and
circulating, but its velocities are fixture units and are not compared with km/Myr. The separate settled-contact smoke reads 28.58 ms
best / 65.66 ms mean at 4,000 bodies and is noisy enough that best and mean are both
reported; lowering solver iterations did not provide a cheap win. Sources:
`planck-look.js`, `planck-smoke.js`, and the M0 report.

The pages therefore use custom SoA solvers. Planck remains the requested
circles-with-temperature-and-lift look reference. If a future matched leg wins both the
behavior panel and frame cost by 2x, this selection must be reopened; using planck in a
page would additionally require a determinism gate and an explicit waiver of the
no-allocation rule.

**Substeps and overshoot.** The displacement clamp (`substepCap = 0.35`) is part of the
solver, not an optional safety hope. It raises the global substep count at the fast clock
or a plume peak and the HUD prints k. The selected leg has k = 1 at its 100/200/300 Myr
checkpoints; the 500-kyr stability matrix remains finite and contained. Tunnelling is
**not** structurally absent in a position-based solver: M0's unclamped pair crosses in one
step, while the clamped pair takes 12 substeps and stays separated (§5.3).

**GPU.** CPU-first: the node harnesses are the truth, the pages must hit the budget on
CPU (§8), and the CPU code is the reference forever. The GPU port is its own release
(0.6.0, §13) — WebGPU compute for SP's pair walk and HY's red-black sweeps (both are
already local and fixed-iteration), behind a capability check with the CPU path as
fallback. Parity is gated by ledger drift and bounded tolerance over a fixed replay, not
bitwise — parallel reductions reassociate — with the analytic invariants (ledger closure,
energy bounds) carried over unchanged. Precedent: the globe's own GPU leg is an asymmetric
deliverable in its acceptance, and this mirrors it. No WebGL fallback, no dual GPU paths.

**Variable size on GPU.** The request's worry ("may be slower on GPU than same size")
is answered by structure and then measured: the hash cell is sized to 2·d_max regardless,
so mixed sizes change the *neighbour-count variance*, not the algorithm. The M0 leg
measures uniform vs material vs heat-sized neighbour distributions and frame cost on the
same workload; if the variance cost is real, `sizeByHeat` stays off in the shipped default
and remains a toggle — which is exactly what the option is for.

## 7. Rendering and UI

- Particles are additive splats sized by the *warped* diameter (screen-round by

## 12. Risks

- **The warped plane's consistency** (mass ∝ J, forces through the metric, conduction
  normalisation) is the newest math in the plan. Mitigated by being the first fixture
  (`sp-metric.js`, M4's entry gate) and by the `space: 'km'` option as a live cross-check.
- **SP's lid may not plate.** 0.3 needed a hand-added slab-pull moment to make plates
  drift at all; SP has real pull but its magnitude is uncalibrated, and the welding
  ceiling's constant is a transplant from a different load distribution. M5 owns this
  with the drift gate and the recalibration fixture; the failure mode is a planet of
  one immovable lid (0.3's measured failure too) and the remedy is the same family of
  constants.
- **HY's two-way coupling can ring** (particle load ↔ projection). M3 lands one-way
  first and gates the load-back behind a stability fixture at three load magnitudes.
- **PBF at density extremes** (a heat-shrunk melt phase next to cold giants) may starve
  constraints; the HUD's unsatisfied-constraint count makes it a reading, and
  `sizeByHeat` defaults off until measured.
- **The Ra ceiling.** 0.3 measured grid-scale content outrunning the mesh above Ra
  ~3e6; SP's equivalent ceiling (population-scale content) is measured in M4, not
  assumed away.
- **planck wins the bake-off.** The pre-registered selection rule (§6) stays in force;
  the current matched thermal legs put planck at 1.37 / 3.86 / 12.74 ms mean for
  403 / 1,000 / 1,960 bodies against SP's 0.27 / 0.68 / 1.37 ms.
- **GPU parity tolerance discipline** — deferred to 0.6.0 but the CPU-reference rule is
  fixed now so the port cannot quietly become the truth.
