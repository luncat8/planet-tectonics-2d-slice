# 0.3.0 — Draft: the particle engine

Reference record of the request that opened this line of work. The plan itself is
`0.3.0-plan.md`; this file is here so a later session can see what was asked, in the words
it was asked in.

## What was asked

Continue development by the existing plan series, and additionally:

- the current engine is not good enough for every purpose — plate collision and eruptions
  especially;
- make a plan for a new engine, a new page (HTML), that works with **particles**;
- **hybrid**: the fluid is a grid *and* particles; the solid is particles only;
- **plates must emerge** from the particles while the planet cools. They must form smooth
  connections, and a connection's strength must be proportional to temperature (and to age);
- choose an arbitrary demonstration time scale for the particles so that convection, plumes
  and eruptions are all visible to the viewer.

## Constraints carried into the plan

- the request is for a plan (a design document) first — a fork or a reimplementation must be
  possible from that document alone, with no reference to a chat;
- the existing engine and its 0.1.x / 0.2.x plans keep their own track; the new engine is
  additive and gets its own page;
- the repository's standing rules apply: file:// friendly, classic script tags, no build, no
  modules, guarded `module.exports`, single-tab indentation, preallocated typed arrays, no
  allocation in the frame loop, no internet links;
- every number in the plan must come from a script in `experiments/`, so it can be re-run.

## What the plan had to answer, therefore

1. what a particle is, and how a "solid" differs from a "fluid" in this model;
2. what makes a bond break or hold, in terms of temperature and age, and how that produces
   rigid plates, rifts and sutures without any plate ever being a data structure;
3. how the same particles can be mantle, melt and lava without a second toy system;
4. how big the pixel motion is at the demonstration clock, and why that clock shows a plume,
   a plate and an eruption at once;
5. what it costs per frame, from measurements, not estimates.
