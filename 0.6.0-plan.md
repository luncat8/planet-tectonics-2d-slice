# 0.6.0 — Plan: the line and the section

A pack (`0.5.0-plan.md`) is a line plus a profile. This release turns it into a **running
cross-section**: the column engine's state is composed from the imported map data, is legal for
the engine's own kernels, and continues from the map's epoch. Where the map's data ends, the
section says so instead of pretending; where the map speaks, the section must reproduce it
exactly.

Contracts C1..C8 of `roadmap.md` §4 and the ownership table in §5 apply. This plan adds no new
physics: the kernels stay the ones `0.1.0-design.md` and `0.2.0-plan.md` describe. What is new
is one kernel that builds a state from an imported profile, the rules it must follow, and the
evidence that it built the right one.

## 1. What a cross-section can and cannot be told

The map project simulates a sphere of Lagrangian crustal columns: thicknesses by lithology,
age, damage, plate rotation, six ore potentials, a dynamic-topography field and a plume field.
It does not simulate a lithospheric mantle, a geotherm below the crust, bedding, porosity, fault
geometry or a water inventory. The reconstruction is therefore a **model with a stated data
horizon**, not a measurement:

| quantity | in the pack | how the section gets it | confidence |
|----------|-------------|------------------------|------------|
| surface elevation | `z_m`, `zDyn_m` | recomputed from the imported crust by the shared isostasy (F7), compared with `z_m` | exact identity |
| crustal thickness and lithology | `hMaf_m`, `hFel_m`, `hSed_m` | beds pushed bottom-up with the engine's own stack primitive | imported |
| mobile load | `hMob_m`, `hMobFel_m`, `hMobPla_m` | extra sediment/placer beds, above the deposited sediment | imported |
| crustal age | `age_myr` | `colAge`, hence the fan's lid and the thermal field | imported |
| thermal structure below the crust | — | the engine's own lid law (half-space cooling with its `kappa`) scaled to the imported `Tm` | **modelled** |
| lithospheric mantle thickness | — | not exported; the section's own age law | **modelled** |
| asthenosphere and deeper | plume/wave blocks | the section's plume chain and fan, seeded from the pack when present | **modelled** |
| plate motion | `vE_mmyr`, `vN_mmyr`, `plate_1` | the along-line component becomes the scalar plate rate; the normal component is reported | imported, projected |
| boundaries | `edge_1`, `pol_1`, `trench_1`, `relN_mmyr` | the section's edge state and its slab ribbon at each crossing | imported, projected |
| ore potentials, fertility | `oVms_1`..`oPla_1`, `fert_1` | the section's potentials and fertility, hence its own deposits | imported |
| sea level | `settings sea_km` | a display datum for the section's sea line | imported, display only |

Two consequences the plan makes explicit and the UI repeats:

- **Below the Moho the section is a model.** The imported information ends at the base of the
  crust; everything under it comes from the section's own thermal law and the pack's optional
  plume/wave blocks. The renderer marks the horizon (8.2) and the assumption record names it.
- **The section is a flattened planet.** Horizontal distance is arc length and depth is radial,
  which is the standard flattening but not isometric: over the default 3000 km window a
  horizontal distance is 0.9 % shorter on the sphere than on the strip, and the direction of
  "down" rotates by 27° across it (and 360° across the full circumference). The section's
  physics is the engine's existing flat-planet physics; the reconstruction records the window
  length so the error is stated rather than implied.

## 2. Geometry

### 2.1 The frame

- `x` = arc length along the great circle, in metres, increasing A -> B; the section wraps at
  `P.wrap = 2*pi*R`.
- `y` = radial depth from the sea datum, as the engine already means it (the log/asinh scale is
  the renderer's business, not the state's).
- The pack's `x0_m = 0` is placed at the engine's `colX[0] = 0`, so **the picked point A is the
  section's periodic seam**. The strip and the section draw a seam marker labelled with A's
  latitude and longitude, and the wrap fixture checks that the two sides of the seam are
  continuous in the imported fields (the producer's interval walk and its continuity guard,
  `0.5.0-plan.md` §3.4 and §2.5).

### 2.2 Circle and arc

- `mode=circle`: the pack covers the whole circumference, so every column is imported and the
  section is seamless. This is the default.
- `mode=arc`: the pack covers `[0, length]`; the remainder of the domain is filled by a
  **clamp** — the end column's stack is repeated with an age ramp to the far side and the HUD
  marks the two clamped spans. A clamped span is never allowed to look imported: it is drawn
  with the modelled-material hatch (8.2) and counted in the assumption record.

### 2.3 The column grid

- The column count is `n = clamp(records, 128, colCap - 32)` unless a quality level fixes it, so
  a fine pack is not thrown away and a coarse one does not create slivers.
- Each column's `colX` is the midpoint of its arc interval, in the interval order, and widths
  come from the engine's own width pass, not from the pack.
- The pack's intervals are aggregated onto the column grid by the interval-slicing rule
  (`0.5.0-plan.md` §4.4): thicknesses are length-weighted means, features are sliced, nothing is
  nearest-sampled.
- Reference densities of the grid: the section's shipped `nCols` is 512 over the 40 030 km
  circumference (78.2 km per column). The map's own spacing is 223 km at L5, 112 km at L6 and
  56 km at L7, so a full circle yields about 180, 359 and 717 intervals. **An L5 pack is
  coarser than the section** (three columns share one cell's thickness) and an L7 pack resolves
  it; the import report says which case it is and the panel recommends L6 or finer.

## 3. The mapping

Every rule below is applied in the pass order of §5. Each has an assertion in the milestone
gates of §9 and a fixture in §10; a rule with an assumption in it also writes an entry in the
assumption record (§8).

### 3.1 Crust to beds

For each column, bottom-up, using `COL.push` (so the stack ordering the audit checks is the same
primitive that built a fresh planet):

1. `hMaf_m` of `maf`, split into at most 3 beds by `COL.splitBeds` when it exceeds 12 km;
2. `hFel_m` of `fel`, split into `max(1, round(hFel/12e3))` beds, age decreasing upward from the
   column age to the column age minus 45 % of it (the shape `COL.continental` already uses);
3. `hSed_m` of `sed`, split into at most 4 beds; their ages are the **assumption**: the map has
   no sediment age, so the reconstruction ages the beds linearly inside
   `[max(0, t - ageSed), t]` with `ageSed = min(age_myr, 100)` Myr and records it;
4. `hMobFel_m` and the rest of `hMob_m` as the youngest `sed` beds, and `hMobPla_m` as placer
   load on the top bed (`S.colPla`), not as a bed of its own;
5. whenever a column's total exceeds `P.hCollapse`, the excess above it is not pushed: it is
   booked to the assumption record as `over-collapse` and the column is flagged. The map's own
   collapse limit books that material to its mantle ledger; the section cannot invent a mantle
   ledger line for a world it does not own, so it reports instead of absorbing silently (C6).

The reconstruction must not call `COL.makePlanet`, `COL.continental` or `COL.oceanic`: those
invent a mask, an age ramp and a bedding pattern that the pack already answers for.

### 3.2 Age to thermal field

- `colAge[i] = age_myr` (clamped into `[0, lidAgeCap]`).
- The fan is built by the engine's own pass (`COL.initFanT`), which the fixture checks against
  half-space cooling with the engine's `kappa` and against the map's own subsidence term
  `350*sqrt(min(age, 80))`: the two laws describe the same cooling picture, and the identity of
  §3.3 is what ties them together.
- `Tm` starts at the pack's `tm` and follows the engine's cooling curve from `t = pack t`, so
  the section's thermal history starts where the map's was rather than at a fresh planet's.

### 3.3 Plates and rates

- Contiguous runs of `plate_1` become the section's plates (`S.nPl`, `S.plN`, `S.plX0`), in the
  pack's order; runs shorter than `P.minPlateCells` are merged into the longer neighbour and
  counted in the assumption record.
- Each plate's rate is the **along-line** mean of `(vE, vN)` projected on the local tangent, in
  m/Myr. The discarded normal component is reported per plate (a number in the import report,
  not a silent projection).
- `S.plDmg` is the run's mean `dmg_1`.
- Because the section's plate rate is a scalar and the map's is a rotation, the section's plate
  *geometry* can drift from the map's after the import. That is expected and is exactly why the
  section is authoritative only for its strip (roadmap §5). The sync of 0.7 re-imports at a
  cadence rather than trying to make the scalar follow the rotation.

### 3.4 Boundaries, slabs and arcs

At every interval whose `edge_1 != 0`:

- `S.edge[i]` and `S.edgeRPlate[i]` are set to the map's type (convergent -> the engine's
  `subduct` or `collide` depending on `pol_1`: `pol_1 = 2` is a continental collision, `±1` is a
  subduction with that side on top), `S.edgePol[i]` from the sign of `pol_1`, and
  `S.edgeAge[i] = 0` (the section's own hysteresis starts now).
- A convergent crossing with `pol_1 = ±1` spawns one slab ribbon at the trench column with
  `SLAB.newRibbon`, whose dip profile (45° growing to 60° by 660 km) is the engine's existing
  law. The ribbon's descent rate is set from the pack's `relN_mmyr` so the imported convergence
  drives the sink instead of the engine's default.
- `trench_1` positions the arc window: columns with `trench_1 in 1..3` on the overriding side
  get the arc-melt column flag the map's own arc logic uses, and `MAG`/`CRU` treat them as arc
  columns from frame 1.
- A divergent crossing imports as an open edge with the rate from `relN_mmyr`; the engine's own
  ridge/spawn logic then behaves as it does in a fresh world.

### 3.5 Plumes and dynamic topography

- `zDyn_m` imports directly into `S.zDyn`.
- `plumeT_1` becomes the initial fan anomaly at that column's surface cell.
- When the pack carries a `plumes` block, the section spawns up to `P.plumeCap` plumes at the
  crossing x of each listed plume, with the listed strength and age; when the block is absent
  the section uses its own seeded plumes and the assumption record says so. The plume chain's
  geometry below the crust is modelled either way (1).

### 3.6 Ore potentials and fertility

- `fert_1` imports directly, and each `oXxx_1` imports into the matching `S.oXxx`.
- The potentials are what the section *has* at import: deposit instantiation is the 0.2.0-M3
  kernel, still to land. Until then the section stores and displays the potentials and its
  deposit count stays zero; the fixture asserts the potentials, not deposits.
- The linkage rule that makes the return path of 0.9 meaningful: a bed that carries an ore flag
  keeps the potential that was live at its own age, so a deposit's class and depth in the
  section can be compared with the map's own ranked list for the same cells.

### 3.7 Sea level

The pack's `sea_km` becomes `S.recon.seaDatum`; the renderer draws its sea line there and labels
it `map sea level (display)`. Physics keeps the engine's own `z < 0` test, so a nonzero display
datum changes the picture and not the model — the same split the map project's 0.3.5 made.

## 4. The below-Moho floor

Everything below the imported crust base is modelled. The plan names the horizon
`recon.moho[i] = hTot[i]` at import time and draws it (8.2). Below it:

- the lithospheric mantle continues the imported age's cooling to the engine's own
  `COL.lithDepth(age)`;
- the asthenosphere below is the fan's own convective field, seeded from `Tm`, the plume block
  and the section's own seeded perturbations;
- no imported number is claimed under the floor in the HUD, the probe or the capture.

## 5. The kernel

`js/slice.js`, DOM-free, node-guarded, allocation-free after `init`, called once per import
(never in the frame loop). Pass order:

1. **validate** — the pack parses, the required fields are present, `records > 0`, the
   continuity and seam guards pass (the codec's job, re-checked here for a programmatic call);
2. **geometry** — choose `n`, compute the column grid from the pack's intervals, aggregate
   (0.5.0 §4.4);
3. **stacks** — clear the layers, push beds per §3.1, run `COL.sums`;
4. **plates** — §3.3, then the legality pass of §6;
5. **boundaries** — §3.4, including ribbon spawns;
6. **fields** — `zDyn`, `plumeT`, potentials, fertility, damage, sea datum (§3.5-3.7);
7. **thermals** — `colAge` and `COL.initFanT`;
8. **profile** — the engine's own `SURF.profile()` so `z`, `wet` and the slope follow from the
   stacks the engine just built;
9. **ledgers** — the map-equivalent volume ledger, the assumption record, and the identity check
   of §9 M3 against the pack's own `z_m`;
10. **quiet start** — set the event timers to zero (`S.edgeAge`, `S.edgeSlow`, `SIM.evT` = 0) and
    the engine's RNG to the documented stream (§7), so the run begins with a known state.

The kernel takes an `out` scratch record allocated at init; the only allocation permitted is the
one-time growth of the codec's record buffer when a larger pack arrives (0.5.0 §4.1).

## 6. Legality of the reconstructed state

The engine's kernels assume a sorted, gapless, positive-width column list; the reconstruction
must hand over a state that passes the same audit a fresh planet does:

- `colX` strictly increasing over `[0, wrap)` in interval order, `colPlate` runs contiguous;
- every width positive and every plate at least `P.minPlateCells` columns (else merge);
- `S.nCol <= colCap - 32`, `S.nPl <= plateCap`, `S.nRib <= ribCap`, `S.nPlm <= plumeCap`;
- every stack within `layerCap`, every lithology inside its enum, every age finite and
  `>= 0`, every thickness `>= 0`;
- `S.hash()` finite and reproducible for the same pack and seed.

The audit runs in the kernel, not in a fixture, and a failure refuses the import with the
offending column index: a broken imported world must never enter the frame loop.

## 7. Running forward

- The section starts at `SIM.t = pack t`; its clock, event cadence and cooling curve continue
  from there (0.4.0's transport applies: pause, step, run).
- **Quiet start.** The imported state is not a solution of the engine's own dynamics, so the
  first frames are allowed to relax and are *reported*: the HUD shows the spin-up residual (the
  change in per-lithology volume and in the mean elevation over the first 100 frames) and the
  fixture bounds it. At `dt = 10 kyr/frame` no column may be consumed, no boundary may change
  type in the first Myr, and the crust volume may change by no more than the engine's own
  documented production/consumption terms at that rate.
- The reconstruction seeds a separate bedding stream from the pack's fold (so bedding is a
  deterministic function of the pack) and then re-seeds the engine's RNG from `(P.seed, frame 0)`
  exactly once; both values go into the capture. The determinism gate is "same pack + same seed
  => identical hash after 1000 frames".
- The map's `t` is not authoritative while the section runs alone (0.7 makes it so): the capture
  records the pack's epoch and the section's own clock, and the panel shows both.

## 8. The assumption record and the UI

### 8.1 The record

One fixed-size record on the state (`S.recon`), written by the kernel, read by the HUD, the
probe, the capture and the fixture:

- the pack's identity (fold, producer, level, seed, t, mode, length) and the codec version;
- the mapping version, a constant bumped whenever a rule in §3 changes;
- the assumption counters, indexed by an enum: `sediment-age`, `sub-Moho-thermal`,
  `lithosphere-depth`, `plume-default`, `over-collapse`, `short-plate-merge`, `clamped-span`,
  `discarded-normal-velocity`, `sea-datum-display`;
- the data horizon per column (`moho[i]`) and the across-line footprint per column (`A_j`);
- the spin-up residual and the identity check's maximum error.

A capture with no pack writes `pack none`; a capture with one writes the fold, the line, the
mapping version and the assumption counters, so a reported section can be reproduced and its
model content read off.

### 8.2 Drawing the model

- Below `moho[i]`, the body is drawn with a 45° hatch instead of the normal texture, at low
  contrast, in the section's own palette. The hatch is a renderer flag on the overlay bus
  (`data-overlay="model"`), so a viewer who wants the "solid planet" look can switch it off.
- A clamped span (arc mode) uses the same hatch, plus a vertical tick at its edge.
- The seam at `x = 0` gets a labelled tick with A's coordinates.

### 8.3 The panel and the probe

- A `reconstructed from map ...` banner in the section's frame: producer, level, seed, epoch,
  mapping version and the assumption count (a number, clickable to the list).
- The probe shows, per column, the map cell it came from, its interval, its age and whether it
  sits above or below the horizon.
- A **raw/reconstructed overlay** switch draws the pack's own `z_m` profile as a faint line
  under the section's synthesized `z` (the pack strip of 0.5 zoomed to the section's window), so
  a mismatch is visible rather than asserted. At frame 0 the two must coincide (the F7 identity);
  after that they diverge by exactly the section's own evolution, which is the point.

## 9. Milestones

### M0 — the grid and the aggregation

`js/slice.js` skeleton: `n` selection, the column grid, the interval-slicing aggregation, the
volume ledger. Gate: a uniform pack aggregates exactly; a one-cell spike keeps its volume; the
grid is legal for `records` = 1, 180, 717 and 4096; nothing allocates after init.

### M1 — stacks and thermals

§3.1, §3.2, §4. Gate: hand-built columns have the exact thicknesses and bed order; the fan
matches half-space cooling; the `over-collapse` counter fires; the layer-cap audit refuses an
over-deep stack with its column index.

### M2 — plates, boundaries, fields

§3.3, §3.4, §3.5, §3.6, §3.7. Gate: plate runs and rates match a synthetic pack; a subduction
crossing spawns exactly one ribbon with the engine's dip profile; the arc window lands on the
overriding side; potentials and fertility import; the legality audit passes on every synthetic
case.

### M3 — the identity and the quiet start

§7, and the identity of §9 M3. Gate: `|z_recon - z_m| <= 1e-3 m` for every column of every test
pack, including the real golden pack, at frame 0; the spin-up residual inside the recorded
bound; no consumption and no boundary-type change in the first Myr.

### M4 — the UI

§8: the banner, the hatch, the seam tick, the probe provenance, the raw/reconstructed overlay,
the capture line. Gate: `experiments/ui-parity.js` and a DOM-stub run of the panel.

### M5 — the self round-trip

The strongest fixture available without the map project: run the column engine 200 Myr from a
seeded planet, export **its own** transect with the same codec (`tools/transect-export.js` gains
a `--column` mode that reads a column-engine save and writes a pack with the fields the column
engine has, marking the missing ones absent), import it back, and require: identical per-column
thicknesses and ages, the same `S.hash()` for the parts a pack carries, and a forward run of
1000 frames that is inside the spin-up bound. This validates the codec, the grid, the
aggregation and the mapping's geometry without any judgement about the map project's
conventions.

### M6 — acceptance with a real pack

`experiments/slice-recon.js` on the golden pack: the identity (M3), the ranges of every imported
field, the volume ledger ratio inside its recorded bound, the assumption counters at their
recorded values, 1000 frames without NaN and with the ledger closing, and the raw/reconstructed
overlay's frame-0 coincidence.

## 10. Fixtures

| fixture | contract |
|---------|----------|
| `experiments/slice-recon.js` | grid and aggregation, stacks, thermals, plates, boundaries, the F7 identity, the quiet start, 1000 frames |
| `experiments/slice-self.js` | the column engine's own pack round-trips into an equivalent state (M5) |
| `experiments/ui-parity.js` (0.4.0) | the section panel's ids, the banner, the hatch flag, the capture line |
| `experiments/slicepack.js` (0.5.0) | the aggregation rule the reconstruction depends on |

The identity check is worth restating because it is unusual: it exists because both crust
engines already share the isostasy and subsidence constants (roadmap F7). It is a *hard* gate
(1e-3 m), not a visual check. If it ever fails, the failure is in the import, not in the physics.

## 11. Acceptance

1. A pack from the map project imports into a running section; the elevation identity holds to
   1e-3 m at frame 0; the volume ledger and the assumption counters are reported.
2. The section is legal: the engine's own audit passes, `S.hash()` is stable for the same pack
   and seed, and 1000 frames at 10 and at 100 kyr/frame produce no NaN, no capacity refusal and
   a closing ledger.
3. The first Myr is quiet: no column consumed, no boundary type change, the spin-up residual
   inside the recorded bound.
4. The panel shows where the data came from, where the model starts (hatch below the Moho, the
   clamp in arc mode) and what was assumed (counters).
5. The self round-trip (M5) reproduces a section-engine world from its own pack to within the
   hash the pack carries.
6. `smoke.js`, `ledger-check.js`, `section-check.js`, `contact-audit.js` and the 0.4.0 fixtures
   stay green: the reconstructed section runs on the same kernels and must satisfy the same
   invariants as a fresh planet.

## 12. Risks and open questions

- **The first frame is the dangerous one.** A state that is not a fixed point of the engine's
  dynamics can produce a violent transient. The quiet-start reporting and the bound are the
  mitigation; if the bound is exceeded in practice, the plan's answer is to *report a longer
  spin-up* (a stated number of frames excluded from the ledger comparison), not to hide it by
  changing the imported numbers.
- **The normal velocity is dropped.** For a line drawn obliquely to a boundary the scalar rate
  understates the convergence. The report names it; 0.7's picker can snap the line perpendicular
  to a boundary, which is the physical fix rather than a modelling one.
- **Sediment age is invented.** It is bounded (100 Myr), counted and drawn as an assumption. A
  later pack revision can carry a per-bed sediment age if the map project ever tracks one.
- **The scalar plate model will drift from the map's rotation** as soon as the section runs
  alone. That is by design (roadmap §5); 0.7 re-imports rather than trying to reconcile.
- **Deposits are not there yet.** The potentials import now, the deposit kernel is 0.2.0-M3. The
  linkage rule in §3.6 is stated now so that kernel does not have to be reopened later.
- **The packing of the mapping version.** Any change to §3 changes reconstructed worlds; the
  version is in the record and in the capture, and a fixture asserts a world rebuilt with the
  same version has the same hash. Changing the version is allowed; changing the rules behind the
  same version is a bug.
